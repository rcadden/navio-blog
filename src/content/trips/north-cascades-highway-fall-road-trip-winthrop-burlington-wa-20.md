**The North Cascades Scenic Highway (Washington State Route 20) runs 145 miles from Winthrop west to Burlington, with a realistic drive time of 3.5–4.5 hours plus stops. Mid-October is the single best window for this route: subalpine larches above Washington Pass turn gold, big-leaf maples flame orange in the Skagit Valley gorge, and the highway hasn't yet closed for winter.** Plan two days to do it justice — one night in Winthrop before heading west, arriving in Burlington the following afternoon. This is a point-to-point drive with no good loop option, so either deadhead back or fly out of Seattle-Tacoma International Airport, 75 miles south of Burlington on I-5.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** 145 miles (Winthrop to Burlington via WA-20)
- **Total drive time:** 3.5–4.5 hours driving; recommended 2 days
- **Best season:** Early-to-mid October for peak larch and maple color
- **Number of stops:** 6 main stops
- **Starting point:** Winthrop, WA
- **Ending point:** Burlington, WA

## Who This Route Is For

- **Fall color chasers** who want subalpine larch gold and valley maple orange in a single corridor — two distinct ecosystems in one drive
- **Weekend drivers from Seattle** who want to avoid the Seattle-to-Portland corridor covered everywhere else; Winthrop is 3 hours from Seattle via US-2 and WA-20 eastbound
- **Not recommended for RVs over 35 feet** — Washington Pass has tight switchbacks and limited pull-outs; the National Park Service advises against large rigs above the Gorge
- **Solo drivers or couples** comfortable with a point-to-point format; families with young children will find Newhalem's trail network genuinely accessible

## Stop 1: Winthrop, WA

Winthrop is your staging town. Fill the tank here at the Chevron on WA-20 (the last reliable fuel before Marblemount, 100 miles west — do not skip this). The town's Western-themed main street has good breakfast options; we recommend the Rocking Horse Bakery on Riverside Avenue for coffee and a breakfast sandwich before the mountain push. October mornings in Winthrop run cold (28–40°F), so start no earlier than 8 a.m. to let any overnight frost burn off the road surface above the pass.

## Stop 2: Mazama (25 minutes from Winthrop)

Mazama is 14 miles west of Winthrop and the last outpost before the climb to Washington Pass. The Mazama Store stocks sandwiches, firewood, and basic camping supplies — useful if you're stopping at a Forest Service campsite on the way down. By mid-October most campgrounds along WA-20 are first-come, first-served with reduced fees. The subalpine larch color typically peaks between October 5–20 at elevations above 5,500 feet; Mazama sits at 2,500 feet, so you'll see cottonwood gold in the valley floor here.

## Stop 3: Washington Pass Overlook (35 minutes from Mazama)

At 5,477 feet, Washington Pass is the high point of the route and the best single viewpoint on WA-20. The paved overlook parking lot sits just off the highway and takes about 10 minutes to walk. Liberty Bell Mountain and the Early Winters Spires frame a foreground of golden larch that photographers fly across the country to shoot. **Seasonal hazard:** The Washington State Department of Transportation typically closes WA-20 between Early Winters and Ross Lake for the winter sometime between late October and mid-November — exact dates depend on snowfall. Check the WSDOT Highway Alerts page before you leave Winthrop. If you're driving in the third or fourth week of October, there is a real chance of a temporary closure after a storm system; have a Plan B via US-97 south to I-90.

## Stop 4: Diablo Lake Overlook (45 minutes from Washington Pass)

Diablo Lake's turquoise water — the color comes from glacial flour suspended in the water — contrasts sharply with October's orange and red canopy on the surrounding slopes. The overlook is a pull-out directly on WA-20 with room for 15–20 cars. In mid-October, the big-leaf maples lining the Skagit River below Diablo are at or near peak color. If you have 45 extra minutes, the Thunder Knob Trail (2.3 miles round trip) starts from Colonial Creek Campground just west of the overlook and puts you above the lake.

## Stop 5: Newhalem (20 minutes from Diablo Lake Overlook)

Newhalem is the company town for Seattle City Light's Skagit Hydroelectric Project and sits inside North Cascades National Park. The Visitor Center (open through mid-October) has clean restrooms and free exhibits on the dam system. The Trail of the Cedars (0.4-mile loop) passes old-growth western red cedar and is worth the 20-minute walk. Fuel note: there is no gas in Newhalem. You should still have plenty from Winthrop if you filled up there; Concrete is 25 miles west.

## Stop 6: Concrete, WA (25 minutes from Newhalem)

Concrete is a working Skagit Valley town — not a tourist destination — but it has a 76 station on WA-20, a diner (Cascade Diner on Main Street), and a good stopping point before the final push to Burlington. The Skagit Valley floor here is broad and flat; in October, the surrounding hillsides hold the last of the maple color while pumpkin stands line the road. If you want a second overnight, the North Cascades Inn in Concrete is basic but clean.

## Stop 7: Burlington, WA (35 minutes from Concrete)

Burlington is where WA-20 meets I-5. Trip ends here. The Skagit Valley is Washington's tulip country in spring, but in October it's a patchwork of harvested fields and remaining tree color. Burlington has full services — fuel, hotels, and a Skagit Valley Food Co-op on Metcalf Street for a proper meal before the drive home or onward to Seattle via I-5 south (about 65 miles, 1 hour).

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive WA-20 from Winthrop to Burlington?

The 145-mile drive takes 3.5–4.5 hours of pure driving time, but plan 6–7 hours with stops at Washington Pass, Diablo Lake, and Newhalem. We recommend two days with an overnight in Winthrop.

### When does the North Cascades Highway close for winter?

WSDOT typically closes WA-20 between Early Winters Sno-Park and Ross Lake (roughly milepost 134 to 170) sometime between late October and mid-November, depending on snowfall. The closure is announced on the WSDOT Highway Alerts page, often with 24–48 hours' notice. Mid-October drives are generally fine but check conditions the morning you depart.

### Is WA-20 safe to drive in October?

For the most part, yes. The road is paved, well-maintained, and regularly patrolled. The main risks are early-season snow above 4,000 feet (possible after October 15), morning ice on shaded switchbacks, and occasional rockfall in the gorge section west of Newhalem. All-season tires are sufficient for most October dates; carry chains if you're traveling after October 20.

### Where do you fill up gas on the North Cascades Highway?

Fill up in Winthrop before heading west — the Chevron on WA-20 is the last reliable station for 100 miles until Marblemount (which has a small Shell). Concrete's 76 station is the next option. Do not rely on Mazama or Newhalem for fuel.

### Are there RV restrictions on WA-20 through the North Cascades?

The NPS and WSDOT do not formally prohibit RVs on WA-20, but they advise against vehicles over 35 feet on the stretch between Early Winters and Diablo Lake due to tight curves and limited turnaround points. Class A motorhomes and fifth-wheels should stick to US-2 or I-90 across the Cascades.

### Is a National Park pass required for this drive?

No pass is required to drive through North Cascades National Park on WA-20. You only need a pass or day-use fee if you park inside the park boundary for hiking. The Diablo Lake Overlook pull-out is technically on NPS land; enforcement is minimal in October, but an America the Beautiful Annual Pass ($80) covers it and every other federal site you'll visit.
