**The Natchez Trace Parkway National Scenic Byway runs 444 miles total; this Tupelo-to-Nashville segment covers approximately 310 miles in 6–7 hours of pure driving time, with no commercial trucks, no billboards, and no traffic lights for most of its length. We rate it the most underrated long-distance drive in the American South.** Managed by the National Park Service, the Trace follows a corridor used by Chickasaw and Choctaw nations for centuries, later tramped by flatboat crews returning north from Natchez. In late June, the forest canopy is full and green, temperatures are warm but tolerable in the morning hours, and the route is uncrowded compared to any coastal or mountain alternative.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~310 miles (Tupelo, MS to Nashville, TN via the Trace)
- **Total drive time:** 6–7 hours (speed limit is 50 mph throughout; plan 2–3 days)
- **Best season:** Spring and fall are peak; summer (June) is excellent — foliage is full, crowds are thin
- **Number of stops:** 5 major stops
- **Starting point:** Tupelo, MS
- **Ending point:** Nashville, TN

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for history buffs and slow travelers** who want depth over distance — every pull-off has a dated, interpretive sign worth reading
- **Great for solo drivers or couples** seeking a genuinely quiet two-lane experience without interstate fatigue
- **Good for most passenger vehicles and smaller RVs** (under 35 ft); larger rigs should check NPS length advisories, as some pull-offs have tight turning radii
- **Not the right fit for families needing constant activity stops** — the Trace is contemplative, not theme-park dense

## Tupelo, MS — Start Here

Tupelo is the practical launch point for the northern Mississippi section of the Trace. Fill your tank here — gas stations do not exist on the Parkway itself, and the next reliable cluster of stations is 45 miles north near Tishomingo. We recommend Sunflower Restaurant on Main Street for breakfast before you roll: biscuits, country ham, and coffee that will carry you well into mid-morning. Elvis Presley's birthplace is two blocks off the Trace entrance on US-78 if you want a quick 20-minute detour before the quiet begins.

## Tishomingo State Park
*(Approx. 45 minutes / 43 miles from Tupelo)*

This is the most geologically distinct stop on the Mississippi portion of the route. Tishomingo State Park sits in the foothills of the Appalachians — actual exposed sandstone outcrops and clear Bear Creek cutting through rock that looks nothing like the rest of Mississippi. In June, the creek is running well and swimmable. The park entrance is just off the Trace at milepost 304. Day-use fee is nominal; no reservation needed for a swim and a walk on the swinging bridge trail.

## Tennessee River at Colbert Ferry
*(Approx. 30 minutes / 28 miles from Tishomingo)*

At milepost 327, the Trace crosses into Alabama briefly and reaches the Tennessee River at the Colbert Ferry site. There is no ferry now — just a wide, calm river view, a boat ramp, and a picnic area. This was once the busiest crossing on the old Trace; a ferry operator named George Colbert reportedly charged Meriwether Lewis's party $75 to cross in 1809 — a sum worth arguing about even then. Stop for 15 minutes, stretch, and watch the river traffic. Fill a water bottle before leaving; the next services are in Tennessee.

## Meriwether Lewis Monument & Grinder's Stand
*(Approx. 1 hour 20 minutes / 80 miles from Colbert Ferry)*

At milepost 385.9 near Hohenwald, Tennessee, the Trace passes the site where Meriwether Lewis died in October 1809 under circumstances still debated by historians. The monument is a broken column — intentional, symbolizing a life cut short. The reconstructed Grinder's Stand cabin marks where he spent his last night. This is a genuine historical anchor, not a tourist reconstruction, and it takes about 30 quiet minutes to walk the grounds. There is a small NPS campground here (Meriwether Lewis Campground) that makes an excellent overnight stop if you're splitting the drive across two days.

## Gordon House & Ferry Site
*(Approx. 45 minutes / 45 miles from Meriwether Lewis Monument)*

At milepost 407.7, the Gordon House is the only structure still standing along the entire Natchez Trace that was built during the Trace's operational heyday (circa 1818). It's a Federal-style brick house in the middle of the forest, which makes it quietly surreal. The Duck River crossing here was another major ferry point. Stop for 10 minutes — the placard is worth reading — then continue the final push toward Nashville.

If you're hungry before Nashville, exit the Trace at TN-96 near Franklin and head into downtown Franklin for lunch. Puckett's Grocery & Restaurant on Fifth Avenue South serves reliable Tennessee comfort food and has been operating in some form since 1950.

## Nashville, TN — End Point
*(Approx. 45 minutes / 35 miles from Gordon House)*

The Trace formally ends at milepost 444 near the Pasquo area of Nashville, depositing you onto TN-100. From there it's a 20-minute drive into downtown. After 310 miles of 50 mph serenity, the contrast is immediate and intentional. Nashville's Broadway district is the obvious first stop, but if you've had enough crowds, the Nations neighborhood on the west side has better bars and shorter lines.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive the Natchez Trace Parkway from Tupelo to Nashville?
The 310-mile segment takes 6–7 hours of driving time at the posted 50 mph speed limit. With stops at the major historical sites, most drivers spread this over two days. The NPS campground at Meriwether Lewis (milepost 385.9) is the most convenient overnight break point.

### Are there gas stations on the Natchez Trace Parkway?
No. The Parkway has no commercial services — no gas stations, no fast food, no convenience stores. Fill up in Tupelo before you enter and plan exits at TN-96 near Franklin or US-72 near Tishomingo if you need fuel mid-route. A full tank from Tupelo will comfortably cover the 310 miles in a standard passenger car.

### Is the Natchez Trace Parkway safe to drive in summer?
Yes, with standard precautions. The road is well-maintained, two lanes, and lightly traveled. In June, the primary hazard is deer at dawn and dusk — the forest is dense and animals cross frequently. Heat is a factor if you break down; carry extra water. There are no significant flood or storm closures typical of June.

### Can you drive the Natchez Trace Parkway in an RV?
Most RVs up to 35 feet handle the Trace without issue. The speed limit and the no-truck rule actually make it more relaxed than interstate driving. Some pull-offs and the campground at Meriwether Lewis have limited space for slide-outs; check the NPS Natchez Trace Parkway campground page before booking.

### Do you need a permit or pass to drive the Natchez Trace Parkway?
No permit or entrance fee is required to drive the Parkway — it is a free National Park Service road. Camping at NPS campgrounds along the route requires a standard fee (currently around $20/night) payable at self-service kiosks. America the Beautiful passes are accepted.

### What is the best time of year to drive the Natchez Trace Parkway?
Spring (late March through May) and fall (October through November) offer the most dramatic scenery — wildflowers in spring, color in fall. June is an excellent summer choice: full green canopy, low visitation, and no weather closures. Avoid January and February if possible, as ice events can close sections without much warning.
