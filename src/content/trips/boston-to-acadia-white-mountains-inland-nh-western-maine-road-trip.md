**This inland route from Boston to Acadia National Park covers roughly 320 miles and 6–8 hours of pure drive time, threading north on I-93 through Franconia Notch, east on US-302 through North Conway and Bethel, and north on ME-27 and US-2 into the park. It's the best choice for drivers who want alpine terrain, smaller crowds, and a genuinely different experience from the coastal US-1 corridor.** The coastal run on US-1 gets the most attention, but this inland line through the White Mountains and western Maine river valleys earns its place as a legitimate alternative — and in late June, the high-elevation roads are fully open and the wildflowers along Franconia Ridge are at their peak.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~320 miles (Boston to Bar Harbor)
- **Total drive time:** 6–8 hours (without stops)
- **Recommended trip length:** 3–4 days
- **Best season:** Late May through early October; peak summer is June–August
- **Number of major stops:** 5
- **Starting point:** Boston, MA
- **Ending point:** Acadia National Park (Bar Harbor), ME

## Who This Route Is For

- **Hikers and outdoor-focused travelers** who want trail access at multiple stops, not just a scenic drive
- **Drivers avoiding peak coastal traffic** — US-1 through Portland and Brunswick in late June is stop-and-go on weekends; this route largely isn't
- **Families with kids 8+** who can handle moderate hikes; the flume gorge at Franconia Notch is a reliable crowd-pleaser
- **Not recommended for RVs over 30 ft** — the Franconia Notch Parkway (I-93 narrows to a two-lane parkway through the notch) has height and length restrictions; check NHDOT advisories before departure

## Boston, MA — Departure Point

Leave Boston via I-93 North. Fill the tank before you leave the metro — gas prices drop once you clear the Massachusetts border, but stations thin out north of Plymouth, NH. If you're starting from downtown, grab coffee and a breakfast sandwich at Flour Bakery + Café on Washington Street before you go; there's no reason to stop for food before Franconia unless you want to.

Plan to be on the road by 7:30 a.m. on a summer weekend. I-93 through Manchester, NH moves freely that early; by 9 a.m. it can back up near the toll plazas.

## Franconia Notch State Park

*(Approx. 2 hours from Boston via I-93 North)*

Franconia Notch State Park sits at the southern end of the White Mountain National Forest, bisected by the Franconia Notch Parkway — the section of I-93 that narrows through the mountains. The two anchors here are the Flume Gorge (a 2-mile boardwalk loop through a granite gorge; $18/adult, $15/child in 2025, confirm current rates at nhstateparks.org) and Echo Lake Beach, which has a swimming area and clean restrooms.

For serious hikers, the Franconia Ridge Loop — accessed from the Lafayette Place Campground trailhead — is one of the best above-treeline ridge walks in the Northeast: 8.9 miles, ~3,500 ft of gain, plan 5–6 hours. In late June, expect temperatures 15–20°F cooler than Boston at the summit; pack a layer.

**Seasonal note for June 2026:** The Flume Gorge typically opens May 1; no closures expected in late June. The parkway itself has no seasonal closure, but afternoon thunderstorms build over the Whites most summer days — aim to be off exposed ridges by 1 p.m.

**Gas:** Fill up at the Irving station in Lincoln, NH (Exit 32 off I-93) before entering the notch. There are no fuel stops inside the park.

## North Conway, NH

*(Approx. 45 minutes from Franconia Notch via I-93 South to Exit 35, then NH-112 East to US-302 East)*

North Conway is a working gateway town with a main street (White Mountain Highway / US-302) lined with gear shops, restaurants, and outlet stores. It's not a quiet village — treat it as a fuel-and-food stop, not a destination.

For lunch, Moat Mountain Smoke House & Brewing Co. on Main Street serves solid smoked brisket and pulled pork with their own craft beer; expect a 20–30 minute wait on summer Saturdays. If you want faster, Flatbread Company two blocks north has wood-fired pizza and shorter lines at midday.

Fill up on gas here at the Sunoco on US-302 near the Eastern Slope Inn — it's consistently cheaper than stations deeper in Maine. This is the last reliable low-price fuel before Bethel.

## Bethel, ME

*(Approx. 40 minutes from North Conway via US-302 East)*

US-302 crosses into Maine at Fryeburg and follows the Saco River valley east to Bethel — a small mountain town with a genuine Main Street and Sunday River ski resort looming to the north. In late June, Sunday River's access road (ME-26 North) is open for mountain biking and scenic chair rides; the resort operates a summer bike park with rentals on-site.

Bethel is the right place to overnight if you're splitting this into two days. The Bethel Inn Resort has updated rooms and a decent on-site restaurant; the Sunday River Brewing Company in town is the better dinner choice — their burger and IPA combination after a day of hiking is hard to argue with.

From Bethel, you have two onward options: continue on US-2 East toward Rumford and then north on ME-17 and ME-4 toward Farmington, or cut directly northeast on ME-26 toward Norway and Auburn to pick up I-95. The inland route through Farmington is slower but keeps you off the interstate.

## Farmington, ME

*(Approx. 1 hour from Bethel via US-2 East and ME-4 North)*

Farmington is the commercial hub of Franklin County and a practical midpoint if you're doing this trip in two days. It has a Hannaford supermarket, a Dunkin', and several motels. It's not a sightseeing stop — use it to resupply, refuel at the Irving on ME-4, and push on.

From Farmington, the most direct route to Bar Harbor runs northeast: ME-4 to US-2 East to I-95 North to Bangor, then ME-1A or ME-3 East to Ellsworth and the park entrance. This leg is about 2.5 hours.

## Acadia National Park

*(Approx. 2.5 hours from Farmington via US-2 East, I-95 North, and ME-3 East)*

Acadia's main entrance on Mount Desert Island is reached via ME-3 through Ellsworth. The park entrance fee is $35/vehicle for a 7-day pass (America the Beautiful annual pass accepted). In summer 2026, the park's timed-entry reservation system is expected to remain in effect for the Cadillac Summit Road and Jordan Pond area — book reservations at recreation.gov well in advance; popular windows sell out weeks ahead.

For the first evening, drive the 27-mile Park Loop Road (one-way in sections) to get oriented. Sand Beach, Thunder Hole, and the Otter Cliff pullout are the three mandatory stops on that loop. Sunrise from Cadillac Mountain (the highest point on the US East Coast north of Rio de Janeiro) requires a timed-entry reservation for the summit road; the trailhead parking lots on the South Ridge Trail do not.

For dinner in Bar Harbor, Café This Way on Mount Desert Street serves a reliable lobster bisque and grilled fish without the tourist-trap pricing of the waterfront spots. Reservations recommended in late June.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Boston to Acadia National Park via the White Mountains?

Pure drive time on this inland route (I-93 North to US-302 East through North Conway and Bethel, then US-2 and I-95 to Bar Harbor) is 6–8 hours without stops. With a hike at Franconia Notch and lunch in North Conway, most drivers arrive in Bar Harbor after 10–11 hours total. We recommend splitting it into two days with an overnight in Bethel or North Conway.

### Is the Franconia Notch Parkway safe for RVs and large vehicles?

The Franconia Notch Parkway narrows I-93 to a two-lane undivided road through the notch, with posted restrictions for vehicles over 30 feet. RVs and trailers should check current NHDOT advisories before departing. The route is fine for standard passenger vehicles, minivans, and smaller camper vans.

### Do you need a timed-entry reservation for Acadia National Park in summer 2026?

As of current NPS planning, timed-entry reservations are required for the Cadillac Summit Road and the Jordan Pond parking area during peak summer hours (typically 6 a.m.–3 p.m.). Reservations open on recreation.gov roughly 90 days in advance and sell out quickly for July and August dates. Arrive before 6 a.m. to access the summit road without a reservation.

### What is the best place to stop overnight between Boston and Acadia on the inland route?

Bethel, ME is the strongest overnight option: it sits roughly at the midpoint, has multiple lodging choices across price ranges, and positions you well for an early start toward the park the next morning. North Conway, NH is a good alternative if you want more dining options, but it adds slightly more driving on day two.

### Is US-302 through the White Mountains open in late June?

Yes. US-302 is a year-round highway with no seasonal closure. By late June all White Mountain roads — including NH-112 (Kancamagus Highway) and the access roads to major trailheads — are fully open. Snow is possible above 4,000 feet through late May but is not a concern in late June. Afternoon thunderstorms are the primary weather hazard in summer; check the Mount Washington Observatory forecast before planning above-treeline hikes.

### How much does it cost to enter Acadia National Park?

The standard vehicle entrance fee is $35 for a 7-day pass. The America the Beautiful annual interagency pass ($80) covers this fee and is worth buying if you'll visit any other federal lands in the same year. There is no fee to drive ME-3 through the park's non-fee zones or to access Bar Harbor itself.
