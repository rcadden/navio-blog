**This Chicago-to-Door-County road trip covers roughly 245 miles and 3 hours 45 minutes of pure drive time, following I-94 North to I-43 North and then Wisconsin State Highway 57 (WI-57) up the Door Peninsula. It is the most practical route that lets you stop at working lakefront cities before trading urban infrastructure for cherry orchards and cliff-top parks.** Most Chicago drivers skip the interesting middle section entirely and blast straight up I-94 to US-41. This guide takes you along the Lake Michigan shoreline instead, adding two or three worthy stops without meaningfully extending your day.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~245 miles
- **Total drive time:** 3 hours 45 minutes (driving only; plan 6–7 hours with stops)
- **Best season:** Late June through early September; cherries peak mid-July
- **Number of stops:** 6 (Kenosha, Sheboygan, Manitowoc, Green Bay, Sturgeon Bay, Fish Creek)
- **Starting point:** Chicago, IL (I-94 North from the Dan Ryan)
- **Ending point:** Sister Bay, WI

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for families with kids** who need stretch breaks every 90 minutes — Kenosha's lakefront park and the Wisconsin Maritime Museum in Manitowoc are legitimately engaging.
- **Good for couples on a long weekend** who want a mix of small-city food stops and peninsula hiking without flying anywhere.
- **Not recommended for RVs over 35 feet** on WI-57 north of Sturgeon Bay; the road narrows and summer traffic through Fish Creek is stop-and-go on weekends.
- **Works well for solo drivers** who want a full day of movement rather than a straight freeway sprint — this route stays interesting.

## Chicago, IL — Starting Point

Leave Chicago via I-94 North from the Dan Ryan Expressway. Fill up in the city or just across the Illinois state line; gas is reliably cheaper in Wisconsin. Avoid departing after 3 p.m. on a Friday — the I-94 corridor through Evanston and Waukegan runs heavily congested until 6:30 p.m. in summer.

## Kenosha, WI — 1 hour from Chicago

Kenosha sits just off I-94 at Exit 344 and earns a 25-minute stop. Drive three blocks east to the Kenosha lakefront, where a restored electric streetcar still runs along the waterfront. Simmons Island Beach is clean and free, and the Kenosha Public Museum (free admission) has a surprisingly good natural history collection if the weather is threatening. Get back on I-94 North toward Milwaukee, then merge onto I-43 North.

## Sheboygan, WI — 45 minutes from Kenosha

Sheboygan is the bratwurst capital of the Midwest, and that is not a marketing claim — it's a per-capita consumption statistic. Pull off I-43 at Exit 123 and drive east on WI-23 to the lakefront. Charcoal Inn South (1313 S. 8th St.) has been grilling brats over real charcoal since 1956. Order a double with kraut. Sheboygan's harbor is also a serious sport-fishing port in late June; if you are interested in a charter, book weeks ahead. Fill up gas here — it's your last major commercial corridor before the peninsula narrows.

## Manitowoc, WI — 30 minutes from Sheboygan

Continue north on I-43 to Exit 149 and follow US-151 East into Manitowoc. The Wisconsin Maritime Museum (75 Maritime Dr.) houses the USS Cobia, a fully restored World War II submarine you can board and walk through. Budget 45–60 minutes. The museum doubles as a real ferry terminal — the SS Badger car ferry crosses Lake Michigan to Ludington, Michigan from here, though that's a separate adventure. Manitowoc is also where WI-57 begins, which is the road that carries you all the way to the tip of the Door Peninsula.

## Green Bay, WI — 45 minutes from Manitowoc

Follow WI-57 North through Kewaunee County into Green Bay. The city is far more than Lambeau Field — the waterfront along the Fox River has been significantly redeveloped, and Bay Beach Amusement Park (1313 Bay Beach Rd.) offers rides for under $3 each. If you are driving through on a Friday evening in summer, the Green Bay waterfront farmers market runs until 6 p.m. Continue north out of Green Bay on WI-57, which transitions from a commercial strip to a two-lane rural highway within about 10 miles. This stretch through Luxemburg and Algoma is fast and scenic, running parallel to the bay.

## Sturgeon Bay, WI — 45 minutes from Green Bay

Sturgeon Bay is the gateway to the Door Peninsula, and the only town on the peninsula with full-service infrastructure. This is where you should stop for groceries, a pharmacy, or a sit-down meal if you are staying in a rental cottage farther north. The downtown drawbridge over the Sturgeon Bay Ship Canal is a genuine working canal — freighters still transit it. If you are arriving on a Saturday in July, the Canal Station Lighthouse hosts free walking tours. From here, WI-57 continues north; alternatively, WI-42 runs up the western (Green Bay) shore if you prefer that approach to Fish Creek.

## Fish Creek, WI — 30 minutes from Sturgeon Bay

Fish Creek is the social center of the peninsula and gets congested on summer weekends. Peninsula State Park (9462 Shore Rd.) is immediately north of the village — its Eagle Trail offers lake views from 180-foot dolomite bluffs and is manageable for most fitness levels in about 90 minutes. The park requires a Wisconsin State Park vehicle sticker ($28 for out-of-state vehicles, valid for one year). White Gull Inn (4225 Main St.) has been serving Door County fish boils since 1896; the Wednesday and Friday evening boils are reservation-only in summer, so book ahead.

## Sister Bay, WI — 15 minutes from Fish Creek

Sister Bay is the northern hub and a practical place to end the drive. Al Johnson's Swedish Restaurant (702 Bay Shore Dr.) is famous for the goats that graze on its sod roof — the line moves slowly at lunch but the Swedish pancakes justify it. The Sister Bay waterfront beach is swimmable in late June, with water temperatures typically reaching 65–68°F. From here, WI-57 continues to Ellison Bay and Gills Rock at the peninsula's tip, adding another 20 minutes if you want to reach the northernmost point and watch the Washington Island ferry.

## Seasonal Notes for Late June 2026

Cherries on the peninsula typically peak in mid-July; in late June you will find early sweet cherries at some orchards but the full harvest is still two to three weeks out. Mosquitoes in Peninsula State Park are active through early July — bring repellent. WI-42 and WI-57 have no closures expected in summer, but weekend afternoon traffic between Fish Creek and Ephraim can back up significantly; plan to arrive at Fish Creek before noon or after 4 p.m.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Chicago to Door County?
Pure driving time on I-94 North to I-43 North to WI-57 is approximately 3 hours 45 minutes to Sister Bay. With stops in Sheboygan, Manitowoc, and Sturgeon Bay, budget 6–7 hours total for a comfortable day.

### What is the best route from Chicago to Door County, WI?
The most efficient route is I-94 North to I-43 North, then WI-57 North from Manitowoc through Green Bay to the peninsula. This keeps you near the Lake Michigan shoreline and avoids the inland US-41 corridor, which offers less to stop for.

### Is WI-57 a good road for RVs?
WI-57 south of Sturgeon Bay handles RVs without issue. North of Sturgeon Bay, the road narrows through Fish Creek and Ephraim, and summer pedestrian and bicycle traffic makes it stressful for rigs over 35 feet. Consider parking a large RV at a Sturgeon Bay campground and driving a tow vehicle north.

### Do you need a permit to hike in Peninsula State Park?
No hiking permit is required, but you do need a Wisconsin State Park vehicle admission sticker — $28 for non-resident vehicles as of 2025. It is valid at all Wisconsin state parks for one year from purchase date and is available at the park entrance station.

### Where should I fill up gas before the Door Peninsula?
Sheboygan is the last city with competitive gas pricing and multiple station options before the peninsula. Sturgeon Bay has gas stations, but prices run higher. There are no stations north of Fish Creek on WI-57 until you double back, so fill up in Sturgeon Bay at the latest.

### Is Door County crowded in late June?
Late June is busy but slightly less packed than the July 4th week, which is the single most congested period of the year. Expect full campgrounds and booked lodging on weekends; midweek arrivals find shorter lines and easier parking throughout the peninsula.
