**This Atlanta-to-Savannah route runs approximately 320 miles via GA-16, US-129, and US-1, with 6.5 to 8 hours of pure drive time spread best over two days. Skip I-16 and instead trace Georgia's antebellum interior through Macon, Milledgeville, and the Oconee River corridor — the most historically layered version of this classic Georgia crossing.**

Most drivers default to I-75 South to I-16 East and roll into Savannah in under four hours without seeing anything. This route does the opposite: it trades interstate miles for Georgia's red-clay back roads, passing two former state capitals, one of the oldest state parks in the country, and a stretch of longleaf pine and reservoir that most Georgians haven't driven since childhood.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~320 miles
- **Total drive time:** 6.5–8 hours (not counting stops)
- **Recommended duration:** 2 days / 1 night
- **Best season:** Year-round; summer works well, though heat and humidity are significant (see hazards below)
- **Number of stops:** 5 main stops plus Atlanta start and Savannah finish
- **Starting point:** Atlanta, GA
- **Ending point:** Savannah, GA

## Who This Route Is For

- **History-focused drivers** who want antebellum architecture, Georgia's pre-Civil War capital, and genuine Southern context rather than highway miles
- **Weekend road trippers** comfortable with two-lane state roads and small-town fuel stops — not a route for drivers who want to arrive fast
- **Families with older kids (10+)** who can engage with history sites; less suited to families with toddlers who need frequent play stops
- **Not recommended for RVs over 32 feet** — GA-16 through Jasper and Putnam counties has narrow shoulders and low-clearance tree canopy in spots

## Stop 1: Atlanta, GA — Starting Point

Fuel up in Atlanta before leaving. Once you drop south on I-75 to the GA-16 junction near Griffin, gas stations thin out. We recommend filling the tank at the QT on Moreland Avenue SE or any station near I-285 South before committing to GA-16 East. Depart early — Atlanta's southbound morning traffic on I-75 toward US-19 can add 30–45 minutes on summer Fridays.

## Stop 2: Jackson, GA — Indian Springs State Park

**(~55 miles from Atlanta; approximately 1 hour via I-75 S to GA-16 E)**

Indian Springs State Park, just south of Jackson off GA-42, is one of the oldest state parks in the United States and a legitimate reason to stop. The sulfur spring that gave the park its name still flows, and the lake is swimmable — a real advantage in August heat that regularly hits 95°F in middle Georgia. This is also your last reliable full-service fuel stop before Macon on this corridor; the Chevron on GA-16 in Jackson proper is well-stocked. Plan 45–60 minutes here.

## Stop 3: Macon, GA

**(~40 miles from Jackson; approximately 45 minutes via GA-16 E)**

Macon earns a proper half-day. The Ocmulgee Mounds National Historical Park (US-80 East) preserves one of the most significant Native American earthwork complexes in the Southeast — the Earth Lodge reconstruction alone is worth the stop. Downtown, Ingleside Village Pizza on Rogers Avenue is a Macon institution and one of the better lunch stops on any Georgia road trip. In August, the Macon heat peaks mid-afternoon; plan outdoor sightseeing before noon.

Fuel note: Fill up in Macon before heading northeast toward Milledgeville. US-129 North has stations in Gray, but options are sparse between Gray and Milledgeville.

## Stop 4: Milledgeville, GA

**(~30 miles from Macon; approximately 35 minutes via US-129 N)**

Milledgeville served as Georgia's state capital from 1804 to 1868, and the built environment reflects it. The Old Governor's Mansion (120 S. Clarke St.) is open for tours Tuesday through Saturday and gives a direct look at antebellum Georgia political life. Georgia College's campus surrounds the mansion and is walkable. Flannery O'Connor's childhood home, Andalusia Farm, sits just north of town on US-441 — worth 45 minutes if literary history is your thing. Plan 1.5–2 hours total in Milledgeville.

## Stop 5: Oconee National Forest and Lake Sinclair

**(~15 miles from Milledgeville; approximately 20 minutes via US-441 N to GA-212 E)**

The Oconee National Forest and Lake Sinclair corridor along GA-212 and GA-16 East is the route's decompression stretch — long sight lines over the reservoir, longleaf pine stands, and almost no traffic on a weekday. If you're splitting this into two days, Eatonton (the county seat of Putnam County, just north on US-441) has a handful of motels and is a logical overnight stop. The Dairy Queen on US-441 in Eatonton is not glamorous, but it is cold and open late.

## Stop 6: Louisville, GA

**(~65 miles from Eatonton; approximately 1 hour 10 minutes via GA-16 E to US-1 S)**

Louisville (pronounced LOO-iss-ville locally) was Georgia's capital before Milledgeville and is undervisited even by Georgia road trippers. The Old Market House on Broad Street, built around 1758, is one of the oldest public buildings in Georgia. Louisville sits at the junction of US-1 and GA-24, and this is your last comfortable fuel and food stop before committing to the final run south to Savannah. The stretch of US-1 South from Louisville toward Sylvania and then I-16 East into Savannah is largely rural — 110 miles with limited services.

## Stop 7: Savannah, GA — Finish

**(~110 miles from Louisville; approximately 1 hour 50 minutes via US-1 S to I-16 E)**

You'll pick up I-16 East near Metter and ride it the final 50 miles into Savannah. Arrive on the west side of downtown via I-16's terminus at MLK Jr. Boulevard. If you're arriving on a summer Saturday evening, expect significant pedestrian and vehicle congestion in the Historic District. The Forsyth Park garage on Whitaker Street is the most reliable paid parking for overnight visitors.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Atlanta to Savannah via Macon and Milledgeville?

Plan on 6.5 to 8 hours of driving time across the full 320-mile route, not counting stops. With meaningful stops in Macon and Milledgeville, most drivers split this into two days, overnighting in Eatonton or Milledgeville.

### Is GA-16 safe to drive in summer?

GA-16 is a standard two-lane state highway — paved, well-maintained, and generally low-traffic. The main summer hazard is afternoon thunderstorms, which hit middle Georgia almost daily in July and August, often between 2 and 5 p.m. Pull over if visibility drops; these storms pass quickly but can be intense.

### Are there EV charging stations along this route?

DC fast chargers are sparse outside Macon. Tesla Superchargers exist in Macon (near I-75) and in Savannah, but the Milledgeville-to-Louisville segment has no reliable fast charging as of mid-2026. EV drivers should charge fully in Macon before proceeding and plan a conservative range buffer.

### Do you need reservations to tour the Old Governor's Mansion in Milledgeville?

Walk-ins are accepted Tuesday through Saturday during regular hours, but guided tour slots can fill on summer weekends. Booking a timed entry online through Georgia College's website the day before is recommended in August.

### What is the best time of year to drive this route?

March through May and October through November offer the most comfortable temperatures for outdoor stops. August is fully drivable but expect heat indexes of 100–105°F in middle Georgia — time outdoor walking for morning hours and keep the AC running.

### Is there anything to see on US-1 South between Louisville and Savannah?

The US-1 South corridor through Sylvania and Statesboro is mostly agricultural with limited attractions, though Georgia Southern University in Statesboro has a small natural history museum. This segment is primarily a transit leg — plan accordingly and fuel up in Louisville.
