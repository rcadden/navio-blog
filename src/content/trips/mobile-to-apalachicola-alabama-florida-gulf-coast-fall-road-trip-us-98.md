**This 310-mile Gulf Coast drive runs US-98 east from Mobile, Alabama to Apalachicola, Florida, covering six distinct stops in roughly 6 hours of pure drive time — best spread across 2–3 days. The verdict: the finest stretch of the central Gulf Coast in fall, when crowds thin, water temps stay warm, and oyster season peaks in Apalachicola.** 

September gives way to October along this corridor, and the timing is nearly perfect. Air temperatures sit in the low-to-mid 80s°F, the Gulf water hovers around 78–80°F, and the summer crowds that pack Gulf Shores and Destin have largely cleared out. There's also a practical reason to go now: US-98 east of Panama City Beach can close or flood during peak hurricane season, and by late September, most storm threats have passed. Check the National Hurricane Center forecast before departure — a named storm can still develop through November.

## Who This Route Is For

- **Best for couples and solo drivers** who want a slow coastal crawl with serious seafood stops and uncrowded beaches in shoulder season.
- **Good for families with older kids** (10+); Dauphin Island's Estuarium and the Gulf State Park trail system offer structured activity without theme-park pricing.
- **Not ideal for RVs over 35 ft** — the Dauphin Island ferry crossing has length restrictions, and the two-lane sections of US-98 east of Port St. Joe have tight shoulders.
- **Works as a one-way fly-in/fly-out trip** — fly into Mobile Regional Airport (MOB) and out of Northwest Florida Beaches International Airport (ECP) near Panama City Beach, or continue to Tallahassee Regional Airport (TLH).

## Mobile, AL — Start Here

Fuel up in Mobile before heading south — the Chevron stations on Government Street near I-65 are your last easy fill before the Causeway. Mobile is underrated as a food city: Wintzell's Oyster House on Dauphin Street has been shucking Gulf oysters since 1938 and is worth a proper lunch before you hit the road. The USS Alabama Battleship Memorial Park off Battleship Parkway (US-90) is a solid first-morning stop if you're starting early — plan 90 minutes.

## Dauphin Island, AL (45 min from Mobile)

Take I-10 west briefly to AL-193 south, crossing the Dauphin Island Bridge. The Dauphin Island Sea Lab's Estuarium is a hands-on aquarium focused entirely on Gulf Coast ecosystems — not a tourist trap, genuinely informative. The island's west end beach is free, wide, and almost always quiet in late September. If you want to skip backtracking, the Fort Morgan Ferry (AL-180) crosses Mobile Bay directly to Gulf Shores — the crossing takes about 40 minutes and costs around $19 per standard vehicle (verify current fares at mobilebayferry.com before departure). This saves 45 miles of driving and is the more scenic option.

## Gulf Shores / Orange Beach, AL (30 min from Dauphin Island via ferry, or 1 hr 15 min driving)

US-98 picks up at Gulf Shores and runs east through Orange Beach. Gulf State Park, accessed via AL-135, has 28 miles of trails and a sugar-white beach that rivals anything in Florida. For dinner, The Shrimp Basket on Gulf Shores Parkway is a no-frills local chain that's been doing fried Gulf shrimp right for decades — a better call than the waterfront tourist traps on AL-59. Fill your tank in Orange Beach before continuing east; gas stations become sparse approaching the Florida state line on US-98.

## Fort Walton Beach / Destin, FL (1 hr from Orange Beach)

Cross into Florida on US-98 and you're on the Emerald Coast. The Indian Temple Mound Museum in Fort Walton Beach sits directly on US-98 and is the oldest structure on the Gulf Coast — a 12-foot-tall ceremonial mound occupied around 1400 CE. Destin Harbor Boardwalk is busy even in fall, but it's the right place to book a half-day fishing charter if that's your thing; the fall amberjack and king mackerel runs are legitimate. If you're splitting the trip into two nights, Destin is the logical overnight stop — lodging is plentiful and rates drop significantly after Labor Day.

## Panama City Beach, FL (45 min from Destin)

US-98 runs straight through Panama City Beach. Skip the strip and head instead to St. Andrews State Park at the east end of the beach — it's consistently rated one of Florida's best state parks and has snorkeling in the jetty area and a ferry to Shell Island (ferry typically runs through late October; confirm seasonal schedule at floridastateparks.org). The Schooners beach bar at the end of Gulf Drive has been a locals' institution since 1984; their grouper sandwich is the real deal.

## Apalachicola, FL (1 hr 45 min from Panama City Beach)

US-98 east from Panama City Beach crosses the Hathaway Bridge, passes through Port St. Joe, and rolls into Apalachicola over the John Gorrie Memorial Bridge. This is the payoff stop. Apalachicola Bay produces roughly 90% of Florida's oysters, and late September through March is peak harvest season. Papa Joe's Oyster Bar & Grill on Water Street has been the go-to raw bar for decades. The Apalachicola National Estuarine Research Reserve runs along the waterfront and is free to walk. The town itself — a preserved 19th-century cotton port — is compact enough to cover on foot in two hours. Book accommodation early even in fall; the town has limited rooms and fills on weekends.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Mobile, AL to Apalachicola, FL?

The straight-line drive on US-98 (with the Dauphin Island ferry) covers approximately 310 miles and takes about 6 hours of moving time. With stops at beaches, state parks, and restaurants, most drivers spread this over 2 full days.

### Is US-98 along the Gulf Coast dangerous to drive in fall?

US-98 is a well-maintained two-lane and four-lane highway for most of its length, but sections east of Port St. Joe sit at near sea level and can flood during tropical weather events. Late September still falls within Atlantic hurricane season (which runs through November 30), so monitor the National Hurricane Center at nhc.noaa.gov before and during your trip.

### Are Gulf Coast beaches in Alabama and Florida open in late September?

Yes. All major beaches on this route — Dauphin Island, Gulf State Park, and St. Andrews State Park — remain open through fall. Water temperatures stay swimable (78–80°F) through October. Lifeguards may operate on reduced schedules after Labor Day; swim near staffed areas when possible.

### Do you need a reservation for the Dauphin Island Ferry?

No reservation is required for the Fort Morgan Ferry (AL-180) — it operates on a first-come, first-served basis. However, the ferry does not run 24 hours; check current seasonal schedules at mobilebayferry.com. Vehicles over certain lengths or weights may be restricted.

### When is oyster season in Apalachicola, FL?

Apalachicola Bay oysters are harvested year-round, but the season peaks from October through March when cooler water temperatures improve flavor and meat quality. Arriving in late September puts you right at the start of the prime window.

### Is this road trip suitable for a solo driver?

Yes. The route is entirely on paved, well-signed US and state highways with cell coverage throughout most of the corridor. Destin and Panama City Beach both have 24-hour services, and the drive segments between stops are short enough (45 minutes to 1 hr 45 min) that fatigue is not a significant factor.
