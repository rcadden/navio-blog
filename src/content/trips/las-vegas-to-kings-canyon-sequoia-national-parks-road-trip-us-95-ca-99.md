**This Las Vegas to Sequoia and Kings Canyon road trip covers roughly 440 miles via I-15 South, CA-58, and CA-99 North, with a total drive time of 8–9 hours spread across 2–3 days. It's the best summer alternative for drivers who want towering trees and alpine scenery without competing for Yosemite Valley's overloaded permit system.**

In July 2026, Yosemite Valley day-use reservations are still required and frequently sell out weeks in advance. Kings Canyon National Park (CA-180) and Sequoia National Park (Generals Highway / CA-198) offer nearly identical giant-tree payoffs — including the world's largest tree by volume, General Sherman — with far more accessible entry. This route threads through the Mojave Desert, the San Joaquin Valley, and straight into the Sierra Nevada foothills, and it's doable in a standard passenger car for most of the drive.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~440 miles
- **Total drive time:** 8–9 hours (behind the wheel only)
- **Recommended trip length:** 2–3 days
- **Best season:** Late May through early October; peak summer is July–August
- **Number of stops:** 5 major stops
- **Starting point:** Las Vegas, NV
- **Ending point:** Giant Forest, Sequoia National Park, CA

## Who This Route Is For

- **Families with kids** who want the giant-tree experience without a complex permit lottery
- **Drivers in standard sedans or SUVs** — most of this route is freeway until the final mountain segment; Generals Highway has tight switchbacks but is passable for vehicles under 22 ft
- **Not recommended for RVs or trailers over 22 feet** on Generals Highway (CA-198) between Potwisha Campground and Giant Forest — the National Park Service enforces this restriction
- **Good for first-time western road trippers** who want a clear, logical corridor rather than a loop

## Stop 1: Las Vegas, NV — Departure and Fuel

Before leaving the Las Vegas metro, fill the tank completely. The stretch of I-15 South into the Mojave is not the problem — gas is available in Baker — but prices spike sharply once you leave the Nevada state line. We recommend fueling at a Costco on the south side of Las Vegas (near the I-15/I-215 interchange) if you have a membership; it consistently runs 20–30 cents per gallon cheaper than Mojave corridor stations. Depart early: the Mojave in July regularly hits 108–115°F by midday, and you want to clear Baker before 11 a.m.

## Stop 2: Baker, CA (approx. 1 hr 30 min from Las Vegas via I-15 South)

Baker is your last reliable fuel and water stop before Barstow. The Mad Greek Café on Baker Boulevard has been feeding road trippers on this corridor for decades — the gyro plate and date shakes are both worth a 20-minute stop. Check your coolant and tire pressure here, especially in summer heat. The famous World's Tallest Thermometer sign makes for a quick photo op. Do not skip fueling here if you're below half a tank.

**Summer hazard:** Extreme heat warnings are routine in Baker in July. If your vehicle is running hot or your AC is struggling, address it here — the next stretch to Barstow on I-15 has limited pull-off infrastructure.

## Stop 3: Barstow, CA (approx. 35 min from Baker via I-15 West)

Barstow is a practical pivot point, not a destination. From here, take CA-58 West toward Bakersfield — this is the faster, less congested alternative to dropping south to I-10. The drive on CA-58 through the Mojave Desert and over Tehachapi Pass is genuinely interesting: wind farms blanket the ridgelines above Tehachapi, and the elevation gain to roughly 4,000 feet brings welcome temperature relief. Grab supplies at a Stater Bros. in Barstow before the CA-58 leg if you're planning to cook at a campsite in the parks.

## Stop 4: Bakersfield, CA (approx. 2 hrs from Barstow via CA-58 West)

Bakersfield sits at the bottom of the San Joaquin Valley and is your gateway north toward the Sierra Nevada. It's not a scenic stop, but it's essential for logistics: fuel here (gas is cheaper in the valley than anywhere in the parks), grab a full grocery run at a Vons or Grocery Outlet, and eat a real meal before the mountain drive. Sonder's Café on H Street is a local breakfast and lunch spot worth a stop if you're passing through midday. From Bakersfield, take CA-99 North roughly 50 miles to Fresno, then CA-180 East into Kings Canyon National Park.

## Stop 5: Kings Canyon National Park — Grant Grove & Cedar Grove (approx. 2 hrs from Bakersfield via CA-99 N and CA-180 E)

The Kings Canyon Scenic Byway (CA-180) descends into one of the deepest canyons in North America — deeper than the Grand Canyon at its lowest point. Grant Grove Village is your first major stop: the General Grant Tree Trail is a 0.5-mile paved loop around the second-largest tree on Earth, manageable for most ages and fitness levels. The Kings Canyon Visitor Center here has current road conditions and fire information, which matters in July when the Cedar Grove section of CA-180 can occasionally close for emergency maintenance or fire activity.

**July 2026 note:** Cedar Grove Road (the lower canyon section of CA-180) is typically open by late May and remains open through October, but check nps.gov/seki for any active closures before committing to the canyon descent. The road dead-ends at Road's End — there's no through route to Sequoia from Cedar Grove; you must backtrack to Grant Grove and take Generals Highway south.

Fill your gas tank at the Grant Grove Village area before heading south — the Generals Highway stretch into Sequoia has no fuel.

## Stop 6: Sequoia National Park – Giant Forest (approx. 1 hr from Grant Grove via Generals Highway / CA-198)

Generals Highway connects Kings Canyon and Sequoia and is the scenic centerpiece of the entire trip. The road climbs through mixed conifer forest to over 7,000 feet before dropping toward Giant Forest. The General Sherman Tree — the largest tree by volume on the planet at 52,500 cubic feet — sits just off the Wolverton Road spur. The Congress Trail loop (2 miles, paved) connects Sherman to a dozen other massive sequoias and is the single best walk on this entire route.

Lodging options include Wuksachi Lodge (book well in advance for July) or campgrounds at Lodgepole (reservable through recreation.gov). Cell service is minimal inside both parks — download offline maps before leaving Bakersfield.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Las Vegas to Sequoia National Park?
The drive is approximately 440 miles and takes 8–9 hours of total driving time, not counting stops. Most drivers split it over two days, overnighting in Bakersfield or Fresno before the final mountain leg on CA-180 and Generals Highway.

### Is Generals Highway open in July?
Yes, Generals Highway (the road connecting Kings Canyon and Sequoia national parks) is typically fully open from late spring through October. Check nps.gov/seki the day before your drive for any emergency closures related to fire or rockfall, both of which occur occasionally in summer.

### Do you need a reservation or permit to enter Sequoia and Kings Canyon in summer 2026?
As of current NPS policy, Sequoia and Kings Canyon do not require timed-entry reservations the way Yosemite Valley does. Standard vehicle entry fees apply (covered by an America the Beautiful pass if you have one). Campsite reservations at Lodgepole and other developed campgrounds are strongly recommended and book out weeks ahead for July weekends.

### Can you drive an RV from Las Vegas to Sequoia National Park?
The I-15 and CA-99 freeway portions are RV-friendly. However, the NPS restricts vehicles over 22 feet on Generals Highway between Potwisha Campground and Giant Forest. RVs over that length must use the Lodgepole/Wuksachi area as their base and cannot access the lower Foothills section via CA-198 without turning around.

### Where should you stop for gas on the Las Vegas to Sequoia drive?
Fuel in Las Vegas before departure, again in Baker (I-15 at Baker Boulevard), and again in Bakersfield before heading into the mountains. There is no reliable fuel between Fresno and the parks on CA-180, and none on Generals Highway inside the parks themselves.

### Is this drive safe in extreme summer heat?
The Mojave Desert segment (Las Vegas to Barstow) is the heat-risk zone, regularly exceeding 110°F in July. Drive it in the morning, ensure your coolant is topped off, carry at least 2 gallons of water per person, and don't rely solely on a single gas station in Baker. Once you reach Tehachapi Pass on CA-58 and the San Joaquin Valley, temperatures moderate significantly.
