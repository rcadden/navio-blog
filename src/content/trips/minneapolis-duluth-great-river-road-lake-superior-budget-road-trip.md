**This 480-mile loop departs Minneapolis, runs south along the Mississippi on the Great River Road (US-61 / MN-35), then swings north to Duluth and up the Lake Superior shore — total driving time of 9–11 hours spread across 3 days. It's one of the most cost-effective summer drives in the Midwest: state park camping runs $20–$28/night, the roads are free, and food is cheap if you know where to stop.**

Most summer road trips drain your wallet before you leave your home state. This route proves the opposite. Minnesota's state park system is genuinely excellent, the scenery shifts from bluff-lined river valley to cold-water lakeshore, and you can do the whole thing — gas, food, camping — for well under $150 per person if you're disciplined. We've driven it, priced it out, and laid it below stop by stop.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~480 miles
- **Total drive time:** 9–11 hours (3 days recommended)
- **Best season:** Late June through August
- **Number of stops:** 6 major stops
- **Starting point:** Minneapolis, MN
- **Ending point:** Split Rock Lighthouse State Park, MN (return to Minneapolis via MN-61 is 2 hrs)

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for budget travelers** who want to average under $50/person/day including gas, food, and lodging
- **Good for tent campers and car campers** — every overnight stop has a state park campground with reservable sites
- **Not recommended for large RVs (over 30 ft)** on the Skyline Parkway segment in Duluth; some pull-offs are tight
- **Solid solo or couples trip** — the route is well-marked and low-stress to navigate

## Stop 1: Minneapolis, MN — Starting Point

Fill the tank before you leave the Twin Cities metro. Gas in Minneapolis proper typically runs $0.10–$0.20/gallon cheaper than rural stations along US-61. Grab breakfast at Al's Breakfast in Dinkytown (cash only, under $12 a plate) before heading southeast on US-61. You'll cross into river bluff country within 45 minutes.

## Stop 2: Red Wing, MN
*(About 60 miles / 1 hour from Minneapolis via US-61)*

Red Wing sits where the Mississippi widens into Lake Pepin — the largest naturally occurring lake on the river. Park free at Levee Park and walk the waterfront. Barn Bluff trailhead is a 10-minute walk from downtown; the 1.5-mile round trip to the summit gives you a panoramic view of the river valley at zero cost. If you need lunch, Hanisch Bakery & Coffee Shop on West 3rd Street has sandwiches under $9.

This stretch of US-61 is part of the Great River Road National Scenic Byway — the green pilot's wheel signs mark the route the whole way south.

## Stop 3: Winona, MN
*(About 75 miles / 1 hour 15 minutes from Red Wing via US-61)*

Winona is a bluff-top river town that most people drive through without stopping — a mistake. The Winona County Historical Society Museum charges $5 admission and covers the region's logging and river trade history well. More importantly, Garvin Heights Park is a free overlook 575 feet above the river with a paved road to the top. Camp tonight at Winona KOA (tent sites ~$32) or drive 12 miles northwest to Whitewater State Park (reservable sites from $20 — the better value).

## Stop 4: Great River Bluffs State Park
*(About 20 miles / 25 minutes from Winona via US-61 then MN-12)*

This is an underused state park perched on a 500-foot bluff above the Mississippi floodplain. The King's Bluff Nature Trail (2.5 miles) is the standout hike — free with a $7/day vehicle permit or included if you already bought an annual Minnesota State Parks pass ($35, worth it if you're hitting multiple parks). Campsites here run $20/night. Fill up on gas in La Crescent before leaving — the next reliable station north is back on US-61 heading toward the Twin Cities.

## Stop 5: Duluth, MN — Canal Park and Skyline Parkway
*(About 155 miles / 2 hours 30 minutes from Winona via I-90 W to I-35 N)*

Duluth is the budget road tripper's secret weapon in the upper Midwest. Canal Park is free to walk, and watching 1,000-foot ore boats pass through the aerial lift bridge costs nothing. For food, try OMC Smokehouse on West Michigan Street — a pulled pork sandwich runs about $12 and the portions are large. Skyline Parkway (a city-maintained scenic road) loops 27 miles above the city with multiple free overlooks; Enger Tower is the best single stop.

Camp at Jay Cooke State Park, 15 miles southwest of Duluth on MN-210 (sites from $20), or Spirit Mountain Campground inside the city (sites from $28 with hookups if you need them). This is your best refueling point before the North Shore — gas in Duluth is consistently cheaper than Two Harbors or Silver Bay.

If you've done any long drives through the Northern Rockies, this corridor has a similar expansive feel — though the [Seattle to Glacier National Park route via US-2 and US-89](https://mynavio.com/blog/seattle-to-glacier-national-park-northern-rockies-road-trip-us-2-us-89) is a different beast in terms of elevation and scale.

## Stop 6: Gooseberry Falls State Park
*(About 40 miles / 50 minutes from Duluth via MN-61)*

MN-61 north from Duluth is one of the best free drives in the country — the road hugs Lake Superior's shoreline with pull-offs every few miles. Gooseberry Falls State Park has five waterfalls accessible from the parking lot; the lower falls are a 5-minute walk from the visitor center. Vehicle permit ($7/day) required. The park's campground books out weeks in advance in July — reserve through the Minnesota DNR reservation system before you leave home.

**Summer hazard note:** MN-61 north of Two Harbors can experience dense lake fog in the morning hours, particularly in early July when lake temperatures are still cold. Reduce speed and use low beams if visibility drops.

## Stop 7: Split Rock Lighthouse State Park
*(About 20 miles / 25 minutes from Gooseberry Falls via MN-61)*

Split Rock Lighthouse State Park is the natural end point for the North Shore leg. The lighthouse itself charges $12 admission for a guided tour (Minnesota Historical Society operates it), but the surrounding trail system and lake access are covered by the state park vehicle permit. The pebble beach below the lighthouse is a good place to end the drive. Return to Minneapolis via MN-61 south — it's 155 miles and about 2 hours 30 minutes.

## FAQ

### How much does this Minneapolis to Duluth road trip cost per person?

Budget $40–$55 per person per day covering gas (split between two people in a car getting 30 mpg), state park camping ($20–$28/night per site), and meals at diners and park-side food. Three days all-in typically runs $120–$165 per person.

### Is MN-61 along Lake Superior safe to drive in summer?

Yes — MN-61 is a well-maintained two-lane highway with passing zones. The main summer hazards are morning fog near the lakeshore and heavy holiday weekend traffic (especially July 4th weekend). Leave Duluth before 8 a.m. on July 4th to avoid northbound congestion.

### Do you need a reservation for Minnesota state park campgrounds in July?

Yes. July is peak season and popular parks like Gooseberry Falls and Split Rock Lighthouse book out 2–3 weeks ahead. Reserve at mnrec.tentanttrailer.com as early as possible; cancellations open up within 48 hours of arrival if you're flexible.

### Where should you fill up gas before the North Shore drive?

Fill up in Duluth before heading north on MN-61. Gas prices in Two Harbors and Silver Bay run $0.15–$0.25/gallon higher than Duluth. There are no stations between Silver Bay and Grand Marais (about 55 miles), so top off again in Silver Bay if you're continuing further north.

### Is the Great River Road (US-61) suitable for motorcycles?

The Great River Road between Minneapolis and Winona is excellent motorcycle territory — smooth pavement, moderate curves, and minimal truck traffic. Watch for gravel on county road intersections and river-bottom humidity that can create slick patches in early morning.

### How long does it take to drive from Minneapolis to Duluth directly?

The direct route via I-35 N is 155 miles and takes about 2 hours 30 minutes without stops. This road trip extends that to 480 miles over 3 days by routing south through the Mississippi River bluffs before heading north to Duluth and the Lake Superior shore.
