**This 520-mile loop from Seattle covers Olympic National Park and Mount Rainier National Park in one cohesive family itinerary, with roughly 10–12 hours of total driving best spread over 5–7 days. It is the most park-dense road trip you can build in Washington state without crossing into Oregon, and summer 2026 conditions make both parks accessible at their best.**

Two UNESCO-recognized landscapes, a temperate rain forest, alpine meadows, and tidepool beaches — this route delivers genuine variety without the gridlock of the Southwest's most-visited corridors. Kids who are bored of desert scenery will find the shift from moss-draped firs to glaciated volcano startling in the best way.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~520 miles
- **Total drive time:** 10–12 hours (driving only)
- **Recommended trip length:** 5–7 days
- **Best season:** Mid-June through September
- **Number of major stops:** 6
- **Starting point:** Seattle, WA
- **Ending point:** Ashford, WA (Mount Rainier's southwest gateway)

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for families with kids ages 5–14** — short hikes, tide pools, and a working volcano hold attention across age groups
- **Good fit for standard SUVs and minivans**; most roads are paved two-lane highways, though the unpaved Mora Road spur near Rialto Beach is passable for passenger vehicles
- **Not recommended for RVs over 35 ft** on the Hurricane Ridge Road switchbacks or the Hoh Rain Forest access road
- **Works well as a first Pacific Northwest trip** for families coming from the Midwest or South who have not yet seen old-growth forest or a glaciated stratovolcano

## Seattle, WA — Start Here

Fuel up in Seattle before crossing Puget Sound. Gas is reliably priced in the city; once you board the Washington State Ferry from Edmonds to Kingston (or drive the Hood Canal Bridge on US-101), stations thin out. We recommend filling the tank at any station near the Edmonds terminal. The ferry crossing itself — about 30 minutes — is a low-cost, kid-pleasing start to the trip.

## Olympic National Park – Hurricane Ridge
*(Approx. 1 hr 45 min from Seattle via ferry + US-101 North to Hurricane Ridge Road)*

From Port Angeles, Hurricane Ridge Road climbs 17 miles to 5,242 feet. The visitor center at the top has ranger-led programs daily in summer, and black-tailed deer graze close enough to photograph without a zoom lens. **Seasonal note for June 2026:** Hurricane Ridge Road typically opens fully by mid-June, but confirm current status with the Olympic National Park visitor center at (360) 565-3130 before departing Port Angeles — late-season snowpack occasionally delays the opening by a week or two. Allow 3–4 hours at the ridge before descending.

## Hoh Rain Forest
*(Approx. 1 hr 50 min from Hurricane Ridge via US-101 South through Forks)*

US-101 South from Port Angeles runs through Forks — yes, that Forks — where you'll find the Forks Coffee Shop on South Forks Avenue, a reliable diner stop for burgers and pie that opens at 6 a.m. Gas up in Forks; the next reliable station heading south toward Kalaloch is 35+ miles away.

The Hoh Rain Forest Visitor Center sits at the end of a 19-mile spur road off US-101. The Hall of Mosses Trail is 0.8 miles and entirely stroller-accessible on packed gravel — the most-photographed old-growth grove in the park. The Hoh River Trail extends further for families wanting a longer walk. Average annual rainfall here exceeds 140 inches, so pack rain layers even in June.

## Ruby Beach
*(Approx. 30 min south of the Hoh Rain Forest spur on US-101)*

Ruby Beach requires a short 0.2-mile walk from the pullout on US-101 to reach sea stacks and tide pools loaded with sea stars, anemones, and hermit crabs. Low tide in late June typically falls in the morning hours — check tide tables before you arrive to time this stop for maximum tidepool access. This is one of the best unstructured science moments on the entire route for kids.

## Lake Quinault
*(Approx. 35 min south of Ruby Beach on US-101)*

Lake Quinault Lodge — a 1926 National Historic Landmark — sits on the lake's south shore and serves lunch daily in its Roosevelt Dining Room. Even if you're not staying overnight, it's worth stopping for a meal and a walk on the Rain Forest Loop Trail (4 miles, mostly flat). This southwest corner of Olympic National Park receives slightly less traffic than the Hoh, and the big-leaf maple groves here are just as impressive.

## Olympia, WA
*(Approx. 1 hr 15 min southeast of Lake Quinault on US-101 South)*

Olympia is a practical overnight stop with full services — grocery stores, fuel, and family-friendly hotels near I-5. It's a good place to do laundry mid-trip. The Hands On Children's Museum downtown is worth two hours if you have kids under 10. Fill your tank here before heading east toward Mount Rainier; the SR-706 corridor through Elbe to Ashford has limited fuel options.

If your family has already explored the Northern Rockies corridor, our [Seattle to Glacier National Park guide](https://mynavio.com/blog/seattle-to-glacier-national-park-northern-rockies-road-trip-us-2-us-89) covers the other major Washington state national park route for comparison.

## Mount Rainier National Park – Paradise
*(Approx. 1 hr 40 min from Olympia via I-5 North to SR-7 East to SR-706)*

SR-706 enters Mount Rainier National Park through the Nisqually Entrance, the park's busiest gate and the one with the longest operating season. The road continues to Paradise at 5,400 feet — the most-visited subalpine meadow in the United States. In late June, wildflower bloom is at or approaching peak, with lupine and avalanche lilies covering the slopes below the Nisqually Glacier.

**Timed entry permits:** Mount Rainier implemented a timed entry reservation system for the Paradise and Sunrise corridors in recent summers. For summer 2026, check recreation.gov starting in early spring — reservations typically release 2–3 months in advance and sell out for peak weekends. Arrive before 9 a.m. or after 3 p.m. to reduce wait times at the Nisqually gate if you don't have a timed entry slot.

The Alta Vista Trail (1.7 miles, 500 ft gain) is the best family hike at Paradise — manageable for kids 6 and up and rewarded with close views of the Nisqually Glacier. The Jackson Visitor Center has exhibits on volcanic geology that hold up well for middle-school-age kids.

Base yourself in Ashford for lodging; Whittaker's Bunkhouse and the National Park Inn at Longmire both book out weeks in advance for summer weekends.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive the Olympic Peninsula loop from Seattle?
The driving-only time from Seattle through Olympic National Park and back to the Mount Rainier area is approximately 10–12 hours total. Spread over 5–7 days with park time included, expect 3–5 hours of driving per day.

### Do you need a permit to enter Olympic National Park in summer 2026?
Olympic National Park does not currently require a timed entry permit for most areas, but the Hoh Rain Forest parking lot fills by 9–10 a.m. on summer weekends. Arriving before 8 a.m. or after 4 p.m. avoids the worst congestion. A standard America the Beautiful pass ($80/year) covers entrance fees for both Olympic and Mount Rainier.

### Is Hurricane Ridge Road safe for family vehicles in June?
Yes, Hurricane Ridge Road is a paved two-lane highway suitable for passenger cars and most SUVs. The road gains 5,000 feet over 17 miles with guardrails on most exposed sections. Fog and occasional late-season snow patches are possible in early June; check road conditions at nps.gov/olym before departure.

### Does Mount Rainier require a timed entry reservation in summer?
Mount Rainier has used a timed entry system for the Paradise corridor during peak summer weekends in recent years. For 2026, reservations are expected to be available on recreation.gov. Day visitors without reservations can still enter early (before 9 a.m.) or late (after 3 p.m.) without a reservation during most operating windows.

### What is the best hike at Mount Rainier for families with young kids?
The Alta Vista Trail from the Paradise parking area is 1.7 miles with 500 feet of elevation gain and is manageable for children 6 and older. The Skyline Trail loop (5.5 miles) is a step up for older kids. Both offer direct views of the Nisqually Glacier and wildflower meadows in late June.

### Where should you fill up gas before entering Mount Rainier National Park?
Fill up in Elbe or Eatonville on SR-7 before turning onto SR-706. There is no fuel available inside the park, and the nearest stations east of the Nisqually Entrance are 15+ miles back toward Ashford or further west in Elbe.
