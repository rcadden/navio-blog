**This 100-mile Florida Panhandle road trip follows US-98 and Florida State Road 30A (FL-30A) from Panama City Beach west to Pensacola Beach, covering roughly 2.5 hours of pure drive time over a recommended 3–4 days. The verdict: the Gulf's clearest water and whitest sand, with enough seafood stops and state parks to justify a long weekend rather than a single push.**

The Panhandle is often overlooked in favor of South Florida, but in late June the Emerald Coast delivers water temperatures in the low 80s°F and quartz-sand beaches that stay cooler underfoot than typical Gulf shores. US-98 is the spine of this trip; FL-30A is the detour that makes it worth doing.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~100 miles (Panama City Beach to Pensacola Beach)
- **Total drive time:** 2.5–3 hours point-to-point; plan 3–4 days with stops
- **Best season:** Late spring and early fall (June is peak summer — expect crowds and high accommodation rates)
- **Number of stops:** 7
- **Starting point:** Panama City Beach, FL
- **Ending point:** Pensacola Beach, FL

## Who This Route Is For

- **Families with kids:** Gulf-side state parks have calm, shallow water and clean facilities — ideal for young swimmers.
- **Seafood-focused travelers:** Every town on this stretch has at least one waterfront fish house worth stopping at.
- **RV and tow-vehicle drivers:** US-98 handles large rigs comfortably; FL-30A has tighter turns and low clearances at some beach-access lots — vehicles over 26 ft should stick to US-98.
- **First-time Panhandle visitors:** This is the most direct way to see the full range of the coast without doubling back.

## Panama City Beach (Start)

Fuel up here before heading west — gas stations thin out on FL-30A. Panama City Beach is busy in late June, but the western end near Camp Helen State Park is quieter. Grab breakfast at Thomas Donut & Snack Shop on Front Beach Road, a local institution that has been serving fried donuts since 1971. From here, pick up US-98 West.

## Grayton Beach State Park (About 45 minutes from Panama City Beach)

Turn south off US-98 onto CR-283 to reach Grayton Beach State Park, consistently ranked among the top beaches in the United States. The park charges a $6 per-vehicle entry fee. Western Lake, a rare coastal dune lake, sits just behind the beach and is worth a short kayak or paddleboard session. Restrooms and freshwater showers are available. **Summer hazard:** Double red flags mean no swimming — check the flag system before entering the water. Rip currents are common along this stretch in June when afternoon thunderstorms roll in.

## Seaside & the FL-30A Corridor (15 minutes from Grayton Beach)

FL-30A peels off US-98 and runs directly along the coast through a string of small communities. Seaside is the most photographed of them — the pastel town squares and beachside pavilions are worth a 30-minute walk. Grab lunch at Bud & Alley's Waterfront Restaurant, which has been serving Gulf fish tacos and grouper sandwiches since 1986. The entire FL-30A stretch is 24 miles; budget 45 minutes if you stop at Watercolor Beach or Rosemary Beach on the eastern end. Rejoin US-98 at the FL-30A western terminus near Santa Rosa Beach.

## Destin (30 minutes from the FL-30A western end)

Destin sits on a narrow peninsula between Choctawhatchee Bay and the Gulf. The Destin Harbor Boardwalk is the place to watch charter fishing boats unload their catch in the afternoon. For dinner, AJ's Seafood & Oyster Bar on Harbor Boulevard has outdoor seating over the water and serves locally caught amberjack and crab claws. Destin is also a practical stop: the Buc-ee's on US-98 East has the cheapest fuel on this stretch and is the last large-format gas station before the less-commercial Navarre Beach segment.

## Fort Walton Beach (15 minutes from Destin)

Cross the Mid-Bay Bridge (toll: $4.00 for passenger vehicles, E-Pass accepted) or stay on US-98 along the bay to reach Fort Walton Beach. The Indian Temple Mound Museum on Miracle Strip Parkway is a legitimate stop — the pre-Columbian ceremonial mound sits right in the middle of town and takes about 45 minutes to explore. This is also a good overnight base: chain hotels here are noticeably cheaper than Destin or Seaside.

## Navarre Beach (20 minutes from Fort Walton Beach)

Navarre Beach is one of the least commercialized towns on this corridor, which makes it useful for a mid-afternoon beach break without fighting for parking. The Navarre Beach Marine Sanctuary has an underwater reef and snorkel trail accessible from the beach — free to use, no permit required. If you're [planning a longer Gulf Coast loop through the Texas and Louisiana coast](https://mynavio.com/blog/austin-to-new-orleans-gulf-coast-road-trip-galveston-lafayette), note that the western Panhandle marks the tonal shift from resort-heavy Florida to the more low-key Gulf towns.

## Pensacola Beach (25 minutes from Navarre Beach)

Cross the Bob Sikes Bridge (US-98 Business) onto Santa Rosa Island to reach Pensacola Beach. The Gulf Islands National Seashore begins here and stretches west into Mississippi — the Fort Pickens area at the western tip of the island is a 30-minute drive from the main strip and worth it for the Civil War-era fort and uncrowded beach. For a final dinner, The Deck Bar & Grill on Fort Pickens Road has open-air seating directly on the Gulf and serves raw oysters and grilled Gulf shrimp. Parking at Fort Pickens requires a National Parks pass or $25 per vehicle weekly fee.

**June-specific note:** Afternoon thunderstorms are nearly daily on the Panhandle in late June. Plan beach time for mornings and use the 2–4 p.m. window for driving or indoor stops. Hurricane season is active — check the National Hurricane Center (nhc.noaa.gov) before departure and monitor forecasts daily.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Panama City Beach to Pensacola Beach?

The direct drive on US-98 West takes approximately 2.5 hours without stops. Adding the FL-30A detour and typical traffic through Destin in summer pushes total driving time to 3.5–4 hours. We recommend spreading this over at least two days.

### Is US-98 through the Florida Panhandle safe to drive in summer?

US-98 is a well-maintained four-lane highway for most of its Panhandle length and is safe for all vehicle types. The primary hazard is heavy tourist traffic between Destin and Fort Walton Beach, where congestion can add 30–45 minutes during peak summer weekends. Avoid driving this segment between noon and 3 p.m. on Saturdays in June and July.

### Do you need a permit to access Gulf Islands National Seashore?

No permit is required, but there is an entry fee: $25 per vehicle for a 7-day pass, or free with an America the Beautiful annual pass. The Fort Pickens area within the seashore sometimes reaches vehicle capacity on peak summer days and may implement a reservation system — check recreation.gov before you go.

### Are there EV charging stations along US-98 on the Florida Panhandle?

Yes. There are Level 2 and DC Fast Chargers in Panama City Beach, Destin, and Pensacola Beach. The Buc-ee's in Destin has multiple DC Fast Charge stations. The FL-30A corridor between Grayton Beach and Seaside has limited charging infrastructure, so EV drivers should arrive at FL-30A with a full charge.

### What are the biggest beach crowds like in late June on the Panhandle?

Late June is peak season. Destin, Seaside, and Pensacola Beach are the most crowded nodes. Beach parking at public access points fills by 9 a.m. on weekends. Grayton Beach State Park and the Fort Pickens section of Gulf Islands National Seashore see fewer visitors than the commercial beaches, making them better daytime choices if you want space.

### Can you drive an RV on FL-30A?

FL-30A is not recommended for RVs or trailers over 26 feet. The road has sharp curves, low-clearance bridges, and parking lots with tight entries. RV travelers should stay on US-98, which runs parallel and connects to all the same towns.
