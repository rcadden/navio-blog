**This Atlanta-to-Savannah road trip loops south and east via I-75, I-95, and US-17, covering roughly 380 miles in 6.5–8 hours of total drive time with stops. It's the best Georgia road trip for drivers who want a beach detour through the Golden Isles before landing in Savannah's historic district.**

Most drivers punch straight up I-16 and call it a day. This route earns its extra mileage: you'll eat real Georgia BBQ in Forsyth, stretch your legs on the undeveloped Atlantic shore of Jekyll Island, and roll into Savannah on scenic US-17 through marsh and live oak. August heat is real — plan accordingly.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~380 miles
- **Total drive time:** 6.5–8 hours with stops
- **Best season:** Late spring and fall; doable in summer with early starts
- **Number of stops:** 4 (Forsyth, Valdosta, Brunswick, Jekyll Island)
- **Starting point:** Atlanta, GA
- **Ending point:** Savannah, GA

## Who This Route Is For

- **Beach lovers** who want a half-day on the Georgia coast before a Savannah arrival
- **Families with kids** — Jekyll Island's compact, car-free beach loop is easy with children
- **Drivers avoiding I-16 monotony** who prefer a longer but more varied route
- **Not recommended for RVs over 35 ft** on the Jekyll Island causeway (low bridge clearance restrictions apply; confirm current limits at the Jekyll Island Authority before departure)

## Stop 1: Forsyth, GA *(about 55 miles, 55 minutes south of Atlanta on I-75)*

Forsyth is your first real fuel and food stop before the drive opens up into central Georgia farmland. Fill up at any station on GA-42 off Exit 186 — this is the last competitive-price fuel before Macon-area pricing climbs. For lunch, head straight to Cheryl's Diner on North Lee Street, a no-frills counter-service spot that's been feeding I-75 travelers for decades. The meat-and-three plate — usually chicken, collard greens, and cornbread — runs under $12 and hits harder than anything you'll find at a chain. Budget 30–40 minutes here.

Note for August 2026: Afternoon temperatures in central Georgia regularly exceed 95°F. If you're driving a vehicle prone to overheating, check coolant levels before leaving Atlanta and monitor your temperature gauge through this stretch.

## Stop 2: Valdosta, GA *(about 170 miles, 2 hours 20 minutes south of Forsyth on I-75)*

Valdosta is your midpoint pivot. The city sits at the junction of I-75 and I-10 and is the last major fuel stop before you jog east toward the coast on I-10 and then south on I-95. Fill up here — gas prices thin out favorably compared to coastal Georgia. The Valdosta stretch of I-75 also passes Wild Adventures Theme Park if you're traveling with children who need a longer break, though budget 3–4 hours if you stop.

From Valdosta, take I-75 South briefly, then merge onto I-10 East toward Jacksonville, Florida, before picking up I-95 North toward Brunswick. The drive from Valdosta to Brunswick is about 80 miles and takes roughly 1 hour 10 minutes.

## Stop 3: Brunswick, GA *(about 80 miles, 1 hour 10 minutes from Valdosta on I-10 E / I-95 N)*

Brunswick is the gateway city to the Golden Isles and a worthwhile stop in its own right. The Historic Downtown district on Newcastle Street has genuine 19th-century commercial architecture and a working waterfront on the Brunswick River. Park near Mary Ross Waterfront Park and walk to Indigo Coast Brewing Company for a cold drink before the Jekyll leg. Brunswick is also your last chance to stock up on sunscreen, water, and snacks at a full grocery store (Publix on US-17) before the causeway.

For coastal Georgia context, our [Charleston to Savannah Lowcountry food road trip guide](https://mynavio.com/blog/charleston-to-savannah-lowcountry-food-road-trip-us-17-sc-174) covers US-17's northern extension if you plan to continue up the coast after Savannah.

## Stop 4: Jekyll Island, GA *(about 10 miles, 15 minutes from Brunswick via the Jekyll Island Causeway / GA-520)*

Jekyll Island State Park charges a daily parking/access fee (currently $8 per vehicle; confirm at jekyllisland.com before arrival). The island's 20-mile system of paved paths circles beaches, the Jekyll Island Historic District, and Georgia's largest sea turtle nesting area. In late August, loggerhead sea turtle nesting season is winding down — you may see marked nests on the beach. Stay off them.

Driftwood Beach on the island's north end is the most photographed spot, with bleached tree skeletons standing in the surf. It's worth 30 minutes. Summer water temperatures hover around 82°F, so swimming is comfortable, but afternoon thunderstorms roll in fast off the Atlantic — typically between 2–5 PM in August. Plan beach time for the morning.

Before leaving Jekyll, top off gas back in Brunswick on US-17 North; there are no fuel stations on the island.

## Stop 5: Savannah, GA *(about 80 miles, 1 hour 20 minutes from Jekyll Island via US-17 N)*

From Jekyll, take GA-520 back to Brunswick, then pick up US-17 North directly to Savannah. This stretch of US-17 is the payoff: two-lane road through salt marsh, hammock forest, and tidal creeks, crossing the Altamaha and Ogeechee rivers before the Savannah city limits. It's slower than I-95 by about 20 minutes, but the marsh scenery is worth it.

Arrive via US-17's merge onto US-80 West into downtown Savannah. The Historic District's street parking is metered and competitive in August — Savannah's peak tourist season extends through Labor Day. We recommend the Bryan Street Garage or the Liberty Street Garage for reliable overnight parking if you're staying downtown.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Atlanta to Savannah via Jekyll Island?

Expect 6.5–8 hours total, including a lunch stop in Forsyth, a fuel stop in Valdosta, and 2–3 hours on Jekyll Island. Pure drive time without stops is about 5 hours 15 minutes for the ~380-mile route via I-75 S, I-10 E, I-95 N, GA-520, and US-17 N.

### Is Jekyll Island worth the detour on an Atlanta to Savannah road trip?

Yes, if you want a beach stop — Jekyll Island adds roughly 20 miles and 30–40 minutes of driving versus going straight up I-95, and the $8 access fee is reasonable. The island is far less crowded than St. Simons Island in summer and requires no ferry.

### Is US-17 between Brunswick and Savannah safe to drive?

US-17 is a two-lane state highway with posted speeds of 55 mph. It's well-maintained but has limited passing zones and occasional farm equipment in rural sections. Drive it in daylight — the road has minimal lighting between towns. It is not a good choice in heavy rain or fog.

### Are there any road closures or hazards on this route in August?

In August 2026, watch for tropical weather: the Atlantic hurricane season peaks in mid-August through mid-September. Monitor the National Hurricane Center (nhc.noaa.gov) before departure. I-95 southbound in Georgia can see contraflow evacuations during storm approaches. The Jekyll Island Causeway closes during mandatory evacuations.

### Do you need a reservation to visit Jekyll Island?

No reservation is required to access Jekyll Island — you pay the daily vehicle fee at the causeway toll booth. However, if you plan to stay overnight, Jekyll Island's campground and hotels book out weeks in advance in summer. Day visitors have no booking requirement.

### What's the best time of day to start this drive from Atlanta?

Leave Atlanta by 7:30–8:00 AM to reach Jekyll Island by late morning, hit the beach before afternoon thunderstorms, and arrive in Savannah by early evening. Leaving after 10 AM puts you on Jekyll Island during peak storm window (2–5 PM) and into Savannah during sunset traffic on US-17.
