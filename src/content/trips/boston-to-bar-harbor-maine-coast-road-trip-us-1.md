**This Boston-to-Bar Harbor road trip covers roughly 320 miles along US-1 and I-95 North, with a total driving time of about 5.5 hours point-to-point — but spread across 5 to 6 days, it's the definitive summer coastal drive in the Northeast. The verdict: this is the most rewarding warm-weather route in New England, combining seafood towns, a working waterfront city, and Acadia National Park at the finish.**

Most drivers race straight up I-95 and miss the whole point. The smarter move is to peel off the interstate early, pick up US-1 through the Maine coast towns, and let the trip breathe. In late June the weather is ideal — water temperatures are still cool, crowds haven't peaked yet, and the lupine fields along the Downeast corridor are in full bloom.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~320 miles (Boston to Bar Harbor)
- **Total drive time:** 5 hours 30 minutes (point-to-point, no stops)
- **Recommended trip length:** 5–6 days
- **Best season:** Late June through mid-September
- **Number of stops:** 6 major stops
- **Starting point:** Boston, MA
- **Ending point:** Bar Harbor, ME

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for couples and small families** who want a mix of food, scenery, and one major national park anchor
- **Good for first-time Maine visitors** — this route hits the state's greatest hits without requiring backcountry navigation
- **Not recommended for large RVs over 30 ft** on US-1 through Camden and Rockport — the village streets and parking lots are genuinely tight
- **Works well as a one-way trip** if you fly into Boston Logan and out of Bangor International Airport (BGR), 50 miles northwest of Bar Harbor

## Portsmouth, NH (1 hour from Boston via I-95 N)

Cross into New Hampshire on I-95 North and exit at Portsmouth for your first stop. This compact seaport punches well above its size: the Market Street corridor has serious restaurants, and the working tugboat harbor is worth a 20-minute walk. Fill your tank here at any of the stations along Woodbury Avenue — New Hampshire has no gas tax markup that Maine adds, so prices run slightly lower. For lunch, **Jumpin' Jay's Fish Cafe** on Congress Street is a local institution for chowder and whole-belly clams. Allow 2 hours total before pushing north.

## Kennebunkport, ME (45 minutes from Portsmouth via I-95 N to Exit 25, then ME-35)

Cross into Maine and exit I-95 at Exit 25 (Kennebunk), then follow ME-35 south into Kennebunkport. The Dock Square area is walkable and nautical without being overdone. **Mabel's Lobster Claw** on Ocean Avenue has been serving lobster rolls since 1953 — order the classic warm with butter. This is a good overnight stop if you're splitting the trip over two days from Boston. Gas up before leaving on ME-9 East toward Portland; the next convenient station cluster is in Biddeford on US-1.

## Portland, ME (45 minutes from Kennebunkport via US-1 N)

Portland is the trip's urban anchor and deserves a full evening and morning. The Old Port district on Commercial Street runs right along the working waterfront. **Eventide Oyster Co.** on Middle Street is the best single food stop on this entire route — the brown butter lobster roll has a legitimate national reputation. If you're staying overnight, the West End and Munjoy Hill neighborhoods have walkable streets and good coffee. The Portland Head Light in Cape Elizabeth (a 15-minute drive south on ME-77) is worth the detour at golden hour. Gas is widely available throughout Portland on US-1 and Forest Avenue.

## Brunswick & Freeport, ME (30 minutes from Portland via I-295 N)

These two towns sit just 10 miles apart on I-295. Freeport is the home of L.L. Bean's flagship campus — open 24 hours, year-round — and the surrounding outlet strip. It's a practical stop for any gear you forgot. Brunswick, a few miles north, has a quieter Main Street and Bowdoin College's campus, worth a 30-minute walk. If you're continuing to Camden the same day, this is your last easy fuel and grocery stop before the roads narrow. Stock up on snacks and water; the stretch up US-1 through Waldoboro can feel remote.

## Camden, ME (1 hour 15 minutes from Brunswick via US-1 N)

US-1 through Rockland and Thomaston delivers the first real Maine-coast scenery — tidal coves, lobster pounds on the roadside, and working boat yards. Camden itself sits where the mountains meet the sea, a phrase that's earned here: Camden Hills State Park rises directly above the harbor. Hike the 1.5-mile Mount Battie Trail for panoramic views over Penobscot Bay, or drive the auto road to the summit (fee required in summer). **Boynton-McKay Food Co.** on Main Street is a converted pharmacy turned breakfast-and-lunch spot that locals actually use. This is the strongest overnight candidate on the route — Camden has better lodging value than Bar Harbor and a quieter pace.

For a longer exploration of the coast beyond Camden, our [Maine Downeast Coast road trip guide from Portland to Lubec](https://mynavio.com/blog/maine-downeast-coast-road-trip-portland-to-lubec) covers the stretch east of here in full detail.

## Bar Harbor, ME (1 hour 30 minutes from Camden via US-1 N to ME-3)

From Camden, US-1 runs north through Belfast and Ellsworth. At Ellsworth, pick up ME-3 East onto Mount Desert Island — you're now on the island that holds Acadia National Park. Bar Harbor is the main gateway town, with lodging, restaurants, and gear outfitters clustered around Cottage Street and Main Street. **Thurston's Lobster Pound** in Bernard (on the quieter western side of the island, 20 minutes from Bar Harbor) is worth the drive for a full lobster dinner with a view of Bass Harbor.

For Acadia itself: the Park Loop Road is 27 miles and takes 2–3 hours with stops. The summit of Cadillac Mountain via the Cadillac Summit Road is the most-visited spot — in summer 2026, timed entry reservations are required for the summit road between 7 a.m. and 10 a.m. and again 3 p.m. to sunset. Book through Recreation.gov well in advance; these sell out weeks ahead. Fill up in Ellsworth before entering Mount Desert Island — there are no gas stations inside the park boundary, and Bar Harbor stations charge a premium.

**Seasonal note for June 21, 2026:** Acadia's peak season begins July 4th weekend. Late June means shorter lines at the Jordan Pond House (reserve a table for popovers), accessible hiking without the August heat, and the best wildflower timing on the carriage roads. The park entrance fee is $35 per vehicle; the America the Beautiful annual pass covers it.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Boston to Bar Harbor?

Point-to-point via I-95 North and ME-3, the drive is approximately 5 hours with no traffic. In summer, expect 30–60 minutes of additional time around Portland and the Ellsworth bottleneck on ME-3. We recommend budgeting 5 to 6 days if you want to stop meaningfully along the coast.

### Is US-1 in Maine slow compared to I-95?

Yes — US-1 through coastal Maine towns averages 35–45 mph with traffic lights, village speed zones, and summer tourist congestion. Plan on roughly twice the driving time versus I-95 for the same distance. The tradeoff is direct access to harbors, lobster pounds, and towns that the interstate bypasses entirely.

### Do you need a reservation to drive up Cadillac Mountain in Acadia?

Yes, during peak hours in summer 2026. Timed entry vehicle reservations are required 7–10 a.m. and 3 p.m. to sunset. Reservations are available at Recreation.gov and sell out weeks in advance. Outside those windows, no reservation is needed.

### What is the best overnight stop between Boston and Bar Harbor?

Portland is the most practical overnight stop for a two-day drive — it sits roughly halfway and has the best restaurant and hotel density on the route. Camden is the best choice for scenery and a slower pace if you're splitting the trip into three or more days.

### Are there any road closures or hazards on this route in summer?

The main summer hazard is congestion, not closures. ME-3 into Bar Harbor becomes severely congested on Friday afternoons and Sunday afternoons throughout July and August. Arriving on a Tuesday or Wednesday morning makes a measurable difference. No seasonal road closures affect this route in June.

### Where should I fill up gas before entering Acadia National Park?

Ellsworth, ME is your last reliable, competitively priced fuel stop before Mount Desert Island. There are no gas stations inside Acadia National Park, and the stations in Bar Harbor charge a significant premium in peak season. Fill up in Ellsworth on ME-3 before the causeway.
