**This San Diego to Coachella Valley Preserve road trip covers roughly 195 miles via CA-94, County Road S2, and CA-86, with about 4 hours 15 minutes of pure drive time. It's the best alternative desert loop from San Diego that skips Joshua Tree entirely and delivers something stranger and more memorable.**

Most San Diego drivers sprint up I-15 toward Joshua Tree on autopilot. This route does the opposite: it drops southeast on CA-94, climbs through the Laguna Mountains on the Southern Emigrant Trail Scenic Byway (County Road S2), then descends into the Borrego badlands before swinging north along the Salton Sea's eastern shore on CA-86. The payoff is a California that feels genuinely remote — an inland sea 227 feet below sea level, a ghost town turned outdoor art installation, and a palm oasis fed by the San Andreas Fault. September and October are ideal: daytime highs drop from brutal summer peaks into the low 90s°F at sea level and the mid-70s°F in the mountains, and the slant fall light makes the badlands glow amber.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~195 miles
- **Total drive time:** ~4 hours 15 minutes (excluding stops)
- **Best season:** Late September through November; avoid June–August (extreme heat)
- **Number of stops:** 5 major stops
- **Starting point:** San Diego, CA
- **Ending point:** Coachella Valley Preserve (Thousand Palms Oasis), CA

## Who This Route Is For

- **Great for photographers and solitude-seekers** who want a desert experience without the Joshua Tree weekend crowds
- **Good for sedans and standard SUVs** — all primary roads are paved; Bombay Beach access roads are unpaved but short and manageable in dry conditions
- **Not recommended for vehicles over 28 feet** on S2 through Anza-Borrego; steep grades and tight curves between Scissors Crossing and Ocotillo
- **Ideal as an overnight trip** with a stay near the Salton Sea, though doable as a long day if you start before 7 a.m.

## San Diego (Start)

Fuel up before leaving San Diego — gas stations become sparse quickly once you head east on CA-94. The Chevron at the intersection of CA-94 and Jamacha Road in Spring Valley is a reliable last-call option before the mountain grades. Leave downtown San Diego via I-8 East to CA-94 East, then follow CA-94 southeast toward the border corridor.

## Campo Road Corridor & Laguna Mountains (45 minutes from San Diego)

CA-94 east of Jamul is a genuine two-lane mountain road — narrow, winding, and almost entirely empty on weekday mornings. The highway climbs through oak woodland and chaparral before reaching the small community of Campo, home to the Pacific Southwest Railway Museum (open weekends, free to browse the yard). From Campo, pick up County Road S2 northward — this is the Southern Emigrant Trail Scenic Byway, the same wagon route California-bound settlers used in the 1840s. The climb to the Laguna Mountains peaks near 4,000 feet before a long, dramatic descent into the Anza-Borrego Desert.

For coffee and a breakfast burrito before the desert drop, stop at the **Manzanita Band Café** at the Manzanita Casino on CA-94 near Jacumba Hot Springs — it's the last reliable food stop for 50+ miles.

## Ocotillo Wells / Anza-Borrego Eastern Edge (1 hour from Campo)

S2 delivers you into the eastern Anza-Borrego badlands near Ocotillo Wells. This is not the main Borrego Springs visitor corridor — it's quieter, more raw, and the dirt-bike culture of the Ocotillo Wells State Vehicular Recreation Area gives it a different energy. Fill up at the **76 station in Ocotillo Wells** (on Ranger Road just off CA-78) — it's the last guaranteed fuel before the Salton Sea. From here, CA-78 East connects you to CA-86 North in about 20 minutes.

If you're doing this in late September, the desert temperatures here will be in the low 90s°F by midday. Carry at least two liters of water per person before leaving Ocotillo Wells.

## Salton Sea State Recreation Area (50 minutes from Ocotillo Wells)

CA-86 North traces the Salton Sea's western shore, but we recommend crossing to the eastern shore via CA-78 East to CA-111 South for the better views. The Salton Sea State Recreation Area's North Shore unit sits about 10 miles south of Mecca on CA-111. Pull over at the boat launch area — the combination of mineral-white shoreline, dead tilapia, and the Santa Rosa Mountains reflected in the water is genuinely unlike anything else in California. The smell is real; so is the otherworldly beauty.

Note: Salton Sea water levels and shoreline access change seasonally. As of fall 2026, the North Shore unit remains accessible, but check California State Parks (parks.ca.gov) for current conditions before committing to shoreline walks.

## Bombay Beach (20 minutes from Salton Sea SRA)

Drive south on CA-111 to Bombay Beach, a half-abandoned former resort town that artists have colonized with large-scale sculptures and murals. The **Bombay Beach Ruins** are open access — walk the crumbling streets, find the piano half-submerged in an empty lot, and check out the Ski Inn (est. 1964), the lowest-elevation bar in the Western Hemisphere at 223 feet below sea level. The Ski Inn serves cold beer and simple bar food; it's cash-preferred. Budget 45–60 minutes here.

## Coachella Valley Preserve — Thousand Palms Oasis (1 hour from Bombay Beach)

Backtrack north on CA-111 to I-10 West, then take the Thousand Palms exit north to the Coachella Valley Preserve (Thousand Palms Oasis). This Bureau of Land Management and Nature Conservancy preserve protects one of the largest native California fan palm oases in the state, fed by groundwater pushed to the surface along the San Andreas Fault. The main loop trail is 1.5 miles and takes about 45 minutes. The preserve is open sunrise to sunset; no entry fee as of 2026. This is a genuine end-point worth the drive — the contrast between the dead Salton Sea shoreline and this green, shaded oasis an hour north lands hard.

## FAQ

### How long does the San Diego to Salton Sea drive take?
The drive from downtown San Diego to the Salton Sea State Recreation Area via CA-94 and S2 is approximately 130 miles and takes about 2 hours 45 minutes without stops. The full route to Thousand Palms Oasis adds another 65 miles and roughly 90 minutes of drive time.

### Is County Road S2 safe to drive in a regular car?
Yes, for standard sedans and SUVs in good condition. S2 is paved throughout, but the grade between Scissors Crossing and Ocotillo is steep and the curves are tight. Drive at posted speeds, watch for RVs taking wide lines, and avoid this road in rain or after dark if you're unfamiliar with mountain driving.

### Is the Salton Sea smell really that bad?
It depends on wind, season, and proximity to the shoreline. In fall, with lower temperatures and drier conditions, the odor is present but manageable — more mineral than sewage. Summer is significantly worse. Staying in your car or keeping stops brief is a reasonable strategy if you're sensitive.

### Are there gas stations along S2 or between Borrego and the Salton Sea?
Fuel options are extremely limited. Fill up in San Diego or Spring Valley before CA-94, and again at the 76 station in Ocotillo Wells before heading east on CA-78. There is no reliable fuel on S2 itself, and options are sparse along the eastern Salton Sea until you reach the Mecca/Coachella area on CA-111.

### When is the best time of year to drive this route?
Late September through mid-November is optimal. Daytime highs at Salton Sea level drop from 110°F+ in summer to the low 90s°F by late September and the 80s°F by October. The Laguna Mountain section of S2 can see frost and occasional snow from December through February, which can make the descent into Borrego slick.

### Do you need a permit to visit Thousand Palms Oasis or Bombay Beach?
No permit is required for either. Thousand Palms Oasis is managed jointly by the Coachella Valley Mountains Conservancy and is free to enter during daylight hours. Bombay Beach is an unincorporated community with public streets; the art installations are on private and public land and are generally accessible on foot.
