**This 405-mile route follows US-61 (the Blues Highway) and the Great River Road south from Memphis, TN to New Orleans, LA — roughly 7–8 hours of pure driving spread across 2–3 days. It's the single best food-focused road trip in the American South, stacking world-class BBQ, Delta tamales, and Creole cooking into one continuous corridor.**

US-61 is not a fast road. It's a two-lane that cuts through cotton fields, river towns, and roadside shacks that have been feeding travelers for a century. That slowness is the point. Budget at least two full days; three is better if you want to eat everything on this list without rushing.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** 405 miles
- **Total drive time:** 7–8 hours (not counting stops)
- **Recommended trip length:** 2–3 days
- **Best season:** Spring and fall are ideal; summer (our current window) works fine but expect heat and humidity — keep water in the car and fill up AC before departure
- **Number of major stops:** 6
- **Starting point:** Memphis, TN
- **Ending point:** New Orleans, LA

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for food-focused travelers** who want to eat their way through the South rather than tick off scenic overlooks
- **Good for solo drivers and couples** — most restaurants are counter-service or casual sit-down, no reservations needed
- **Not recommended for RVs over 30 ft** on the smaller river-road segments near Vicksburg and St. Francisville, where parking is limited
- **Not a family-with-young-kids speed run** — the stops are spread out and the route rewards lingering, not rushing

## Memphis, TN — Stop 1

Start at the source. Memphis is arguably America's BBQ capital, and the debate between its two most famous joints is worth having in person. Charlie Vergos' Rendezvous (52 S. Second St.) has been serving dry-rubbed ribs in a basement since 1948 — get the half-slab. For pulled pork with a different character, Central BBQ on Central Avenue runs a consistent operation with excellent smoked wings. Fill your gas tank before leaving Memphis; the stretch of US-61 through the Delta has stations but they thin out between towns.

Summer note: Memphis in August sits around 90–95°F with high humidity. Early morning departures (before 8 a.m.) keep you ahead of the worst heat on the road.

## Clarksdale, MS — Stop 2 (about 1 hr 20 min from Memphis)

Clarksdale is the cultural heart of the Mississippi Delta and home to one of the most specific regional foods in America: the Delta tamale. Rust's Tamales and Doe's Eat Place both operate here, but the essential stop is Doe's Eat Place (502 Nelson St.) — a converted grocery store that has been serving hot tamales and massive steaks since 1941. Order a dozen tamales to go and eat them in the parking lot. They are spicier and more tightly wrapped than any Tex-Mex version you've had.

Gas up in Clarksdale if you didn't top off in Memphis. The next reliable stretch of stations is Greenville, about 75 miles south.

## Greenville, MS — Stop 3 (about 1 hr 15 min from Clarksdale)

Greenville is another Delta tamale hub and also the home of Doe's Eat Place's original location — the Greenville outpost at 502 Nelson St. is the original, predating the Clarksdale franchise. If you skipped Clarksdale, do not skip this stop. The Delta tamale tradition here traces back to Mexican laborers who worked the Delta farms in the early 20th century; the local adaptation uses cornmeal instead of masa and is simmered rather than steamed.

For a quick fuel-up of the human variety, Taqueria San Marcos on US-82 near downtown serves honest tacos at lunch and is popular with locals.

## Natchez, MS — Stop 4 (about 2 hrs from Greenville)

Natchez sits on a bluff above the Mississippi River and is the official southern terminus of the Natchez Trace Parkway — if you're interested in a parallel scenic option, our [Natchez Trace Parkway road trip guide](https://mynavio.com/blog/natchez-trace-parkway-road-trip-tupelo-to-nashville) covers the full northern run to Nashville. For this food route, stay on US-61 and stop at Magnolia Grill (49 Silver St.) in the Natchez-Under-the-Hill district for fried catfish and hush puppies. It's a straightforward Southern fish house with a river view and portions that require a second wind before Baton Rouge.

Fill up on gas in Natchez. The stretch south on US-61 through St. Francisville, LA is manageable but don't assume every station is open on a Sunday.

## Baton Rouge, LA — Stop 5 (about 1 hr 30 min from Natchez)

Once you cross into Louisiana, the food shifts register. Baton Rouge is the practical fuel-and-food stop before the final push to New Orleans. Parrain's Seafood Restaurant (3225 Perkins Rd.) is the local standard for boiled crawfish and fried shrimp — in August, crawfish season is winding down so expect the menu to lean toward shrimp and oysters. If you want something faster, Raising Cane's Chicken Fingers was founded in Baton Rouge in 1996 and the original location on Highland Road near LSU is worth the detour for context alone.

Top off your gas tank in Baton Rouge. I-10 west into New Orleans is straightforward but you'll want a full tank to avoid stopping in traffic near the city.

## New Orleans, LA — Stop 6 (about 1 hr 20 min from Baton Rouge)

End the route in the French Quarter or the Bywater neighborhood. For a proper final meal, Dooky Chase's Restaurant (2301 Orleans Ave.) is the standard-bearer for Creole cooking — fried chicken, red beans, and gumbo in a dining room that has hosted everyone from Ray Charles to Barack Obama. Reservations are recommended for dinner. For a more casual landing, grab a roast beef po'boy at Parkway Bakery & Tavern (538 Hagan Ave.) in Mid-City, a no-frills institution that has been running since 1911.

Summer in New Orleans means afternoon thunderstorms, typically between 2–5 p.m. Plan your arrival for late morning or after 6 p.m. to avoid driving into downtown during peak storm windows.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Memphis to New Orleans on US-61?

The drive on US-61 and connecting roads is approximately 405 miles and takes 7–8 hours of pure driving time. Most travelers split it into two days, stopping overnight in Clarksdale or Natchez. Three days is the right pace if you plan to eat at every stop on this list.

### Is US-61 safe to drive in summer?

US-61 through the Mississippi Delta is a standard two-lane highway with no significant safety hazards in summer. The main considerations are heat — temperatures regularly exceed 95°F in August — and isolated stretches with limited cell service between Greenville and Natchez. Keep water in the car and maintain a full tank of gas before entering long rural segments.

### What is a Delta tamale and where can I get one on this route?

A Delta tamale is a regional variant found only in the Mississippi Delta, made with a cornmeal dough (not traditional masa) and simmered in a spiced broth rather than steamed. The tradition dates to early 20th-century Mexican agricultural workers. Doe's Eat Place in Clarksdale and Greenville are the most reliable stops on this route; both serve them by the dozen.

### Do you need reservations at Dooky Chase's in New Orleans?

Reservations are strongly recommended for dinner at Dooky Chase's Restaurant, especially on weekends. The restaurant is closed Sundays and Mondays. Walk-ins are sometimes accommodated at lunch on weekdays, but call ahead at (504) 821-0600 to confirm current hours and availability.

### Are there any road closures or detours on US-61 in summer 2026?

As of August 2026, there are no major announced closures on US-61 through Mississippi. However, the Louisiana section near Baton Rouge on I-10 and I-110 frequently has lane reductions due to ongoing bridge and interchange work — check the Louisiana DOTD traffic map at 511la.org before departure. Summer flooding near the river can also temporarily affect low-lying county roads off the main highway.

### What is the best overnight stop between Memphis and New Orleans?

Natchez, MS is the strongest overnight option at roughly the midpoint of the route. It has a compact walkable historic district, multiple mid-range hotels, and easy access back to US-61. Clarksdale works for travelers who want to catch live blues music in the evening — the Ground Zero Blues Club (0 Blues Alley) runs shows most nights.
