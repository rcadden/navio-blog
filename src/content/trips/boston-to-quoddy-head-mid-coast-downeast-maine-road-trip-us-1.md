**This Boston-to-Quoddy Head road trip covers roughly 360 miles along US-1 through mid-coast and Downeast Maine, with 7–8 hours of pure driving best split across two or three days. It's the most rewarding way to reach the easternmost point in the contiguous United States without retracing the Acadia crowds.** The route trades the peak-summer traffic of Mount Desert Island for working fishing harbors, wild blueberry barrens, and a lighthouse perched at the edge of the Atlantic. August is the sweet spot: warm enough for the coast, and the wild blueberries along ME-9 and US-1 east of Machias are at peak harvest.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~360 miles (Boston to Quoddy Head State Park)
- **Total drive time:** 7–8 hours (driving only; plan 2–3 days)
- **Best season:** Mid-July through early September
- **Number of stops:** 7
- **Starting point:** Boston, MA
- **Ending point:** Quoddy Head State Park, Lubec, ME

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for couples or solo drivers** who want a quieter alternative to the Acadia/Bar Harbor corridor in peak summer
- **Good for seafood obsessives** — every major stop offers lobster rolls, fish chowder, or raw oysters in working-harbor settings
- **Manageable for most passenger vehicles and smaller campervans** (under 26 ft); the approach to Lubec on US-189 is narrow in spots but fully paved
- **Not recommended as a single-day drive** — the mileage is doable in one push, but you'll miss everything that makes the route worth taking

## Portland, ME (Stop 1)

Leave Boston on I-95 North and cross into Maine at Kittery. Portland is 1 hour 50 minutes from Boston (109 miles). This is your fuel-and-food stop before the route thins out. Fill the tank here — gas stations become sparse east of Ellsworth. For lunch, join the line at **Eventide Oyster Co.** on Middle Street for brown butter lobster rolls and local oysters. If you arrive early, walk the Eastern Promenade for a view of Casco Bay before pushing north. Portland's Old Port also has multiple grocery options if you're stocking a cooler for the drive.

## Brunswick & Harpswell Neck (Stop 2)

**30 minutes north of Portland on US-1 (28 miles).** Brunswick is where US-1 starts to feel genuinely coastal rather than suburban. Detour south on ME-123 and ME-24 to reach Harpswell Neck — a 20-minute side trip that puts you on a narrow peninsula with lobster pounds directly on the water. **Morse's Cribstone Bridge**, connecting Bailey Island, is one of the few open-crib granite bridges still in use in the country. Get a boiled lobster at Cook's Lobster & Ale House on Bailey Island before returning to US-1.

## Rockland, ME (Stop 3)

**1 hour 10 minutes from Brunswick on US-1 (58 miles).** Rockland is a working fishing port with a serious arts scene built around the Farnsworth Art Museum (Wyeth collection). The **Rockland Breakwater Lighthouse** is a 4.3-mile round-trip walk on a granite jetty — flat, exposed, and worth every step on a clear August day. For dinner, **Primo** on Main Street is one of the best farm-to-table restaurants in New England; reservations are essential in August. This is a logical overnight stop — the town has solid motel and inn options along US-1.

## Belfast, ME (Stop 4)

**45 minutes north of Rockland on US-1 (38 miles).** Belfast is compact, walkable, and underrated. The harbor front along the Passagassawakeag River has a mix of independent coffee shops and chandleries that feel authentic rather than curated. Grab coffee at **Rollie's Bar & Grill** or the nearby Penobscot Bay Provisioners. Belfast is also the last reliable spot for a sit-down meal before Machias — stock up on snacks and top off the tank at the Irving station on US-1 before heading east.

## Ellsworth, ME (Stop 5)

**55 minutes from Belfast on US-1 (48 miles).** Ellsworth is the commercial hub for all of Downeast Maine — the last Walmart, the last Target, and the last reliable fast-food corridor before the route gets genuinely remote. If you need anything (camping supplies, a phone charger, extra water), get it here. Ellsworth sits at the junction of US-1 and ME-3 (the road to Bar Harbor); ignore the Bar Harbor turnoff and stay on US-1 East. Note: if you're continuing our [Downeast Coast road trip from Portland to Lubec](https://mynavio.com/blog/maine-downeast-coast-road-trip-portland-to-lubec) as a longer loop, Ellsworth is where those two routes converge.

## Machias, ME (Stop 6)

**1 hour 20 minutes from Ellsworth on US-1 (68 miles).** The highway east of Ellsworth is two lanes, heavily forested, and largely free of traffic — this is the Maine most people never see. Machias sits on the Machias River and hosts the **Wild Blueberry Festival** every third weekend of August (August 21–23, 2026 — check dates before you go, as this coincides with your travel window and brings significant local traffic). Stop at **Helen's Restaurant** on Main Street, a Machias institution since 1950, for blueberry pie that is as good as the regional reputation suggests. Fill up at the Citgo on Court Street — it's the last reliable gas before Lubec (27 miles).

## Quoddy Head State Park (Stop 7)

**45 minutes from Machias on US-1 and US-189 (27 miles).** US-189 branches off US-1 at Whiting and leads directly into Lubec, the easternmost town in the contiguous United States. West Quoddy Head Lighthouse, with its distinctive red-and-white candy-cane stripes, marks the easternmost point. The park has 4.5 miles of coastal trails along the Bold Coast cliffs, and on clear mornings you can see New Brunswick, Canada, across the Quoddy Narrows. Fog is common in August — arrive early for the best visibility. Lubec has limited lodging; book well ahead or plan to stay in Machias and day-trip the final 27 miles.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Boston to Quoddy Head State Park?
The direct drive is approximately 360 miles and takes 7–8 hours without stops. Most drivers split it into two days, overnighting in Rockland or Belfast, to make the coastal stops worthwhile.

### Is US-1 in Downeast Maine safe to drive in summer?
Yes. US-1 between Ellsworth and Machias is a well-maintained two-lane highway with no technical hazards. Traffic is light except during the Wild Blueberry Festival weekend in Machias (mid-to-late August). Watch for moose at dawn and dusk, especially between Ellsworth and Machias.

### Do you need a reservation to enter Quoddy Head State Park?
As of 2026, Maine State Parks do not require advance reservations for day-use entry at Quoddy Head State Park. A standard Maine day-use fee applies. No camping is available within the park itself.

### Where is the last gas station before Lubec, Maine?
The last reliable gas stations are in Machias on Court Street (US-1), approximately 27 miles west of Lubec. There is a small general store in Whiting, but supply is inconsistent — fuel up in Machias.

### What is the best time of year to drive from Boston to Quoddy Head?
Mid-July through early September offers the most reliable weather and the longest daylight hours. August also aligns with wild blueberry season in Washington County. Avoid this route in November through April unless you have winter-rated tires — fog, ice, and limited services make Downeast Maine genuinely remote in the off-season.

### Can you combine this route with a visit to Acadia National Park?
Yes. From Ellsworth (Stop 5), ME-3 leads to Mount Desert Island and Acadia in about 30 minutes. Adding Acadia turns this into a 4–5 day trip.
