**This 210-mile fall foliage road trip runs south-to-north on Vermont Route 100 (VT-100) before cutting east on US-302 into North Conway, New Hampshire. Total drive time is approximately 5.5 hours of seat time, or a full two-day trip with stops. Verdict: the most color-dense foliage corridor in the Northeast, with far less traffic than I-89 or I-91.**

Peak foliage in Vermont and northern New Hampshire typically lands between late September and mid-October — the first two weeks of October are historically the sweet spot. Book lodging three to four months out; this region sells out fast. If you're planning now in June for a September or October trip, that's the right call.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** 210 miles
- **Total drive time:** 5.5 hours (seat time only)
- **Best season:** Late September–mid-October for peak foliage
- **Number of stops:** 6
- **Starting point:** Stowe, Vermont
- **Ending point:** North Conway, New Hampshire

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for couples or solo drivers** who want a focused, high-payoff foliage drive without a multi-state loop
- **Good for drivers with smaller vehicles** — VT-100 through the Mad River Valley has tight village roads; wide trucks and large fifth-wheels will find it awkward
- **Not recommended for RVs over 30 feet** on the Warren Village section of VT-100, which includes a narrow covered bridge approach
- **Works well as a two-day trip** with an overnight in Montpelier or St. Johnsbury

## Stop 1: Stowe, Vermont (Starting Point)

Stowe sits at the base of Mount Mansfield, Vermont's highest peak, and the foliage here starts turning earlier than most of the state — typically the last week of September. Before leaving, fill up at the Cumberland Farms on Mountain Road (VT-108) in Stowe; the next reliable fuel stop southbound on VT-100 is about 30 miles away in Waitsfield. Breakfast at the Stowe Diner on Main Street (open from 6 a.m.) sets you up right — the place has been feeding leaf-peepers since the 1940s.

From Stowe, head south on VT-100. The road follows the Waterbury River valley, with sugar maples and birches pressing close on both sides. This stretch is as good as foliage driving gets.

## Stop 2: Mad River Valley — Waitsfield (about 30 miles / 45 minutes from Stowe)

Waitsfield is the commercial heart of the Mad River Valley and a logical fuel and stretch stop. The Valley has a strong local food scene; grab a sandwich at the Mad River Food Hub on VT-100 if you're passing through around lunch. The covered bridges here — including the Warren Bridge just south of town — are worth a five-minute detour. Photographers should note morning light is best on the western hillsides.

## Stop 3: Sugarbush / Warren Village (about 8 miles / 15 minutes from Waitsfield)

Warren Village is one of the more photogenic stops on VT-100: a white church, a general store, and a single-lane covered bridge that frames the foliage behind it. The Warren Store has been operating since 1839 and sells good coffee and deli food. Note: the covered bridge approach from VT-100 is narrow and has a low clearance — passenger vehicles only. This is also the turnoff for Sugarbush Resort if you want an elevated viewpoint via the access road.

## Stop 4: Montpelier, Vermont (about 35 miles / 50 minutes from Warren)

Montpelier is the smallest state capital in the U.S. and a practical overnight base. It's also a fuel stop before the longer rural stretch north toward St. Johnsbury. The Capitol building's gold dome against peak foliage is one of the most-photographed images in Vermont. For dinner, the Sarducci's Restaurant on Main Street has been a local staple for decades. From Montpelier, you'll pick up US-2 East briefly before cutting back north toward St. Johnsbury on US-2 East — a well-maintained divided highway with strong fall color along the Winooski River corridor.

## Stop 5: St. Johnsbury, Vermont (about 40 miles / 55 minutes from Montpelier)

St. Johnsbury is an underrated stop. The Fairbanks Museum and Planetarium (founded 1889) is worth an hour, and the St. Johnsbury Athenaeum — a National Historic Landmark art gallery — is free and genuinely impressive. Fuel up here at one of the Irving stations on Railroad Street before heading east on US-2 into New Hampshire, where gas stations thin out until you reach Littleton, NH. The US-2 corridor through the White Mountains is one of the best foliage drives in New Hampshire, with the Presidential Range visible to the south.

For a scenic variation, take US-302 south from Bethlehem, NH through Crawford Notch State Park — this adds about 25 miles but delivers dramatic notch scenery with hardwood color on the valley walls. Crawford Notch Road (US-302) is fully paved and appropriate for standard passenger vehicles; the notch itself is narrow but not technically challenging.

## Stop 6: North Conway, New Hampshire (about 65 miles / 1 hour 20 minutes from St. Johnsbury via US-2 and US-302)

North Conway is the natural endpoint — a full-service town with lodging, restaurants, and outlet shopping if you need it. The Conway Scenic Railroad runs foliage excursions in October that are worth booking in advance. Elevation here is lower than the Vermont stops, so foliage peaks slightly later, typically the second week of October. This timing gap is actually useful: you can catch early color in Stowe and later color in North Conway on the same trip.

## Seasonal Hazards and Road Conditions

For a late September or October trip, watch for:
- **Frost and black ice** on VT-100 above 1,500 feet elevation after mid-October, especially overnight
- **Leaf-wet pavement** — fallen leaves on curves can be as slick as ice
- **Weekend traffic congestion** on VT-100 between Waitsfield and Warren during peak weekends (typically the first two weekends of October); plan to drive that section before 9 a.m. or after 4 p.m.
- **Crawford Notch (US-302)** can see early snow above 2,000 feet after October 15 — check New Hampshire DOT road conditions before entering

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive VT-100 from Stowe to the New Hampshire border?

The drive from Stowe south on VT-100 to Montpelier is roughly 65 miles and takes about 1 hour 30 minutes without stops. Adding the Mad River Valley and Warren stops realistically pushes that to 3 hours. Budget a full day for the Vermont portion alone if you want to do it properly.

### When is peak fall foliage in Vermont and New Hampshire?

Historically, peak color in northern Vermont (Stowe, Mad River Valley) runs September 25–October 8. Central Vermont peaks October 1–12. Northern New Hampshire near North Conway peaks October 5–15. These windows shift by a few days year to year depending on summer rainfall and early frost dates.

### Is VT-100 safe to drive in a regular car?

Yes. VT-100 is a well-maintained state highway suitable for all standard passenger vehicles. The Warren covered bridge approach is the only section requiring extra caution — it's one lane and has a 9-foot height clearance. Drivers of large trucks or RVs should use the bypass.

### Do you need a reservation to drive through Crawford Notch State Park?

No permit or reservation is required to drive US-302 through Crawford Notch State Park. Day-use parking areas inside the park charge a small fee during peak season. The road is open year-round but can close temporarily after heavy snowfall — check nhstateparks.org before your visit in late October.

### Where should I stay overnight on this route?

Montpelier and St. Johnsbury are the most practical overnight stops if you're splitting the trip over two days. Stowe has the widest range of lodging but books out earliest. North Conway has the most chain hotel options at the end of the route. Avoid arriving without a reservation during the first two weekends of October.

### Is this route good for a long weekend trip?

Yes. Two nights and three days is enough to drive the full 210 miles with meaningful time at each stop. A single overnight is possible but rushed. If you only have one day, the Stowe-to-Waitsfield-to-Warren section of VT-100 (about 40 miles) delivers the highest concentration of foliage scenery per mile.
