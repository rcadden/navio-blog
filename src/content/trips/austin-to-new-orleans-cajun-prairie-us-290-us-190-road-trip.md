**This Austin-to-New Orleans road trip covers approximately 530 miles and 8.5–9.5 hours of drive time, routing through the Cajun Prairie on US-290 East and US-190 East rather than the interstate slab. The verdict: it's the most flavorful version of this drive, trading highway monotony for roadside boudin stops, longleaf pine corridors, and a genuine cross-section of Louisiana culture most visitors never see.**

Most drivers default to I-10 for this run, or the Gulf Coast swing through Galveston and Lafayette (which we've already covered in our [Austin to New Orleans Gulf Coast guide](https://mynavio.com/blog/austin-to-new-orleans-gulf-coast-road-trip-galveston-lafayette)). This route takes the middle path — east through the Texas Hill Country fringe, north of the Atchafalaya Basin, and through the Cajun Prairie towns of Opelousas and Eunice before dropping south to Baton Rouge and finally New Orleans. September is ideal: summer heat breaks slightly, the Acadiana Festival calendar kicks into gear, and the pine forests of East Texas show early hints of color change.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~530 miles
- **Total drive time:** 8.5–9.5 hours (driving only; add 3–4 hours for stops)
- **Best season:** Fall (September–November); avoid peak summer heat
- **Number of stops:** 7 stops between Austin and New Orleans
- **Starting point:** Austin, TX
- **Ending point:** New Orleans, LA

## Who This Route Is For

- **Food-focused drivers** who want to eat their way through Texas smokehouse and Louisiana boudin culture in a single trip
- **History and music travelers** interested in Cajun Prairie heritage, zydeco roots, and small-town Louisiana that most road trippers skip
- **Passenger cars and standard SUVs** — US-190 through the Cajun Prairie is two-lane in stretches but fully paved and well-maintained
- **Not recommended for large RVs over 35 feet** — downtown Eunice and Opelousas have limited RV parking; consider the I-10 corridor instead

## Austin, TX — Starting Point

Fill your tank in Austin before leaving; gas prices in the city are generally competitive, and the next reliable cluster of stations is Bastrop, about 30 miles east. If you're leaving early, grab coffee and a breakfast taco at Juan in a Million on E. Cesar Chavez — the Don Juan taco is a legitimate send-off meal.

## Bastrop, TX (30 minutes from Austin via TX-71 East)

Bastrop sits along the Colorado River and marks the western edge of the Lost Pines region, an isolated stand of loblolly pines that feels out of place this far west of the Piney Woods. Bastrop State Park reopened its full trail network after recovering from a major wildfire, and the fall light through the pines in September is genuinely striking. Keep the stop to 45–60 minutes — walk the Park Road 1C loop or grab a kolache at Maxine's Cafe & Bakery on Main Street, a Bastrop institution since 1989. **Seasonal note:** East Texas and the Lost Pines area can see lingering tropical moisture in early September; check the forecast before the drive as TX-71 can flood near the Colorado River bottoms.

## Brenham, TX (1 hour 10 minutes from Bastrop via TX-21 East to US-290 East)

Brenham is Blue Bell country, and the Blue Bell Creameries (US-290, Brenham) offers free ice cream samples on their self-guided tour — a reliable crowd-pleaser and a good leg stretch. The town square has a solid lunch option in Must Be Heaven sandwich shop on Market Street. Top off your gas tank here on US-290; the stretch ahead through the Sam Houston National Forest has limited services.

## Huntsville, TX (45 minutes from Brenham via US-290 East to I-45 North briefly, then TX-19)

Huntsville is worth a quick stop for its genuinely unusual character — home to Sam Houston State University, the Texas Department of Criminal Justice headquarters, and a 67-foot statue of Sam Houston visible from I-45. The Wynne Home Arts Center hosts rotating exhibits and the fall schedule typically includes regional art shows. For lunch or an early dinner, New Zion Missionary Baptist Church BBQ on Montgomery Road is a James Beard America's Classic — brisket and ribs served out of a church fellowship hall on weekdays. Call ahead to confirm hours.

## Livingston, TX (40 minutes from Huntsville via US-190 East)

Livingston is your last reliable fuel stop before crossing into Louisiana's less-populated pine belt. Fill up here on US-190. The town sits on the edge of Lake Livingston, the second-largest lake in Texas, and the Alabama-Coushatta Tribe of Texas operates a cultural center and campground just east of town on US-190 — a worthwhile 20-minute stop if you have the bandwidth. From Livingston, US-190 heads due east through the Big Thicket region and into Louisiana; this is one of the more underappreciated drives in the South.

## Opelousas, LA (2 hours from Livingston via US-190 East)

Opelousas is the third-oldest city in Louisiana and the self-proclaimed Zydeco Capital of the World — a claim backed by a real live music infrastructure. The Opelousas Museum and Interpretive Center on North Market Street covers Creole and Acadian history with more depth than most travelers expect. For food, Palace Café on Court Street is a downtown anchor serving Cajun standards: stuffed pork chops, red beans and rice, and a bread pudding worth the detour. September often coincides with the tail end of summer festival season; check the St. Landry Parish tourism calendar for any weekend events during your visit.

## Eunice, LA (25 minutes from Opelousas via US-190 West to LA-13 South — or stay on US-190)

Eunice is the heart of the Cajun Prairie music tradition. The Liberty Theater on Second Street hosts the Rendez-Vous des Cajuns live radio broadcast every Saturday evening — a French-language variety show that has run since 1987. If your trip falls on a Saturday, plan your schedule around it. The Cajun Music Hall of Fame and Museum is directly across the street. For boudin, Pick-A-Dilly on Maple Avenue is a local favorite — the cracklins alone justify the stop. **This is the route's cultural centerpiece; don't rush through it.**

## Baton Rouge, LA (1 hour 30 minutes from Eunice via I-49 South to I-10 East)

Baton Rouge gives you a city recharge before the final push to New Orleans. The Louisiana State Capitol building — the tallest state capitol in the US at 450 feet — is worth a 20-minute walk around the grounds. For dinner, Parrain's Seafood Restaurant on O'Neal Lane serves serious Gulf seafood without the French Quarter price tag. I-10 East from Baton Rouge to New Orleans crosses the Atchafalaya Basin on the Whiskey Bay Pilot Channel Bridge, an 18-mile elevated causeway over the largest river swamp in North America — a fittingly dramatic entry into the final leg.

## New Orleans, LA (1 hour 20 minutes from Baton Rouge via I-10 East)

You arrive in New Orleans having eaten through two states and crossed terrain most road trippers miss entirely. Park near the Marigny or Bywater neighborhoods for easier street parking than the French Quarter, and walk in from there.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Austin to New Orleans via US-190?

The US-190 route through the Cajun Prairie runs approximately 530 miles and takes 8.5 to 9.5 hours of pure drive time. With stops in Bastrop, Brenham, Huntsville, Eunice, and Baton Rouge, budget a full two-day itinerary or a single very long day.

### Is US-190 a safe road to drive through Louisiana?

US-190 through St. Landry and Pointe Coupee parishes is a standard two-lane US highway — well-maintained but with at-grade intersections and occasional farm equipment in rural stretches. Drive the speed limit, especially through small towns where local law enforcement is active. Night driving on this stretch is not recommended for unfamiliar drivers.

### What is the best time of year to drive through the Cajun Prairie?

Fall (September through November) is the most practical window: temperatures drop from summer highs into the 70s–80s°F, festival season is active, and hurricane risk diminishes after mid-October. Avoid late August through early September if tropical systems are active in the Gulf.

### Do you need a reservation for the Liberty Theater in Eunice?

The Rendez-Vous des Cajuns show at the Liberty Theater is free admission and walk-in only — no reservations required. Doors typically open at 5:30 PM with the show starting at 6:00 PM on Saturdays. Seating is first-come, first-served, so arrive early during fall festival weekends.

### Where should you stop for gas on the Austin to New Orleans US-190 route?

Fill up in Austin before departure, then again in Brenham (US-290) and Livingston (US-190) before crossing into Louisiana. Between Livingston and Opelousas, gas stations exist in Jasper and Newton, TX, but plan for Livingston as your primary fill-up point before the Louisiana state line.

### Is this route suitable for the Memphis to New Orleans Blues Highway comparison?

They're distinct experiences. The US-61 Blues Highway from Memphis to New Orleans, covered in our [Memphis to New Orleans BBQ and Creole guide](https://mynavio.com/blog/memphis-to-new-orleans-bbq-creole-food-road-trip-us-61), focuses on Delta music and history. This Cajun Prairie route is Acadian and Creole in character — zydeco over blues, boudin over tamales. Both are worth doing; they cover different cultural geography entirely.
