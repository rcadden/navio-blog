**This 520-mile route runs from Emporia, Kansas to Valentine, Nebraska in roughly 9–11 hours of total driving time, best spread across three days. It traces the Flint Hills Scenic Byway (US-177), crosses the central Kansas plains on US-81 and US-136, and pushes north into the Nebraska Sandhills on US-83 — making it the most geologically varied Great Plains drive you can do in a long weekend.** The Flint Hills are the largest remaining tallgrass prairie in North America, and the Sandhills are the largest sand dune formation in the Western Hemisphere. Neither gets the credit they deserve. This route fixes that.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~520 miles
- **Total drive time:** 9–11 hours (3-day itinerary recommended)
- **Best season:** Late May through mid-September; peak wildflower bloom late May–June
- **Number of stops:** 8 major stops
- **Starting point:** Emporia, KS
- **Ending point:** Valentine, NE

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for drivers who want genuine solitude** — US-83 north of Stapleton, NE sees fewer than 500 vehicles per day in most stretches
- **Good for car campers and tent campers** — Tallgrass Prairie, Harlan County Lake, and Valentine's Smith Falls State Park all have established campgrounds
- **Not recommended for large RVs over 35 ft** on the section between Ord and Valentine, where road shoulders narrow on US-183/US-83
- **Strong fit for photographers and birders** — June is peak nesting season for upland sandpipers, dickcissels, and greater prairie-chickens in the Flint Hills

## Stop 1: Emporia, KS — Starting Point

Emporia sits at the intersection of I-35 and US-50, making it an easy launch point from Kansas City (1.5 hours west) or Wichita (1 hour east). Fill up on gas here before heading north — stations thin out quickly once you're on the Flint Hills Scenic Byway. For breakfast, Radius Brewing Company on Commercial Street opens at 11 a.m., but the downtown Emporia Diner on 6th Avenue handles early risers with solid biscuits and gravy.

## Stop 2: Tallgrass Prairie National Preserve (45 min from Emporia)

Head north on the Flint Hills Scenic Byway (US-177) to the Tallgrass Prairie National Preserve near Strong City. The preserve protects 10,894 acres of unplowed tallgrass — big bluestem and indiangrass reach shoulder height by late June. The 3-mile Southwind Nature Trail is the best short walk; the Bottomland Trail adds creek crossings that are manageable in summer but can run high after heavy June storms, so check trail conditions at the visitor center. No entrance fee. The historic Spring Hill Ranch house is worth the 20-minute ranger-led tour.

## Stop 3: Cottonwood Falls, KS (15 min from the Preserve)

Cottonwood Falls is a genuine working cattle town with one of the best county courthouses in the country — the 1873 Chase County Courthouse (limestone Romanesque, still in active use) sits on a hill above the Cottonwood River. The Grand Central Hotel serves lunch and dinner; the green chile burger is the move. This is also your last reliable fuel stop before pushing toward Salina on US-56 west, so top off.

## Stop 4: Salina, KS (1 hr 45 min from Cottonwood Falls via US-56 W and US-81 N)

Salina is your overnight option if you're doing this in three days. It's a practical fuel-and-food hub — nothing more. Cozy Inn on South Santa Fe Avenue has been serving sliders since 1922 and is worth the stop for the novelty alone. From Salina, pick up US-81 north toward Concordia.

## Stop 5: Concordia, KS (1 hr from Salina via US-81 N)

Concordia's Brown Grand Theatre — a 1907 opera house still running live performances — is the kind of place that rewards a slow stop. The town also marks the start of the transition from cultivated wheat fields to rougher, emptier terrain. Head east briefly on US-36 to reach the Kansas-Nebraska border, then pick up US-136 east into Nebraska. Seasonal note: June thunderstorms across north-central Kansas can be severe; the Storm Prediction Center often has elevated outlooks for this corridor. Check the National Weather Service Dodge City forecast before this leg.

## Stop 6: Red Cloud, NE (1 hr 15 min from Concordia via US-136 E)

Red Cloud was Willa Cather's hometown and the setting for *O Pioneers!* and *My Ántonia*. The Willa Cather Foundation runs tours of her childhood home and several preserved sites in town. It's a small stop — plan 90 minutes — but it provides the only real cultural anchor on this stretch of the route. Gas up here; the next reliable station north is Kearney.

## Stop 7: Kearney, NE (1 hr 30 min from Red Cloud via US-281 N and I-80 W)

Kearney sits on I-80 and is the largest city on this route north of Salina. The Archway Monument spanning the interstate is a legitimate roadside attraction. More practically, Kearney is where you stock water, snacks, and a full tank before turning north on US-183 toward the Sandhills. The stretch from Kearney to Ord transitions from irrigated cornfields to the first rolling sand ridges — the landscape shifts visibly within 30 miles.

## Stop 8: Ord, NE (1 hr 15 min from Kearney via US-183 N)

Ord is the last town of any size before Valentine. The Loup River runs through town and offers wade fishing for channel catfish in summer. Fill up gas here — the next 120 miles on US-183 north to US-83, then northwest to Valentine, passes through ranching country with long gaps between pumps. The Sandhills proper begin just north of town: grass-covered dunes, shallow lakes, and a sky that feels outsized.

## Stop 9: Valentine, NE — End Point (2 hrs from Ord via US-183 N and US-83 NW)

Valentine anchors the heart of the Nebraska Sandhills and sits adjacent to the Niobrara National Scenic River. Tubing the Niobrara on a June afternoon — the water runs cold and clear off the sandhills aquifer — is one of the better summer experiences in the central US. Outfitters like Niobrara Natures operate tube rentals from Memorial Day through Labor Day. Smith Falls State Park, 12 miles east of Valentine on NE-12, has Nebraska's tallest waterfall (63 ft) and a riverside campground. For dinner, the Bunkhouse Bar & Grill on US-20 handles post-float hunger reliably.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Emporia, KS to Valentine, NE?

The total drive is approximately 520 miles and 9–11 hours of moving time depending on your exact routing. We recommend three days minimum to do the stops justice. Driving it straight through in one day is possible but skips most of what makes the route worthwhile.

### Is US-83 through the Nebraska Sandhills dangerous?

US-83 is a two-lane highway with long sight lines and light traffic — it's not technically dangerous, but deer and cattle crossings are common, especially at dawn and dusk. Drive at posted speeds and give yourself extra stopping distance. Cell coverage is limited north of Stapleton; download offline maps before you leave Kearney.

### When is the best time to drive the Flint Hills Scenic Byway (US-177)?

Late May through mid-June gives you the tallest grass and peak wildflowers. Ranchers conduct prescribed burns in April, which can leave the landscape blackened early in the season but dramatically green by late May. Avoid late October through March if you want reliable road conditions.

### Do you need a permit to visit Tallgrass Prairie National Preserve?

No permit is required. The preserve has no entrance fee, and the trails are open daily from sunrise to sunset. The visitor center and ranch house tours operate on seasonal hours — confirm at nps.gov/tapr before visiting.

### Are there EV charging stations on this route?

EV infrastructure is sparse outside Emporia, Salina, and Kearney. There are no Level 2 or DC Fast Chargers between Concordia, KS and Kearney, NE, and none between Kearney and Valentine. This route is not recommended for EVs with a range under 250 miles without significant advance planning.

### What summer weather hazards should drivers expect?

Severe thunderstorms are the primary hazard from June through August across Kansas and south-central Nebraska. Hail, high winds, and brief tornadoes are possible. Check the Storm Prediction Center's convective outlooks each morning. Flash flooding can affect low-water crossings on gravel roads near the Niobrara River after heavy rain.
