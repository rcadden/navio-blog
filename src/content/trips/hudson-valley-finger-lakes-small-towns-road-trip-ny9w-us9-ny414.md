**This 310-mile route runs from Nyack, NY north through the Hudson Valley then west to the Finger Lakes, covering roughly 6.5 hours of driving time over three to four days. It connects six genuinely distinct small towns on NY-9W, US-9, NY-28, and NY-414 — and early September delivers warm days, thin crowds, and the first blush of fall color without the peak-foliage traffic that arrives in October.**

New York State's interior doesn't get the same road-trip attention as its coastlines, but this corridor punches above its weight. Victorian storefronts, working farm stands, gorge trails, and serious wine pours are stacked within a half-day's drive of each other. We've driven this loop in both directions; northbound from Nyack is the cleaner flow.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** 310 miles
- **Total drive time:** ~6 hours 30 minutes (point-to-point, not including stops)
- **Best season:** Early September through mid-October
- **Number of stops:** 6 towns plus start point
- **Starting point:** Nyack, NY
- **Ending point:** Watkins Glen, NY

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for couples or small groups** who want walkable town squares, independent restaurants, and tasting rooms over theme parks or major cities.
- **Good for drivers with standard passenger cars or SUVs** — all roads are paved state highways; no clearance issues.
- **Not recommended for RVs over 35 ft** on NY-9W through Cold Spring, where parking is tight and the road narrows near the riverfront.
- **A strong choice for foliage chasers** arriving in early September who want color without the October gridlock on I-87.

## Nyack, NY — Start

Nyack sits on the west bank of the Hudson River at the foot of the Mario M. Cuomo Bridge (formerly Tappan Zee), about 25 miles north of Midtown Manhattan via I-87. Park on Main Street and walk: the downtown strip holds independent bookshops, a weekend farmers market (Thursdays through fall), and Helen's Restaurant on Main Street, a diner that has been feeding locals since 1980 with no-fuss eggs and griddle cakes. Fill your tank here — the next reliable cluster of gas stations is 30 miles north in Haverstraw or Peekskill.

## Cold Spring, NY (45 minutes from Nyack via NY-9W)

NY-9W north hugs the river bluff and delivers you into Cold Spring in about 45 minutes. The town's main drag, Main Street, runs straight down to a Hudson River dock — one of the few places on the east bank where you can literally stand at water's edge. The Cold Spring Depot restaurant, housed in an 1893 train station, does solid Hudson Valley comfort food for lunch. The Hudson Highlands State Park Preserve trailheads are two miles east on NY-301 if you want to stretch your legs on the Bull Hill trail with river views. September weather is typically 65–75°F, but afternoon thunderstorms are common; check radar before committing to a long hike.

## Rhinebeck, NY (1 hour from Cold Spring via US-9 north)

US-9 north through Beacon and Poughkeepsie is the practical spine of this trip. Rhinebeck is the Hudson Valley at its most polished: Federal-era homes, the Beekman Arms Inn (operating since 1766, making it one of the oldest continuously operating inns in the country), and a walkable village center with real butcher shops and wine bars. The Rhinebeck Farmers Market runs Sundays through November. If you're spending a night, the Beekman Arms fills fast on fall weekends — book at least three weeks out for September dates.

## Hudson, NY (25 minutes from Rhinebeck via US-9 north)

Hudson is the most urban stop on this route — population around 6,000, but Warren Street holds more antique dealers, art galleries, and serious restaurants per block than most cities ten times its size. Stop at Mexican Radio on Warren Street for a late lunch or early dinner; the mole negro is made in-house. Hudson also has the last reliable fuel stop before you turn west onto NY-28 toward Cooperstown — fill up at the Stewart's on US-9 before leaving town.

## Cooperstown, NY (1 hour 45 minutes from Hudson via NY-23 west and NY-28 south)

The routing from Hudson to Cooperstown is the most scenic stretch of the trip. Take NY-23 west from Hudson to Oneonta, then NY-28 south along the Susquehanna River valley to Cooperstown — a two-lane road through farm country with almost no commercial development. Cooperstown itself is famous for the National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum, but the town around it — Victorian storefronts on Main Street, Otsego Lake at the south end of town, the Farmers' Museum on Lake Road — holds its own. Lunch or dinner at Doubleday Café on Main Street keeps things simple and local. Note: NY-28 through the Catskills can have deer on the road at dawn and dusk in September; drive accordingly.

## Seneca Falls, NY (1 hour 30 minutes from Cooperstown via US-20 west)

US-20 west is a long, straight run across central New York's ridge-and-valley terrain. Seneca Falls anchors the eastern end of the Finger Lakes and is home to the Women's Rights National Historical Park, which preserves the site of the 1848 Seneca Falls Convention. It's a compact, walkable site — plan 90 minutes. The town is quieter than the wine-country villages to the south, which makes it a good overnight if you want cheaper lodging before the final push to Watkins Glen. Fuel is readily available on US-20 through town.

## Watkins Glen, NY (45 minutes from Seneca Falls via NY-414 south)

NY-414 south runs the length of Seneca Lake's western shore — vineyards and lake views the whole way. Watkins Glen is the endpoint and earns its place: Watkins Glen State Park (entrance on Franklin Street) has a 1.5-mile gorge trail with 19 waterfalls cut through Devonian-age shale, and it's walkable from the village center. The park can close temporarily after heavy rain due to slippery conditions — check the New York State Parks website the morning of your visit. For a final meal, Seneca Harbor Station on North Franklin Street does lake fish and local wine with dock views. The Finger Lakes wine region is in full harvest mode by early September; Riesling and dry rosé from Seneca Lake producers are the local call.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Nyack to Watkins Glen?

The point-to-point drive covers approximately 310 miles and takes about 6 hours 30 minutes of moving time. With stops in six towns, plan a minimum of three days to avoid rushing.

### Is NY-9W safe to drive in fall?

NY-9W is a well-maintained state highway, but the stretch through the Hudson Highlands between Haverstraw and Cold Spring is two lanes with sharp curves and no shoulder in places. Deer crossings increase significantly after Labor Day; drive at posted speeds and stay alert at dusk.

### Does Watkins Glen State Park require a reservation in September?

As of 2026, Watkins Glen State Park does not require advance reservations for day-use gorge trail access, but parking lots at the main Franklin Street entrance fill by 10 a.m. on weekends. Arrive before 9 a.m. or use the overflow lot on NY-414 and walk in.

### What is the best time of year to drive the Hudson Valley to Finger Lakes route?

Early September through mid-October is the prime window. Foliage typically peaks in the Hudson Valley around Columbus Day weekend and in the Finger Lakes a week or two later. Expect higher lodging prices and more traffic in the second and third weeks of October.

### Are there EV charging stations along this route?

Charging options are available in Poughkeepsie (near US-9), Hudson, and Watkins Glen (at the state park lot). The Cooperstown stretch on NY-28 has limited fast-charging; plan a full charge before leaving Hudson if driving an EV.

### Do any of these towns have good options for solo travelers?

Hudson and Rhinebeck both have walkable, independent restaurant scenes that work well for solo dining. Cold Spring and Watkins Glen are compact enough to explore on foot in a half-day without needing a group itinerary.
