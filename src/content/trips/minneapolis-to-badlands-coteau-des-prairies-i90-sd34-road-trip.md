**This Minneapolis-to-Badlands road trip covers roughly 460 miles via I-90 West and SD-34, with about 8 hours of total drive time split across two days. It's the best route for drivers who want genuine Great Plains context before arriving at the Badlands' eroded buttes — not just a straight interstate sprint.**

Most people floor it down I-90 from Sioux Falls and arrive at the Badlands feeling like they missed something. This route peels off early onto SD-34 to cross the Coteau des Prairies — a glacially raised plateau that gives you real prairie sky, working grain elevators, and a Laura Ingalls Wilder detour before rejoining I-90 at Mitchell. August is peak season; expect temperatures in the high 80s to mid-90s°F at the Badlands and afternoon thunderstorms that can be severe on the open prairie. Plan accordingly.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~460 miles
- **Total drive time:** ~8 hours (recommend 2 days)
- **Best season:** Late May–September; August is hot but fully accessible
- **Number of stops:** 5 (plus start)
- **Starting point:** Minneapolis, MN
- **Ending point:** Badlands National Park, SD (Ben Reifel Visitor Center)

## Who This Route Is For

- **Good for families with older kids** who can appreciate the Ingalls Wilder history in De Smet and the dramatic landscape change from prairie to Badlands
- **Suitable for most RVs and tow vehicles** — I-90 and SD-34 are flat, wide, and well-maintained; no low-clearance issues
- **Ideal for first-time Badlands visitors** who want geographic context rather than a rushed day trip from Rapid City
- **Not recommended for anyone with a tight 1-day schedule** — the SD-34 detour adds an hour but is worth it; if you're time-crunched, stick to I-90 straight through

## Minneapolis, MN — Starting Point

Fuel up before you leave the metro. Gas prices drop once you cross into South Dakota, but the last reliably cheap fill in Minnesota is along I-494 near Eden Prairie. Head west on I-394, then south on I-494 to pick up I-90 West at Albert Lea, MN — or take US-212 West directly from Minneapolis toward Montevideo to start the prairie feeling early. US-212 is slower but more scenic if you have the time.

## Brookings, SD (approx. 3 hours from Minneapolis via I-90)

Brookings is your first real stop and a practical one — fuel, food, and a chance to stretch. The town sits just off I-90 at Exit 132 and is home to South Dakota State University. For lunch, Nick's Hamburgers on Main Avenue has been serving thin, griddle-smashed burgers since 1929. It's a genuine counter-service institution, not a nostalgia act. From Brookings, pick up SD-34 West toward De Smet. This is where the route diverges from the standard I-90 grind.

## De Smet, SD (approx. 1 hour from Brookings via SD-34)

SD-34 cuts through classic prairie — flat, wide-open, and dotted with sloughs that attract waterfowl in late summer. De Smet (population ~1,100) is the real-life setting for several of Laura Ingalls Wilder's *Little House* books, and the Laura Ingalls Wilder Memorial Society operates two sites worth 45–60 minutes: the Surveyor's House (the family's first winter here) and the Ingalls home. Even if you're not a Wilder reader, the town's grain elevator skyline and courthouse square are a good reminder of what the northern plains actually look like. Fuel is available in De Smet — fill up here before continuing west on SD-34, as the next reliable options are 55 miles away in Huron or further south.

## Mitchell, SD (approx. 1.5 hours from De Smet via SD-34 / US-281 South)

From De Smet, take SD-34 west to US-281 South into Mitchell, where you rejoin I-90. Mitchell is known for the Corn Palace — an auditorium covered in murals made entirely of South Dakota-grown corn, rye, and grain. It's genuinely strange and worth 20 minutes. More practically, Mitchell has the last major cluster of gas stations, grocery stores, and restaurants before the landscape opens up. We recommend Depot Bar & Grill on South Main Street for a sit-down dinner if you're overnighting here. Mitchell makes a logical overnight stop on a 2-day itinerary.

## Chamberlain, SD (approx. 45 minutes from Mitchell via I-90 West)

Chamberlain sits at the Missouri River crossing on I-90 — Exit 263. The Dignity sculpture, a 50-foot stainless steel figure of a Lakota woman, stands on the bluff above the river and is visible from I-90. Pull off at the rest area on the westbound side for a proper look. The Missouri River breaks here are also the visual signal that you've left the glaciated eastern plains and entered the drier, more rugged West River country. This is a fuel and water stop, not an overnight — but don't skip it. The landscape west of Chamberlain shifts noticeably within 20 miles.

**August note:** Afternoon thunderstorms are common on I-90 between Chamberlain and the Badlands. Check the National Weather Service forecast for Pennington and Jackson counties before this leg. Hail and strong winds are possible; if a severe thunderstorm warning is active, wait it out in Chamberlain or Murdo.

## Badlands National Park, SD (approx. 2 hours from Chamberlain via I-90 West / SD-240)

Exit I-90 at Exit 131 (Wall) or Exit 110 (Interior) to enter Badlands National Park via SD-240, the Badlands Loop Road. The 32-mile loop connects both exits and passes all major overlooks: Big Badlands Overlook, Yellow Mounds, Pinnacles, and the Door and Window Trails. The Ben Reifel Visitor Center is at Cedar Pass near the Interior entrance. Entry fee is $35 per vehicle (valid 7 days) — America the Beautiful passes accepted.

For lodging, Cedar Pass Lodge inside the park books out weeks in advance in August; confirm your reservation before leaving Minneapolis. Wall, SD (Exit 131) has more motel options and is 8 miles from the Pinnacles entrance. Wall Drug on Main Street in Wall is obligatory — not because of the kitsch, but because it has a reliable hot food counter and the largest parking lot for 100 miles. Our [Black Hills and Badlands budget guide](https://mynavio.com/blog/black-hills-badlands-budget-road-trip-rapid-city-wall) covers cost-saving strategies for this area in detail if you're extending the trip west toward Rapid City.

August sunrise at the Badlands — around 6:45 a.m. MDT — is the best time to photograph the formations and avoid the worst heat. By 2 p.m., surface temperatures on the formations exceed 100°F. Carry at least 2 liters of water per person for any trail longer than 1 mile.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Minneapolis to Badlands National Park?

The direct route via I-90 West is approximately 430 miles and takes about 6.5 hours without stops. This itinerary via SD-34 through De Smet and Mitchell adds roughly 30 miles and 1–1.5 hours. Most drivers split the trip over two days with an overnight in Mitchell or Chamberlain.

### Is SD-34 suitable for RVs and trailers?

Yes. SD-34 is a two-lane state highway with wide shoulders and no significant grades or curves. It handles RVs and tow vehicles without issue. The main consideration is limited services between De Smet and Huron — fuel up before leaving De Smet.

### Do you need a reservation to enter Badlands National Park in August?

Badlands National Park does not currently require a timed-entry reservation for vehicle access. You pay the $35 entry fee at the gate. However, Cedar Pass Lodge and the park campground fill quickly in August — those reservations should be made months in advance through Recreation.gov.

### Are there severe weather risks on this route in late August?

Yes. Late August still falls within South Dakota's active thunderstorm season. Severe cells with large hail and 60+ mph winds can develop quickly over the open plains between Mitchell and the Badlands. Monitor the Storm Prediction Center outlook before driving the I-90 segment west of Chamberlain, and have a shelter plan ready.

### Where should you stop for gas on the stretch between Chamberlain and Wall, SD?

Murdo, SD (I-90 Exit 192) is the main fuel stop between Chamberlain and Wall — it's roughly 60 miles west of Chamberlain. Don't skip it if your tank is below half. The next reliable fuel is Wall, SD, another 57 miles west. Badlands National Park itself has no gas stations.

### Can you do this Minneapolis-to-Badlands route in one day?

Technically yes, but only on the direct I-90 route — roughly 6.5–7 hours of driving. The SD-34 variant through De Smet is not feasible as a single driving day if you want any meaningful time at the Badlands. We recommend two days for anyone wanting more than a windshield view of the park.
