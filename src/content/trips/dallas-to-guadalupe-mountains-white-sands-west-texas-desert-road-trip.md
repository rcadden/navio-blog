**This Dallas to White Sands road trip covers roughly 650 miles and 9.5 hours of driving time, linking two national parks — Guadalupe Mountains and White Sands — across the Chihuahuan Desert via I-20 West and US-180. Plan three days minimum; two of them involve serious desert driving with long fuel gaps. The verdict: more rewarding and less crowded than the standard Big Bend run, especially in July.**

Most Dallas drivers aiming west default to Big Bend. This route takes I-20 through the Permian Basin, then drops south on US-180 into a landscape that shifts from oil-pump country to raw limestone reef to blinding white gypsum dunes — three genuinely different environments in under 650 miles. July heat is real (expect 95–105°F in the lowlands), but both national parks have high-elevation trails and underground options that make summer workable.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~650 miles
- **Total drive time:** ~9.5 hours (spread over 3 days)
- **Best season:** Spring and fall are ideal; summer is doable with early starts and park timing
- **Number of stops:** 5 major stops plus Dallas origin
- **Starting point:** Dallas, TX
- **Ending point:** White Sands National Park, NM

## Who This Route Is For

- **National park collectors** who want two parks (plus Carlsbad Caverns) in one loop without retracing I-10
- **Drivers with standard sedans or crossovers** — all roads are paved and well-maintained; no high-clearance required
- **Not ideal for RVs over 35 ft** at Guadalupe Mountains — the Pine Springs Campground has tight pull-throughs and the US-180 approach has no RV services for 60+ miles
- **Good choice for adults-only or older-kid trips** — White Sands is all-ages magic, but Guadalupe Mountains trails are rocky and exposed, not stroller-friendly

## Dallas to Abilene (Stop 1)

Leave Dallas on I-20 West. Abilene is 180 miles and about 2 hours 40 minutes out — a reasonable first fuel and food stop before the Permian Basin empties out. **Joe Allen's Bar-B-Q** on South 1st Street has been feeding West Texas travelers since 1957; the brisket plate is the move. Fill up here. Gas stations thin considerably west of Abilene along I-20 through Big Spring.

*July note:* Afternoon thunderstorms are common on the eastern Caprock between Dallas and Abilene. Check radar before leaving; they move fast and can drop hail.

## Midland/Odessa, TX (Stop 2)

*From Abilene: ~150 miles, about 2 hours on I-20 West*

Midland and Odessa are primarily a logistics stop — fuel, lodging, and a gear check before the desert stretch. If you're spending night one here, the **Petroleum Club of Midland** is a surprisingly good dinner option if you want something beyond chain restaurants. More practically: top off your tank completely in Midland before heading south on TX-385 or west on I-20 toward Pecos. The 110-mile corridor between Pecos and the Guadalupe Mountains on US-180 has one fuel stop in Dell City — don't skip it.

## Guadalupe Mountains National Park, TX (Stop 3)

*From Midland: ~150 miles, about 2 hours via I-20 West to US-180 South*

Guadalupe Mountains National Park is the least-visited national park in the lower 48, which means you'll have the trails largely to yourself. The Pine Springs Visitor Center sits right on US-180 and is your first stop. **Guadalupe Peak Trail** (8.4 miles round trip, 3,000 ft gain) is the highest point in Texas at 8,749 ft — do it at first light to beat the heat and catch views before afternoon clouds build. If a full summit isn't the plan, the **Devil's Hall Trail** (4.2 miles round trip) is rocky but shaded in the canyon and far more manageable in July heat.

Fuel note: **Dell City** on US-62/180, about 35 miles west of the park entrance, has a gas station. Confirm hours before banking on it — it keeps limited hours. The next reliable fuel east is Van Horn, 65 miles away via TX-54.

## Carlsbad Caverns National Park, NM (Stop 4)

*From Guadalupe Mountains: ~40 miles, about 45 minutes north on US-62/180*

Carlsbad Caverns National Park is the perfect July stop precisely because the Big Room sits at 56°F year-round — a genuine relief from triple-digit surface temps. The self-guided Big Room tour (1.25-mile loop) is accessible via elevator and requires no fitness level. The Natural Entrance route (1.25 miles, steep descent) is the better experience if your group can handle it. **Brazilian free-tailed bat flight** at dusk runs nightly from late May through October; the ranger-led program at the amphitheater is free and genuinely impressive — 400,000 bats is not a small number.

Carlsbad city (30 miles north on US-285) has the widest lodging and restaurant options in the area. **Trinity Hotel Restaurant** in downtown Carlsbad has solid New Mexican food in a restored 1892 bank building.

## White Sands National Park, NM (Stop 5)

*From Carlsbad: ~90 miles, about 1 hour 30 minutes west on US-62/180 through El Paso corridor, then US-54 North to US-70 West*

White Sands National Park is the endpoint and the payoff. The Dunes Drive (8-mile paved loop) is accessible to all vehicles. The **Alkali Flat Trail** (5 miles round trip) is the most immersive option — you walk into the backcountry of pure gypsum dunes with no shade and no landmarks, which sounds punishing but is genuinely disorienting in the best way. Bring 3+ liters of water per person.

*Critical July timing:* White Sands closes Dunes Drive for approximately 2 hours, 2–3 times per week, for **White Sands Missile Range** testing operations on US-70. Closures are unannounced. Check the NPS alerts page the morning you visit and budget extra time. Alamogordo (15 miles east on US-70) is the practical base town — fuel, lodging, and the **Waffle & Pancake Shoppe** for a pre-dunes breakfast.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Dallas to White Sands National Park?

The drive is approximately 650 miles and 9.5 hours of moving time via I-20 West and US-180. Most drivers split it across two days, overnighting in Midland or Carlsbad. A three-day itinerary that includes time at Guadalupe Mountains and Carlsbad Caverns is more realistic.

### Is US-180 through the Guadalupe Mountains safe to drive in summer?

US-180 is a well-maintained two-lane highway with no technical difficulty. The hazards in July are heat-related: tire blowouts from hot pavement are more common, and fuel gaps of 60–70 miles exist between Van Horn, Dell City, and Carlsbad. Carry water in the vehicle, check tire pressure before leaving Midland, and don't let the tank drop below a quarter.

### Do you need a permit to hike Guadalupe Peak?

No permit is required for Guadalupe Peak Trail. A park entrance pass is required ($15 per person, valid 7 days, or covered by America the Beautiful annual pass). There is no backcountry permit required for day hikes.

### When does White Sands close for missile testing?

White Sands Missile Range conducts periodic missile tests that close US-70 (the main access highway) and Dunes Drive. Closures typically last 1–3 hours and occur 2–4 times per week, but schedules are not publicly released in advance. Check the White Sands NPS website or call the park the morning of your visit.

### Is this route doable in a standard passenger car?

Yes. Every road on this itinerary — I-20, US-180, US-62, US-54, and US-70 — is paved and requires no high-clearance or 4WD. The only consideration is heat management: carry at least 2 gallons of water in the car in case of a breakdown in the desert sections between Pecos and Carlsbad.

### What is the best time of day to visit White Sands in July?

Arrive at opening (7 a.m. in summer) or within 90 minutes of sunset. Midday surface temperatures on the dunes can exceed 120°F. The light at golden hour on the gypsum is worth timing your drive around.
