**This 170-mile Utah family road trip runs from St. George to Bryce Canyon City via UT-9 and US-89, covering two iconic national parks in roughly 3.5 hours of total driving time spread across 7 days. It is the most reward-per-mile family park itinerary in the American Southwest — big scenery, short hikes, and no 500-mile slogs between stops.**

Utah's Mighty 5 gets the headlines, but for families with kids under 14, the Zion-to-Bryce corridor is the most manageable and visually rewarding stretch. Driving distances are short enough that you spend your time on trails rather than in the car, and both parks have Junior Ranger programs that keep younger travelers engaged from the first morning briefing.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** 170 miles (St. George to Bryce Canyon City)
- **Total drive time:** ~3.5 hours (spread across 7 days)
- **Best season:** Late spring and early fall; summer works with early starts (see hazards below)
- **Number of major stops:** 4
- **Starting point:** St. George, UT
- **Ending point:** Bryce Canyon City, UT

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for families with children ages 4–14** — both parks have paved, stroller-accessible viewpoints alongside more challenging trails
- **Good fit for standard SUVs and minivans** — all roads are paved; no high-clearance vehicle required
- **Not recommended for RVs over 22 feet** entering Zion Canyon itself — the National Park Service prohibits private vehicles in the main canyon; you park in Springdale and ride the free shuttle
- **A strong choice for first-time Southwest road trippers** who want concentrated scenery without committing to a 1,000-mile loop

## Stop 1: St. George, UT — Base & Gear-Up Day

St. George sits at the junction of I-15 and the gateway to UT-9, making it the logical overnight before entering Zion. Fuel up here — this is your last easy, competitively priced gas before the park corridor. The Maverik on S. Bluff Street (1091 S. Bluff St.) is open 24 hours and reliably cheaper than the single station in Springdale. Stock a cooler: food inside both parks is limited and expensive.

For dinner the night before departure, Cliffside Restaurant on Sunset Boulevard has consistent reviews and handles large family groups without long waits on weekday evenings.

**Summer hazard:** St. George regularly hits 105–110°F in July. Plan all outdoor activity before 10 a.m. or after 5 p.m. This applies throughout the trip.

## Stop 2: Zion National Park (45 min from St. George via UT-9)

From St. George, take I-15 north to Exit 16, then head east on UT-9 through Hurricane and La Verkin. The drive into the park through Zion–Mount Carmel Tunnel is itself a highlight — kids consistently rate the 1.1-mile tunnel and the switchbacks above it among the trip's most memorable moments.

**Permit alert (July 2026):** Zion requires timed-entry permits for Angels Landing. As of the 2025–2026 permit cycle, lottery reservations open four months in advance through recreation.gov. For a July trip, enter the lottery in March. The Angels Landing trail is not appropriate for children under 8 due to exposed chain sections. A better family alternative is the Emerald Pools Trail (Lower and Middle pools: 1.2 miles round-trip, paved), which stays manageable in heat if you start by 7:30 a.m.

The free Zion Canyon Shuttle runs from the Zion Canyon Visitor Center to eight stops inside the canyon. Private vehicles are not permitted on Zion Canyon Scenic Drive from roughly April through October. Park at the Visitor Center lot in Springdale (arrive before 8 a.m. or use the town shuttles from further down UT-9).

We recommend two full days here. Pick up Junior Ranger booklets at the Visitor Center on arrival.

## Stop 3: Springdale, UT — Overnight Town for Zion

**(0 min from park entrance — Springdale borders the park)**

Springdale is a single-street town on UT-9 with lodging, restaurants, and gear shops within walking distance of the shuttle stop. Café Soleil on Zion Park Boulevard opens at 7 a.m. and is the best spot for a real breakfast before an early shuttle ride — the egg burritos are large enough to carry you through a morning hike. Book lodging in Springdale at least 90 days out for July; rates spike and availability drops fast.

## Stop 4: Red Canyon, Dixie National Forest (1 hr 15 min from Springdale via UT-9 East and US-89 North)

Leaving Springdale, continue east on UT-9 to US-89, then head north. Red Canyon sits inside Dixie National Forest just west of UT-12 on US-12 — a short detour off US-89 that most families drive straight past. Stop here. The Pink Ledges Trail (0.9 miles, flat) threads through hoodoos nearly as dramatic as Bryce at a fraction of the crowd density. There is a pit toilet but no gas; fill up in Panguitch (10 miles north on US-89) at the Chevron on UT-143 before heading to Bryce.

This stop works well as a picnic lunch break. The Dixie National Forest Red Canyon Campground has shaded tables even if you are not camping.

## Stop 5: Bryce Canyon National Park (30 min from Red Canyon via US-89 and UT-63)

From Panguitch, take US-89 south briefly, then UT-12 east to UT-63 south into the park. Bryce sits at 8,000–9,000 feet elevation — noticeably cooler than Zion, which makes it more forgiving for July afternoons. The Rim Trail along the canyon edge is largely paved and runs 5.5 miles between viewpoints; families with younger kids can walk any segment between the free park shuttles.

For the signature experience, hike the Navajo Loop Trail (1.3 miles, 550-foot descent) from Sunset Point. The hoodoo formations at close range are unlike anything in the American West. Note: the descent is steep and the return climb is strenuous — bring water and allow 90 minutes minimum.

**No-permit required:** Unlike Zion's Angels Landing, Bryce's main trails require no advance permits as of July 2026.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Zion National Park to Bryce Canyon National Park?

The direct drive via UT-9 east, US-89 north, and UT-63 south is approximately 85 miles and takes about 1 hour 45 minutes without stops. With a stop at Red Canyon, budget 2.5 hours. Most families split this over two travel days.

### Is Zion National Park safe for kids in July?

Yes, with precautions. July temperatures in the canyon floor regularly exceed 100°F by midday. Start hikes before 8 a.m., carry at least one liter of water per person per hour of activity, and avoid strenuous trails between 11 a.m. and 4 p.m. Flash flood risk also increases in July during afternoon thunderstorms — check the park's daily weather alerts before entering The Narrows.

### Do you need a permit to hike in Bryce Canyon National Park?

As of 2026, no timed-entry or hiking permits are required for Bryce Canyon's standard trails, including the Navajo Loop and Queen's Garden. The park does use a free shuttle system in peak season; private vehicles can still drive the main park road to all overlooks.

### Are there EV charging stations along this route?

Springdale has a small number of Level 2 chargers at select hotels, and Bryce Canyon City has a Tesla Destination Charger at The Lodge at Bryce Canyon. DC fast charging is not reliably available between St. George and Bryce; EV drivers should plan around Level 2 overnight charging and confirm availability before booking.

### What is the best age for kids on the Zion–Bryce Canyon road trip?

Ages 6–14 get the most out of both parks. Children under 6 can enjoy viewpoints and the shuttle rides but may struggle with heat and trail distances. Teenagers old enough to qualify for Angels Landing permits (no minimum age, but NPS recommends maturity and comfort with heights) have additional trail options at Zion.

### Can you do Zion and Bryce Canyon in one week with kids?

Yes — one week is the ideal minimum. We recommend two nights in Springdale for Zion, one night in Panguitch or Tropic for the midpoint, and two nights in Bryce Canyon City for the canyon. That leaves a travel day on each end without feeling rushed.
