**This San Diego to Anza-Borrego Desert State Park road trip covers roughly 130 miles via CA-79 and County Road S-22, with a total drive time of about 2 hours 45 minutes point-to-point — but plan a full day or overnight. It's the best fall desert drive within three hours of San Diego, trading the coast for canyon walls, apple orchards, and open bajada as temperatures finally drop to something tolerable.**

By September in Southern California, the inland desert has cooled from its brutal summer highs into the low 90s°F — still warm, but genuinely driveable and hikeable again. Anza-Borrego Desert State Park, California's largest state park at 600,000 acres, draws far fewer visitors than Joshua Tree and rewards drivers with a route that shifts terrain three times before you reach Borrego Springs. This is a trip that earns its scenery.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~130 miles (San Diego to Borrego Springs)
- **Total drive time:** 2 hours 45 minutes (driving only; allow 6–8 hours with stops)
- **Best season:** Late September through November; early October is peak for mild temps
- **Number of stops:** 4 main stops plus start
- **Starting point:** San Diego, CA
- **Ending point:** Borrego Springs, CA

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for weekend warriors** who want genuine desert solitude without a 3-hour freeway slog east on I-10
- **Good for sedans and standard SUVs** — all main route roads are paved; the Fonts Point spur requires a high-clearance 4WD vehicle or a short walk
- **Not recommended for RVs over 28 feet** on S-22 west of Borrego Springs due to steep grades and tight curves descending into the valley
- **Strong choice for photographers and stargazers** — Borrego Springs is an International Dark Sky Community, and October's clear nights are exceptional

## San Diego (Start)

Leave San Diego via I-8 East to CA-79 North at the Japatul Valley Road exit near Alpine. Fill up in Alpine or Descanso — this is your last reliable cluster of gas stations before Julian, and you won't find cheap fuel again until you return to the coast. Alpine's Chevron on Tavern Road is convenient right off I-8.

The climb up CA-79 from Descanso into the Cuyamaca Mountains is immediate. You'll gain over 4,000 feet in about 25 miles, passing through Cuyamaca Rancho State Park where September often brings the first hints of color to the black oaks.

## Julian *(approximately 1 hour from San Diego)*

Julian sits at 4,235 feet on CA-79 and is known for one thing above all else: apple pie. The harvest runs September through November, and Mom's Pie House on Main Street has been serving thick-crusted apple pie since 1984 — get a slice with sharp cheddar if you want the local way. Julian Pie Company one block over is the other anchor; both have lines on fall weekends, so arrive before 11 a.m. or after 2 p.m.

Beyond pie, Julian's Eagle Mining Company offers 45-minute underground gold mine tours that are genuinely interesting and take about an hour with the walk. The town is small — you can cover Main Street in 30 minutes — so don't over-schedule here. Fill up at the Julian Chevron on CA-78 before leaving; it's the last gas before the descent into the desert.

**Seasonal note:** CA-79 through the Cuyamacas can see brief afternoon thunderstorms in early September as the monsoon season winds down. Check the National Weather Service San Diego forecast before departure. Snow is not a factor in September or October, but the road can close after heavy winter storms if you're planning a later fall trip.

## Ranchita / CA-79 to S-22 Junction *(approximately 25 minutes from Julian)*

Head north on CA-79 from Julian through Santa Ysabel and Wynola, then bear right onto County Road S-22 (Montezuma Valley Road) at Ranchita. This is where the trip pivots from mountain to desert. The descent from 4,500 feet to the desert floor over the next 12 miles is one of the most dramatic elevation drops on any paved road in Southern California — the Borrego Valley spreads out below you like a topographic map come to life.

There are no services on S-22 between Ranchita and Borrego Springs. That's 22 miles of switchbacks and open desert. If you didn't fill up in Julian, turn back. S-22 is well-paved but narrow; watch your speed on the descent, especially in an SUV or loaded vehicle.

## Fonts Point Overlook *(approximately 30 minutes from Ranchita junction)*

About 4 miles east of where S-22 levels out onto the valley floor, watch for the signed Fonts Point Wash road on the south side of S-22. The overlook — a 4-mile round-trip dirt road through soft sand — requires a high-clearance 4WD vehicle. If you're in a standard sedan, park at the pullout and walk; it's 2 miles each way on flat ground and worth every step.

Fonts Point sits above the Borrego Badlands, a 1,200-foot drop into eroded clay formations that rival anything in the Colorado Plateau. At this time of year, afternoon light from 4–6 p.m. turns the badlands copper and rust. This is the photo stop of the trip.

## Borrego Springs *(approximately 15 minutes from Fonts Point turnoff)*

Borrego Springs is the only town inside Anza-Borrego Desert State Park and has a population of about 3,500. It's not a resort town in the polished sense — it's a working desert community with a good main street and remarkable dark skies. The Borrego Springs Resort on Palm Canyon Drive has a pool and a full bar, which matters after a dusty afternoon at Fonts Point.

For dinner, Carlee's Place on Christmas Circle has been the local bar and grill for decades — order the carne asada and don't expect anything fancy. The Crossroads Café opens early if you're staying overnight and want breakfast before the return drive.

The Anza-Borrego Desert State Park Visitor Center, just west of town off Palm Canyon Drive, has excellent interpretive displays on the Salton Sea geology and the park's famous metal sculpture installations scattered across the desert floor — Ricardo Breceda's life-size dinosaurs and prehistoric animals are free to visit and are genuinely worth the detour along Di Giorgio Road.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from San Diego to Borrego Springs via CA-79?
The drive is approximately 130 miles and takes about 2 hours 45 minutes without stops via I-8 East, CA-79 North, and S-22 East. With stops in Julian and at Fonts Point, budget 6–8 hours for a day trip or plan to stay overnight.

### Is S-22 (Montezuma Valley Road) safe to drive in a regular car?
Yes — S-22 is fully paved and manageable in any passenger car. The steep descent from Ranchita into Borrego Valley has tight curves; keep speeds under 35 mph on the grade. The Fonts Point spur road is unpaved sand and requires 4WD high-clearance; standard cars should not attempt it.

### Is Anza-Borrego Desert State Park open in September and October?
The park is open year-round. September and October are excellent months to visit — daytime highs drop from summer peaks into the mid-80s to low-90s°F, and the visitor center reopens its full seasonal hours. No entry fee is charged to drive through the park, though developed campgrounds require reservations.

### Where should I fill up gas before driving into Anza-Borrego?
Fill up in Alpine (off I-8) or in Julian on CA-78 before heading down S-22. There is one small gas station in Borrego Springs, but prices run significantly higher than the coast. Do not rely on finding fuel between Ranchita and Borrego Springs — that stretch has no services.

### Are there rattlesnakes or other hazards on fall hikes in Anza-Borrego?
Rattlesnakes are active in September and early October as temperatures remain warm. Stay on established trails, watch where you step around rocks and brush, and wear closed-toe shoes. The monsoon season (typically ending by mid-September) can leave washes flooded briefly after storms — check conditions at the visitor center before hiking slot canyons.

### Can I see the Borrego Springs metal sculptures without paying an entry fee?
Yes. The metal sculptures by Ricardo Breceda are located on private land owned by Dennis Avery and are free to view. The main cluster along Di Giorgio Road and Borrego Springs Road is accessible by any vehicle. A map is available at the visitor center or from the Anza-Borrego Desert Natural History Association.
