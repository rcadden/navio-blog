**The Dallas to Big Bend National Park road trip covers roughly 490 miles and 8–9 hours of driving via I-20 West and US-90 South — best spread across two days with an overnight in Marfa. This is the definitive West Texas route: wide-open Permian Basin highway, a cool art-town stop, and one of the most remote national parks in the lower 48.** Plan your departure early; summer heat in the Chihuahuan Desert is serious business, and you'll want to hike before 10 a.m.

If you've been eyeing Big Bend from Dallas and wondering whether it's worth the haul, it is — but only if you plan around the heat, fuel gaps, and the one or two stops that actually justify the drive. Here's the route we recommend.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~490 miles (Dallas to Panther Junction Visitor Center)
- **Total drive time:** 8–9 hours without stops; 2 days with sightseeing
- **Best season:** Late fall through early spring for hiking; summer (current season) is manageable if you drive at night or early morning and stay hydrated
- **Number of stops:** 4 major stops plus the park
- **Starting point:** Dallas, TX (I-20 West on-ramp near I-635)
- **Ending point:** Big Bend National Park, Panther Junction, TX

## Who This Route Is For

- **Best for solo drivers and couples** who want a genuine remote-Texas experience and are comfortable with long stretches of nothing between towns
- **Workable for families with older kids** (12+) who can handle heat and limited services; the park's Rio Grande Village and Chisos Basin have real facilities
- **Not recommended for large RVs over 24 ft** on the Chisos Basin Road — the NPS explicitly warns that vehicles over 24 ft cannot navigate the switchbacks safely
- **Good for first-time national park visitors** from the Dallas/Fort Worth area who want a park that feels genuinely wild rather than crowded

## Stop 1: Dallas — Your Starting Point

Leave Dallas via I-20 West. If you're coming from the northern suburbs, pick up I-20 near Weatherford to avoid downtown traffic. Fill your tank before leaving the Metroplex — gas is cheaper here than anywhere you'll stop for the next 300 miles. Aim for a 5–6 a.m. departure in summer to reach Abilene before the worst heat.

## Stop 2: Abilene, TX (2 hrs 15 min from Dallas via I-20 W)

Abilene sits right on I-20 at the 150-mile mark and is your first real fuel and food stop. Grab breakfast at Beehive Restaurant on North 3rd Street — a local institution doing solid eggs and biscuits since the 1980s. The town also has a Walmart and full fuel options if you want to stock a cooler for the desert stretch ahead. Don't skip this; the next quality food stop is 130 miles west.

## Stop 3: Midland/Odessa, TX (2 hrs from Abilene via I-20 W)

Midland and Odessa blur together along I-20, and neither is a postcard town, but they're strategically critical. **Fill your tank in Midland** before you drop south — once you leave Fort Stockton on US-285 toward the park, services thin out dramatically. The Petroleum Museum on I-20 in Midland is genuinely interesting if you have kids or any curiosity about how the Permian Basin shaped Texas; budget 45 minutes. Otherwise, fuel up at any station on Loop 250 and push south on TX-349 toward Fort Stockton.

## Stop 4: Fort Stockton, TX (1 hr 45 min from Midland via TX-349 S and US-285 S)

Fort Stockton is your last major resupply point before the park — 110 miles north of Panther Junction. Top off your gas tank here without exception. The H-E-B on US-285 is the best grocery option for stocking park supplies. Historic Fort Stockton (the actual 1867 frontier fort, now a museum) is worth a 30-minute walk-through if you're ahead of schedule. In summer, Fort Stockton regularly hits 100°F by noon; keep water in the car.

**Summer hazard note (June 2026):** Flash floods in the Big Bend area are common from late June through September due to monsoon moisture pushing north from Mexico. Check the NPS Big Bend road conditions page before leaving Fort Stockton — the Old Ore Road and River Road can close with zero notice after storms.

## Stop 5: Marfa, TX (1 hr from Fort Stockton via US-285 S to US-90 W)

Turn west on US-90 at Marathon and you'll hit Marfa in about 25 miles. Marfa is worth a proper overnight — book ahead, because summer weekends fill fast. The Thunderbird Hotel and El Cosmico are the two most-booked properties; El Cosmico suits tent campers and van-lifers, Thunderbird suits anyone who wants AC and a pool. Dinner at Food Shark (a long-running outdoor counter serving excellent falafel and Mediterranean plates) is the standard move, and it's well-earned.

The famous Marfa Lights Viewing Area sits on US-90 about 9 miles east of town — free, open all night, and genuinely unexplained. Marfa also connects to a broader Southwest road trip culture; if you're curious about extending into New Mexico, our [Denver to Santa Fe and Taos guide](https://mynavio.com/blog/denver-to-santa-fe-taos-high-road-new-mexico-road-trip) covers the northern end of that corridor well.

## Stop 6: Big Bend National Park, TX (1 hr 15 min from Marfa via US-67 S and US-385 S)

From Marfa, head south on US-67 to Presidio, then east on FM-170 (the River Road scenic drive) or continue on US-67 to US-385 North into the park — the FM-170 option adds 30–45 minutes but rewards with dramatic canyon views above the Rio Grande. Enter the park on US-385; the entrance fee is $35 per vehicle and is valid for 7 days.

In summer, skip low-elevation hikes like the Boquillas Canyon Trail during midday. The Chisos Basin — at 5,400 ft elevation — is noticeably cooler and where you'll find the Window Trail (5.6 miles round-trip) and the trailhead for the South Rim. Panther Junction Visitor Center has reliable cell service and current conditions. The Rio Grande Village store sells basic supplies and fuel (expensive — fill up in Study Butte/Terlingua just outside the west entrance if you can).

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Dallas to Big Bend National Park?

The drive from Dallas to Big Bend National Park's Panther Junction is approximately 490 miles and takes 8–9 hours of straight driving via I-20 West and US-285 South. We recommend splitting the drive across two days with an overnight stop in Marfa or Fort Stockton.

### Which route from Dallas to Big Bend is better — through Midland or through San Antonio?

The I-20/US-285 route through Midland is about 490 miles and slightly faster for drivers coming from Dallas. The I-10 route through San Antonio and Del Rio is longer (roughly 560–580 miles) but has more services and slightly better roads for RVs. The Midland route is the standard choice for DFW-area drivers.

### Is Big Bend worth visiting in summer?

Big Bend in summer is manageable but demanding. Temperatures at low elevations regularly exceed 105°F in June and July. The Chisos Basin at elevation stays 10–15 degrees cooler and is where most summer hiking happens. Come prepared with at least 1 liter of water per person per hour of hiking, start all hikes before 8 a.m., and avoid canyon-floor trails midday.

### Are there gas stations inside Big Bend National Park?

There is one fuel station at Rio Grande Village inside the park, but it is expensive and occasionally out of stock. Fill your tank in Study Butte or Terlingua just outside the west entrance (TX-118), or at Fort Stockton before heading south. Do not rely on Rio Grande Village fuel as your primary plan.

### Do you need a reservation to camp in Big Bend National Park in summer?

Yes. Chisos Basin Campground and Rio Grande Village Campground both require reservations through Recreation.gov, especially for summer weekends. Book at least 2–3 weeks in advance. Backcountry permits are available at the visitor center but require planning around flash flood conditions during monsoon season (June–September).

### Is US-90 between Marfa and the park dangerous to drive?

US-90 and US-67 are two-lane highways with long straight stretches and minimal services. The roads are in reasonable condition, but deer and javelinas cross at night, cell service is limited, and summer storms can create standing water quickly. Drive the Presidio-to-park stretch during daylight in summer, and let someone know your itinerary.
