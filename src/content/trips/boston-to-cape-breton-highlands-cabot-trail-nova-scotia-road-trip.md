**This Boston-to-Cape Breton road trip covers roughly 700 miles (1,125 km) and 13–15 hours of pure driving, best spread over 3–4 days. The verdict: it's the most underrated summer drive from New England, trading Acadia's crowds for dramatic sea cliffs, moose-lined highways, and a living Celtic music scene.**

Most Boston-area drivers who want ocean scenery and a national park default to Acadia. That's fine — but if you have an extra day and a valid passport, the Cabot Trail in Cape Breton Highlands National Park delivers a genuinely different experience: whale watches off Cheticamp, 300-meter coastal headlands, and roads so empty you'll forget you're near the Atlantic Seaboard. This guide routes you through the best stops on I-95 North, then across the US-Canada border at Calais, ME / St. Stephen, NB, and up through New Brunswick and Nova Scotia.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~700 miles (Boston to Ingonish, NS)
- **Total drive time:** 13–15 hours (without stops)
- **Recommended trip length:** 3–4 days
- **Best season:** Mid-June through September
- **Number of stops:** 7
- **Starting point:** Boston, MA
- **Ending point:** Cape Breton Highlands National Park, NS

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for drivers who've already done Acadia** and want a longer, less crowded alternative with genuine international flavor.
- **Good for couples and solo travelers** comfortable with a single border crossing; NEXUS or a valid passport is required — no REAL ID alone.
- **Not recommended for RVs over 35 ft** on the Cabot Trail's tighter switchbacks, particularly the north-facing descent into Pleasant Bay.
- **Strong fit for wildlife watchers:** July is peak season for minke and pilot whales off the Cape Breton coast, and moose-vehicle collisions are a real hazard on Nova Scotia's Trans-Canada Highway (Highway 104) after dusk — plan daytime driving.

## Stop 1: Portland, ME
*(~2 hours from Boston via I-95 North)*

Portland is a natural first overnight. Skip the tourist traps on Commercial Street and head directly to Duckfat (43 Middle Street) for Belgian frites and a panini — it's a short-line lunch institution and one of the best quick meals in New England. Fill your tank here; gas in Maine is consistently cheaper than in New Brunswick or Nova Scotia. The Maine Turnpike (I-95) runs clean and fast from Boston; expect no significant construction delays in summer 2026 based on current MDOT schedules, though the York Toll Plaza can back up Friday afternoons.

## Stop 2: Bangor, ME
*(~2 hours from Portland via I-95 North)*

Bangor is a practical fuel-and-food stop more than a destination, but it earns its place. Fill up again here — this is your last major US city before rural Maine and the border. If you're spending the night, the Penobscot Inn is clean and reasonably priced. From Bangor, US-9 East cuts toward Calais more directly than staying on I-95 to Houlton; it's two-lane and slower but saves 30 minutes and passes through Washington County's blueberry barrens.

## Stop 3: Calais, ME / St. Stephen, NB — US-Canada Border
*(~2.5 hours from Bangor via US-9 East)*

The Calais–St. Stephen crossing on US-1 is one of the quieter Maine border ports. Summer wait times are typically under 15 minutes in the morning; avoid crossing between 4–6 PM when commercial traffic peaks. Have your passport ready; US citizens do not need a Canadian eTA when entering by land. Once across, reset your odometer to kilometers and note that Nova Scotia speed limits are posted in km/h. Canadian gas stations accept US credit cards but prices are per liter — budget accordingly.

## Stop 4: Moncton, NB
*(~2 hours from St. Stephen via NB-1 East / Trans-Canada Highway)*

Moncton is the halfway fuel and rest stop. The city sits at the head of the Bay of Fundy, and if timing allows, the Tidal Bore on the Petitcodiac River (viewable from Bore Park on Main Street) is a genuine curiosity — the tide visibly reverses twice daily. Check the posted tide schedule at the park. For dinner, Laundromat Espresso Bar on Queen Street serves solid Maritime seafood chowder. Top off fuel here; highway options thin out toward Antigonish.

## Stop 5: Antigonish, NS
*(~3 hours from Moncton via Trans-Canada Highway 104 West)*

Antigonish is the last real town before Cape Breton Island and a logical overnight before tackling the Cabot Trail. The town hosts the Antigonish Highland Games every July — if your trip falls on the third weekend of July, expect limited accommodation availability and book ahead. St. Francis Xavier University's campus anchors the town. For a practical dinner, Gabrieau's Bistro on Main Street has been a local staple for decades. Fill your tank fully here; Cabot Trail gas stations exist but are spaced far apart and priced at a premium.

## Stop 6: Baddeck, NS
*(~45 minutes from Antigonish via Trans-Canada Highway 105 West)*

Baddeck sits on Bras d'Or Lake and is the traditional base camp for Cabot Trail drivers. The Alexander Graham Bell National Historic Site here is legitimately interesting — Bell spent his summers in Baddeck and conducted early aviation experiments on the lake. The Yellow Cello restaurant on Chebucto Street does reliable pan-seared halibut. Book accommodation in Baddeck at least 6 weeks out for July; the town has limited rooms and fills fast. This is also your last guaranteed fuel stop before entering the national park loop.

## Stop 7: Cape Breton Highlands National Park
*(~1.5 hours from Baddeck via Cabot Trail / NS-30 and NS-19)*

The Cabot Trail circumnavigates the northern tip of Cape Breton Island — 298 km (185 miles) of road through the national park. We recommend entering from the Cheticamp (western) gate first: the climb up French Mountain offers the most dramatic sea views, and the descent into Pleasant Bay is where you're most likely to spot moose near dusk. The Skyline Trail (10 km round trip) is the park's signature hike, ending at a headland boardwalk over the Gulf of St. Lawrence — arrive before 9 AM in July to avoid the peak crowds. Park entry requires a Parks Canada Discovery Pass (~CAD $22/day per adult in 2025 rates; confirm current pricing at Parks Canada's website). ## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Boston to Cape Breton Highlands National Park?

Pure driving time is 13–15 hours covering roughly 700 miles (1,125 km). We strongly recommend splitting the drive over at least 3 days with overnight stops in Portland or Bangor, Moncton or Antigonish, and Baddeck before entering the park.

### Do US citizens need a passport to drive from Boston to Nova Scotia?

Yes. A valid US passport (or passport card) is required for land border crossings into Canada. A REAL ID-compliant driver's license alone is not sufficient. Apply or renew at least 8–10 weeks before your trip date.

### Is the Cabot Trail dangerous to drive in summer?

The Cabot Trail is paved and well-maintained, but it includes steep grades and tight curves, particularly on the north-facing descent into Pleasant Bay. The primary hazards in July are moose on the road after dusk and fog on the headlands in the morning. Drive daylight hours on the trail and reduce speed in low-visibility conditions.

### Where do you fill up gas before the Cabot Trail?

Fill your tank completely in Baddeck before starting the Cabot Trail loop. Gas is available in Cheticamp and Ingonish on the trail, but stations are sparse and prices run 10–15% higher than in Baddeck or Antigonish.

### What is the best time of year to drive the Cabot Trail?

Mid-July through early September offers the warmest temperatures and fully open park facilities. July specifically delivers the longest daylight hours and peak whale-watching conditions off Cheticamp. Note that the park can receive fog and cool temperatures even in midsummer — pack a layer.

### Do you need a reservation to camp in Cape Breton Highlands National Park?

Yes. Parks Canada's reservation system (reservation.pc.gc.ca) opens in January for the summer season. Cheticamp and Ingonish campgrounds book out within days of opening for July dates. If you missed the window, check for cancellations or consider a Baddeck B&B as a base.
