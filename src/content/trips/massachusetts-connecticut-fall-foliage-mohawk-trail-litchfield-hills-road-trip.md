**This 210-mile loop drives west along the Mohawk Trail Scenic Byway (MA-2) from Greenfield, MA, then south through the Berkshires and into Connecticut's Litchfield Hills, finishing in New Milford, CT — total seat time is roughly 5 hours, but plan 3 to 4 days to actually see anything. It is the best under-the-radar fall color route in southern New England, avoiding the congestion of Vermont's I-89 corridor while delivering comparable maple and oak canopy at a fraction of the crowd.** Peak foliage in this region typically runs late September through mid-October, with the Berkshires peaking slightly ahead of Connecticut's hills. If you're planning this trip now for fall 2026, aim for the first two weeks of October.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~210 miles
- **Total drive time:** ~5 hours (seat time only)
- **Recommended duration:** 3–4 days
- **Best season:** Late September – mid-October
- **Number of stops:** 8
- **Starting point:** Greenfield, MA
- **Ending point:** New Milford, CT

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for couples or small groups** who want a mix of art, hiking, and small-town dining without fighting Vermont's peak-weekend traffic
- **Good for sedans and small SUVs** — all roads are paved and well-maintained; no clearance concerns
- **Not recommended for RVs over 35 ft** on MA-2 west of Charlemont; the Hairpin Turn descent into North Adams is steep and narrow
- **Strong choice for photographers** targeting covered bridges, river valleys, and elevated ridge views without a long flight or multi-state haul

## Stop 1: Greenfield, MA — Starting Point

Greenfield sits at the eastern gateway of the Mohawk Trail Scenic Byway and is your last reliable gas stop before the route narrows through the hills. Fill the tank here at the Cumberland Farms on Federal Street before heading west on MA-2. Grab breakfast at Greenfield's People's Pint brewpub if you're starting mid-morning — their eggs and locally sourced plates set the right pace. From I-91, take Exit 26 to MA-2 West.

## Stop 2: Shelburne Falls, MA (25 minutes from Greenfield)

About 12 miles west on MA-2, pull off into Shelburne Falls. The Bridge of Flowers — an old trolley bridge replanted as a walking garden — is worth 20 minutes on foot. By early October, the bridge's late-season asters and the surrounding sugar maples hit simultaneously. Cross the Deerfield River on the iron vehicle bridge and walk back on the flower bridge. No admission fee. Street parking is free on a weekday; arrive before 10 a.m. on weekends.

## Stop 3: North Adams, MA (45 minutes from Shelburne Falls)

Continue west on MA-2 through Charlemont and brace for the Hairpin Turn, a sharp switchback descent with a pull-off overlook at the top — do not skip it. The view down into the Hoosic River valley with full foliage color is one of the better elevated perspectives in Massachusetts. In North Adams, the Massachusetts Museum of Contemporary Art (Mass MoCA) anchors the afternoon. The museum's industrial mill complex with outdoor sculpture is worth 2–3 hours. Eat dinner at Public Eat + Drink on Eagle Street, a reliable farm-to-table spot with a solid short-rib option.

**Seasonal note:** MA-2 through the Hairpin Turn can see early-morning frost and black ice by mid-October. If you're driving before 8 a.m. in the second half of October, check MassDOT road conditions at 511.mass.gov before descending.

## Stop 4: Pittsfield, MA (30 minutes from North Adams)

Head south on US-7 from North Adams into Pittsfield, the commercial hub of the Berkshires. This is your best fuel and supply stop before the southern Berkshires and Connecticut. The Berkshire Botanical Garden on MA-102 in nearby Stockbridge is worth a detour if gardens are your thing. Otherwise, Pittsfield functions as a logistics stop — fuel, groceries, and a night's sleep if you're splitting the trip here.

## Stop 5: Great Barrington, MA (25 minutes from Pittsfield)

South on US-7, Great Barrington is the Berkshires' most walkable town. Monument Mountain (a short hike off US-7, no permit required, free parking) gives you an exposed quartzite ridge with 360-degree color views — the 2-mile round trip to Squaw Peak takes about 90 minutes at a moderate pace. Back in town, Baba Louie's on Railroad Street does wood-fired pizza worth stopping for. This is also your last full-service town before crossing into Connecticut.

## Stop 6: Lakeville, CT (40 minutes from Great Barrington)

Cross into Connecticut on US-44 West, then drop south on CT-41 into Lakeville. The town sits on Lake Wononscopomuc and produces a classic New England village reflection shot in fall. The White Hart Inn has been serving travelers since 1806 and remains a solid overnight option with a functional bar and dining room. Gas up in Lakeville — the next reliable stations are in Litchfield, 18 miles east.

## Stop 7: Litchfield, CT (25 minutes from Lakeville)

Take CT-118 East to Litchfield, Connecticut's best-preserved colonial town green. The green itself, ringed by 18th-century homes and framed by October maples, is the visual centerpiece of the Litchfield Hills. White Flower Farm nursery on US-202 South is open through October and worth a stop for gardeners. Litchfield also has a proper coffee shop — West Street Grill for lunch, or Bohemian Pizza for a casual dinner.

## Stop 8: New Milford, CT — End Point (35 minutes from Litchfield)

Finish on US-202 South into New Milford, a river town on the Housatonic. The New Milford town green is one of the longest in Connecticut and frames well against fall foliage. From here, I-84 East connects to Hartford and Boston; I-84 West connects to New York metro. The Silo at Hunt Hill Farm on Upland Road does a Saturday farmers market through October and stocks local cider, which makes a better trip-end souvenir than any gift shop.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive the Mohawk Trail Scenic Byway (MA-2) from Greenfield to North Adams?

The drive is roughly 35 miles and takes about 1 hour without stops. The road narrows significantly west of Charlemont, and the Hairpin Turn descent requires slow speeds regardless of conditions. Budget 2–3 hours if you're stopping at overlooks and Shelburne Falls.

### When does fall foliage peak along the Mohawk Trail and Litchfield Hills?

The Mohawk Trail corridor typically peaks between September 28 and October 12, with higher elevations (above 1,500 ft near the Hairpin Turn) going first. Litchfield Hills in Connecticut generally peak 7–10 days later, in the second week of October. Both windows can shift by 5–7 days depending on summer rainfall and early frost.

### Is the Hairpin Turn on MA-2 safe for large vehicles?

Passenger cars, minivans, and SUVs handle it without issue. RVs over 35 feet and vehicles towing large trailers should use an alternate route — US-7 North to MA-2 from the west avoids the descent entirely. The Massachusetts DOT posts seasonal advisories at 511.mass.gov.

### Do you need a permit to hike Monument Mountain in Great Barrington, MA?

No permit is required. Parking at the trailhead lot on US-7 is free. The lot fills by 9 a.m. on peak October weekends; arrive early or park along the road shoulder. The Trustees of Reservations manages the property.

### Where should you fill up gas before long stretches on this route?

Fill up in Greenfield before starting MA-2 West, and again in Pittsfield or Great Barrington before crossing into Connecticut. Lakeville has one station; after that, Litchfield is your next reliable option. Carry a mental note that rural CT-41 and CT-118 have no stations between towns.

### Is this route driveable as a day trip from Boston or New York?

From Boston, Greenfield is 1.5 hours on I-91 North — technically a long day trip, but you'll shortchange every stop. From New York City, New Milford is about 90 minutes on I-84, making a reverse one-day drive feasible if you skip North Adams. We recommend a minimum of two nights to do the full route justice.
