**This 108-mile route from Charleston, SC, to Savannah, GA, runs primarily on US-17 South and SC-174 with a detour through Beaufort on US-21, totaling about 2 hours of pure drive time and 5-6 hours when you stop to eat. Verdict: the most food-dense short road trip in the American South, period.**

The Low Country corridor between Charleston and Savannah is one of the few places in America where the food actually improves as you leave the city. Roadside shrimp stands, wood-smoke BBQ pits, and old-school oyster bars appear every 15-20 miles. August is hot and humid — temperatures regularly hit 94°F — but every restaurant on this list has AC, and the seafood is at peak summer freshness.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** 108 miles
- **Total drive time (no stops):** ~2 hours
- **Drive time with food stops:** 5-6 hours
- **Best season:** Spring and fall are cooler, but summer seafood quality is exceptional; avoid hurricane watches (peak season August–October)
- **Number of stops:** 5 major stops, 4 food-focused
- **Starting point:** Charleston, SC
- **Ending point:** Savannah, GA

## Who This Route Is For

- **Perfect for couples or friend groups** who want a focused one- or two-day food crawl without a massive mileage commitment
- **Good for most vehicles** — US-17 is a well-maintained two-lane and four-lane highway; no clearance issues for standard SUVs or trucks
- **Not ideal for RVs over 30 ft** on the SC-174 Edisto Island detour; the road narrows through maritime forest and parking at roadside stands is tight
- **Manageable first solo trip** — the route is straightforward, cell coverage is consistent, and towns are close enough that you're never stranded

## Charleston, SC — Starting Point

Fuel up before leaving Charleston. The last reliable gas on the Edisto detour is at the junction of SC-174 and SC-64 in Walterboro — fill the tank on James Island or in West Ashley on US-17 before heading south. For breakfast, **Poogan's Porch** on Queen Street has served shrimp and grits since 1976. Order the Low Country version — stone-ground grits, tasso ham gravy, Gulf shrimp — and you'll understand why this dish defines the region. Budget 45-60 minutes here.

## Edisto Island, SC — SC-174 Detour (45 minutes from Charleston)

Turn south off US-17 onto SC-174 at Jacksonboro. The 22-mile drive onto Edisto Island takes about 30 minutes and drops you into some of the quietest ACE Basin marshland in the state. The food payoff is **The Waterfront Restaurant at Edisto Marina**, which serves local shrimp harvested that morning — ask for the boiled platter with drawn butter, no frills. If it's before noon, **Whaley's** on Dock Site Road opens for lunch with a fried flounder sandwich that's worth the detour alone. Return to US-17 via SC-174 North.

**Summer hazard:** Edisto Beach State Park's campground fills completely on August weekends; if you're considering an overnight, book weeks ahead or skip it on this trip.

## Beaufort, SC — US-21 East (1 hour from Edisto, including backtrack to US-17)

From US-17, take US-21 East into Beaufort — about 25 minutes from the US-17/US-21 junction. Downtown Beaufort sits on a bend of the Beaufort River and its historic district is walkable from any of the main parking lots on Carteret Street. **Breakwater Restaurant** on Sea Island Parkway is the serious lunch stop on this route: whole fried flounder, she-crab soup, and a shrimp burger on a brioche bun. Portions are large enough to share. The downtown also has a small farmers market on Tuesday and Friday mornings in summer, where local vendors sell Sea Island red peas and Datil pepper hot sauce — both worth grabbing for the road.

For those who want BBQ instead of seafood, **Shrimp Shack on St. Helena Island** (US-21, about 4 miles east of Beaufort) is a cash-preferred window counter that's been smoking pork shoulders over hickory since the 1990s.

## Bluffton, SC — US-278 West (35 minutes from Beaufort)

Return to US-17 South and pick up US-278 West into Old Town Bluffton — a 10-minute side trip that punches well above its size for food. **Pepper's Porch** on May River Road does Low Country boils and oyster roasts year-round, and in summer they run a raw bar with May River oysters that are smaller and brinier than their Chesapeake cousins. This is also your last fuel stop before the Georgia state line if you bypassed the Edisto fill-up — there's a BP and a Shell on US-278 just before the US-17 intersection.

## Savannah, GA — US-17 South (30 minutes from Bluffton)

Cross the Savannah River on US-17 and you're in Georgia. Park once in the Savannah Historic District — the city's 22 squares are walkable — and make your last two food stops on foot. **Mrs. Wilkes' Dining Room** on Jones Street runs a family-style lunch (11 AM–2 PM, Monday–Friday) with fried chicken, collard greens, black-eyed peas, and cornbread served at communal tables. Arrive by 10:45 AM or expect a line. For dinner, **The Olde Pink House** on Abercorn Street does Carolina Gold rice pilau and whole Georgia flounder in a pre-Revolutionary building. Reservations are strongly recommended in August — walk-ins wait 90 minutes or more.

**Summer note:** Savannah's squares can feel brutal at midday in August; the city's tree canopy helps, but plan heavy meals for before noon or after 7 PM when temperatures drop a few degrees.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Charleston to Savannah?
The direct route on I-95 South takes about 1 hour 45 minutes for 107 miles. The US-17 and SC-174 scenic route with Beaufort and Edisto detours runs 108 miles of driving but takes 2 hours without stops — budget a full day if you're stopping to eat.

### Is US-17 between Charleston and Savannah safe to drive in summer?
US-17 is a well-maintained two- and four-lane highway and is generally safe. The primary summer risk is afternoon thunderstorms, which are frequent in August along the South Carolina and Georgia coast. Slow down on the narrow marsh causeways during heavy rain and watch for standing water on SC-174.

### Do you need a reservation at Mrs. Wilkes' Dining Room in Savannah?
Mrs. Wilkes' does not take reservations — it is first-come, first-served. Doors open at 11 AM Monday through Friday only; they are closed weekends and most holidays. Arriving by 10:45 AM is strongly advised during summer tourist season.

### What is the best food stop between Charleston and Savannah?
Beaufort's Breakwater Restaurant and Savannah's Mrs. Wilkes' Dining Room are consistently the two highest-quality stops on this corridor. For pure roadside character, the Shrimp Shack on St. Helena Island (US-21) is the most authentic single-stop experience on the route.

### Where should I fill up gas on this route?
Fill up in West Ashley or James Island before leaving Charleston. There is a station at the SC-174/SC-64 junction near Walterboro. Bluffton has reliable fuel on US-278 before the Georgia line. Savannah has stations throughout the historic district perimeter.

### Is this route suitable for a one-day trip or does it require an overnight?
This route works as a long one-day drive if you leave Charleston by 9 AM and skip one or two stops. For the full food itinerary with Edisto, Beaufort, and Bluffton, an overnight in Beaufort or Savannah makes the experience significantly less rushed.
