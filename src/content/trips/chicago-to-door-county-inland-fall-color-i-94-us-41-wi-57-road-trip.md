**This Chicago to Door County road trip covers approximately 310 miles via I-94 North, US-41 North, and WI-57 North, with a total drive time of 5.5 to 6.5 hours behind the wheel. Spread across two to three days, this inland-and-lakeshore hybrid route delivers the best fall color in the Midwest without the stop-and-go traffic of the I-94/US-141 Lake Michigan shoreline corridor — the smarter choice for late September.**

Leaving Chicago on a Friday afternoon in late September means you're hitting Door County right as the hardwoods on the peninsula peak — maples and oaks typically turn in the last week of September through mid-October. The route we recommend swings inland through Sheboygan and Green Bay before picking up Wisconsin State Highway 57 (WI-57) northbound onto the Door Peninsula. It's less congested than the coastal US-141 approach and passes through some of Wisconsin's best small-town food stops.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~310 miles
- **Total drive time:** 5.5–6.5 hours (driving only)
- **Recommended trip length:** 2–3 days
- **Best season:** Late September–mid-October (peak fall color)
- **Number of stops:** 6 (plus origin)
- **Starting point:** Chicago, IL
- **Ending point:** Ellison Bay, WI (tip of Door Peninsula)

## Who This Route Is For

- **Best for couples and friend groups** looking for a long-weekend fall escape from Chicago with genuine color payoff.
- **Good for drivers with standard sedans or crossovers** — all roads are well-maintained two-lane and interstate highways; no 4WD required.
- **Works for RVs up to about 35 feet** through the interstate legs; WI-57 through the peninsula handles most rigs, but parking in Ellison Bay and Fish Creek village centers is tight.
- **Not ideal for anyone who needs to be back in Chicago by Sunday evening** — Door County is popular in fall and traffic southbound on I-43 Sunday afternoons can add 45–60 minutes.

## Stop 1: Chicago, IL — Departure Point

Leave Chicago via I-94 North (the Edens Expressway). Fill up before you cross into Wisconsin — gas stations thin out once you're past Gurnee. If you're leaving early, grab coffee and an egg sandwich at Diner Grill on Irving Park Road; it opens at 6 a.m. and won't slow you down. Budget 20 minutes to clear the North Shore traffic.

## Stop 2: Kenosha, WI (about 1 hour from Chicago via I-94)

Kenosha sits just over the state line and makes a practical first break. Stretch your legs at Simmons Island Park on the Lake Michigan waterfront — the breakwater lighthouse is photogenic in fall morning light. For coffee, stop at Public Craft Brewing Co. on 56th Street; they open at 11 a.m. but the attached café runs earlier on weekends. From Kenosha, continue north on I-94 to Milwaukee, then pick up I-43 North toward Sheboygan.

## Stop 3: Sheboygan, WI (about 1 hour from Kenosha via I-94/I-43)

Sheboygan is the bratwurst capital of the United States and you should take that seriously. Stop at Miesfeld's Market on Calumet Drive for a double brat with brown mustard — this butcher shop has been operating since 1941 and the product is legitimately different from anything you'll find at a gas station. Sheboygan is also your last reliable fuel stop before the route thins out north of Manitowoc; fill the tank here. From Sheboygan, continue north on I-43.

## Stop 4: Manitowoc, WI (about 30 minutes from Sheboygan via I-43)

Manitowoc is a working port city with one genuinely worthwhile stop: the Wisconsin Maritime Museum on Maritime Drive, which houses a World War II-era submarine, the USS Cobia, that you can tour. Allow 90 minutes if you go inside. If you're skipping the museum, at least walk the riverfront — the Manitowoc River mouth at Lake Michigan turns gold in late September. From Manitowoc, stay on I-43 North toward Green Bay.

## Stop 5: Green Bay, WI (about 45 minutes from Manitowoc via I-43)

Green Bay is where the route pivots from interstate travel to the scenic leg. Before leaving, eat at Kroll's West on Military Avenue — a Green Bay institution since 1936, known for butter burgers and Friday fish fry. If you're arriving on a Friday night, expect a wait. From Kroll's, pick up WI-57 North out of Green Bay. This is the road that carries you all the way up the spine of the Door Peninsula through Brussels, Luxemburg, and Algoma before the landscape opens into orchard country.

## Stop 6: Sturgeon Bay, WI (about 1 hour from Green Bay via WI-57)

Sturgeon Bay is the gateway to the Door Peninsula proper, connected to the rest of Wisconsin by a drawbridge over the Sturgeon Bay Ship Canal. Late September is prime apple and cherry season — stop at Lautenbach's Orchard Country Winery and Market on WI-42 for cider, apple butter, and cherry wine. The orchard rows are in full color by the last week of September. Sturgeon Bay also has the peninsula's best fuel prices; fill up again here before heading north on WI-42 or WI-57 toward the tip.

## Stop 7: Ellison Bay, WI — Final Destination (about 45 minutes from Sturgeon Bay via WI-42)

Ellison Bay sits near the tip of the Door Peninsula, roughly 5 miles south of Gills Rock. The village is small and the parking is first-come-first-served, but it's the right place to end: quieter than Fish Creek, less commercial than Ephraim, and close enough to Peninsula State Park that you can drive the park road at sunset without backtracking. For dinner, the Viking Restaurant on WI-42 runs a traditional Door County fish boil — a whole-whitefish-and-potato cauldron that ends with the cook throwing kerosene on the fire to boil over the fish oil. It's theatrical, it's delicious, and it's the most Door County thing you can do.

**Seasonal note for late September 2026:** WI-57 and WI-42 are fully open in fall, but leaf-peeper traffic on weekends can slow the peninsula roads between 11 a.m. and 3 p.m. Plan your Sturgeon Bay to Ellison Bay leg for early morning or after 4 p.m. Peninsula State Park day-use areas can fill by 10 a.m. on Saturdays — arrive early or use the Nicolet Beach entrance off Shore Road.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Chicago to Door County?

The straight-line drive from Chicago to Ellison Bay at the tip of the Door Peninsula is approximately 300–315 miles depending on the route, and takes 5 to 6.5 hours of pure driving. Add stops and the inland WI-57 route and you're realistically looking at a full day or an overnight split at Green Bay or Sturgeon Bay.

### Is WI-57 a good route to Door County from Chicago?

Yes. WI-57 North from Green Bay through the interior of the Door Peninsula is well-maintained, two-lane, and significantly less congested than the US-141/WI-42 approach from the west. Fall color along the corridor peaks in late September to early October, and the road passes through several small towns with fuel and food options.

### When is peak fall color in Door County, Wisconsin?

Peak fall color on the Door Peninsula typically runs from the last week of September through the second week of October. Maples usually lead, followed by oaks. The 2026 season is unlikely to deviate significantly from this pattern, though an early frost can push peak dates earlier by four to seven days.

### Do you need a state park pass for Peninsula State Park in Door County?

Yes. Wisconsin requires a vehicle admission sticker for all state parks, including Peninsula State Park. Day-use fees apply to out-of-state vehicles; you can purchase a daily or annual pass at the park entrance booth or online through the Wisconsin DNR. As of 2026, daily vehicle fees for non-residents run approximately $10–$12.

### Are there EV charging stations on the Chicago to Door County route?

Level 2 and DC fast chargers are available in Kenosha, Sheboygan, and Green Bay with no significant gaps. Sturgeon Bay has a small number of Level 2 chargers near downtown. North of Sturgeon Bay on WI-42, charging infrastructure is limited — EV drivers should charge fully in Sturgeon Bay before continuing to Ellison Bay or Fish Creek.

### Is Door County worth visiting in late September versus summer?

For most drivers, late September is the better visit. Crowds are smaller than July or August, lodging rates drop 20–30% after Labor Day, and the combination of orchard harvests and fall color adds stops that simply don't exist in summer. The tradeoff is that some water-based activities (kayaking tours, some ferry schedules) begin winding down after mid-September.
