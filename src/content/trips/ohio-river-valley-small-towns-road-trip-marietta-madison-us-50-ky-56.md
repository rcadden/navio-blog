**This 420-mile route along the Ohio River connects five genuinely historic small towns — Marietta OH, Gallipolis OH, Maysville KY, Augusta KY, and Madison IN — via US-50 and KY-56, with roughly 8–9 hours of total drive time split comfortably across two days. It's the best summer road trip you haven't done yet: real 19th-century streetscapes, zero theme-park crowds, and a river running the whole way.**

The Ohio River has been a working highway since before the interstate system existed. The towns strung along its banks — on both the Ohio and Kentucky sides — never got swallowed by sprawl, which means their brick downtowns, Victorian storefronts, and river-walk parks are still intact. In late July, temperatures run warm (mid-80s°F), river traffic is high, and every town has a festival or farmers market on the calendar. This corridor is also genuinely underserved by road trip guides, which means parking is easy and diner seats are available.

- **Recommended trip length:** 2–3 days
- **Best season:** Late spring through early fall; peak summer (July–August) is ideal
- **Number of stops:** 5 major towns
- **Starting point:** Marietta, Ohio
- **Ending point:** Madison, Indiana

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for history buffs and architecture fans** — nearly every stop has a National Historic District with walkable blocks of intact 19th-century buildings.
- **Good for couples or small groups** in a standard passenger car or crossover; most town centers are flat and easy to navigate.
- **Not recommended for RVs over 35 feet** — KY-56 between Maysville and Augusta has narrow curves and limited overnight parking in town centers.
- **Works well as a first solo trip** — the route stays close to services the whole way, with no long fuel gaps.

## Marietta, Ohio (Start)

Marietta is the oldest permanent American settlement in the Northwest Territory, founded in 1788, and it earns its status as a starting point. The Campus Martius Museum (601 Second St) lays out the full settlement history in under an hour. Downtown along Front Street, stop for breakfast at Harmar Tavern — the biscuits and gravy are the real deal — before you hit the road. Fill up gas in Marietta; the next reliable cluster of stations is 45 minutes south. Cross the Ohio River via the Williamstown Bridge (WV-14 connector) only if you're detouring; stay on US-50 west to keep the route clean.

## Gallipolis, Ohio (1 hour 10 minutes from Marietta via US-35 W / US-33 S)

Gallipolis — pronounced gal-ih-puh-LEESE locally — sits on a high bank above the Ohio and has one of the most photogenic town commons in the state. The Our House Tavern State Memorial (434 First Ave) is a restored 1819 inn that served flatboatmen and is worth a 30-minute stop. For lunch, grab a table at Ariel's Restaurant on Second Avenue, a local staple with solid sandwiches and river views from the back patio. Note: US-33 through the Wayne National Forest has occasional summer construction delays in 2026; check ODOT's TripCheck before departure.

## Maysville, Kentucky (1 hour 40 minutes from Gallipolis via US-35 S / AA Highway KY-9)

Cross into Kentucky at the Simon Kenton Memorial Bridge and you're immediately in Maysville, one of the most intact antebellum commercial streetscapes in the entire Ohio Valley. The downtown stretch of Limestone Street is a legitimate National Historic District with cast-iron storefronts dating to the 1850s. Harriet Beecher Stowe reportedly drew on the area for *Uncle Tom's Cabin*, and the National Underground Railroad Museum (115 E Third St) tells that story well. Gas up here at the Shell on US-68 — it's the last full-service station before Augusta's more limited options. Dinner at Magee's Bakery and Restaurant on Market Street covers both a meal and a legendary slice of bourbon pecan pie.

## Augusta, Kentucky (35 minutes from Maysville via KY-8 / AA Highway)

Drive KY-8 west along the riverbank — one of the most scenic 20-mile stretches on this entire route — and you'll arrive in Augusta, a town of roughly 1,200 people with a main street that looks unchanged since 1910. The Rosemary Clooney House (106 E Riverside Dr) doubles as a small museum honoring the hometown singer. The Augusta Ferry, one of the last working car ferries on the Ohio River, still runs on demand across to Higginsport, Ohio, if you want a five-minute river crossing just for the experience. Augusta has limited lodging; book ahead at the Lamplighter Inn if you're staying the night, or push on to Carrollton, KY (30 minutes west on KY-36) for more hotel options.

## Madison, Indiana (2 hours 15 minutes from Augusta via KY-36 W / I-71 S / KY-56 / IN-56)

Cross back over the Ohio on the Milton-Madison Bridge (US-421) and you're in Madison, Indiana — the anchor stop of the whole trip and arguably the best-preserved early American river town in the Midwest. The entire downtown is a National Historic Landmark District covering more than 130 blocks. Lanier Mansion State Historic Site (601 W First St) is a Greek Revival showpiece open for tours Tuesday through Saturday. Finish the trip with dinner at Red Pepperoni on Main Cross Street, a local favorite for wood-fired pizza that's been operating since the early 2000s. Madison's riverfront park makes an easy evening walk before you head home or catch I-65 north toward Indianapolis.

**Summer 2026 hazard note:** Ohio River flooding is uncommon in July but possible after heavy upstream rain. Monitor the Army Corps of Engineers river gauge at Maysville (target: below 35 feet for normal road access). KY-8 between Maysville and Augusta sits close to the riverbank and can close briefly during high-water events.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Marietta, Ohio to Madison, Indiana along the Ohio River?

The direct drive on US-50 and connecting highways runs approximately 8–9 hours without stops. With town exploration, meals, and the Augusta Ferry detour, budget two full days. Three days is comfortable if you want unhurried time in each town.

### Is KY-8 between Maysville and Augusta safe to drive in summer?

KY-8 is a two-lane state highway with curves and some narrow shoulders, but it's well-maintained and entirely driveable in a standard passenger vehicle. The main summer risk is brief closures during Ohio River high-water events. Check the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet's road conditions page before departure.

### Do you need a reservation to ride the Augusta, Kentucky ferry?

No reservation is required. The Augusta Ferry operates on demand during daylight hours, typically April through November. The crossing takes about five minutes and carries passenger vehicles. Cash or card is accepted; current fare is a few dollars per car.

### Are there EV charging stations along this route?

Charging infrastructure is sparse between Gallipolis and Augusta. Maysville has a Level 2 charger at the public parking garage on Third Street, and Madison, Indiana has a DC fast charger near the US-421 / IN-56 interchange. EV drivers should plan charging stops carefully and carry a Level 1 backup cable.

### What is the best time of year to drive the Ohio River Valley small towns route?

Late May through early October offers the most reliable weather and the most open attractions. July and August are warm and busy with local festivals but manageable. Avoid January through March when river fog, ice, and occasional flooding make driving less predictable.

### Is this route suitable for families with young children?

Yes. All five towns have walkable, flat downtown areas. The Augusta Ferry is a natural crowd-pleaser for kids. Madison's riverfront park has open lawn space. The route stays on well-serviced roads with restrooms, restaurants, and fuel available throughout.
