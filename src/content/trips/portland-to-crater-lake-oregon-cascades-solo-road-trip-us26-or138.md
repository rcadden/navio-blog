**This 360-mile solo loop runs south from Portland along US-26 over Mount Hood, swings through Bend on US-97, and finishes at Crater Lake National Park via OR-138 — roughly 6.5 hours of pure drive time spread across 3 to 4 days. The verdict: Oregon's best summer solo route for drivers who want volcanic scenery, manageable daily legs, and genuine solitude without crossing a state line.**

June is the sweet spot for this corridor. Crater Lake's Rim Drive (OR-62 loop) typically clears of snow by mid-June, wildflowers are peaking along OR-138, and Bend's craft beer scene rewards an afternoon off the accelerator. You'll cover desert plateau, old-growth forest, and a caldera that drops 1,943 feet to the bluest water in North America — all on roads a solo driver can handle without a co-pilot.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~360 miles
- **Total drive time:** ~6.5 hours (point-to-point; add return leg if looping)
- **Best season:** Mid-June through September
- **Number of stops:** 6
- **Starting point:** Portland, OR
- **Ending point:** Crater Lake National Park, OR

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for first-time solo road trippers** — daily legs average under 2 hours, making it low-stress to navigate alone
- **Good for car campers and tent campers** — Lost Creek Campground (Crater Lake) and Diamond Lake Campground both welcome single-site bookings
- **Not recommended for RVs over 35 ft** — the Rim Drive's hairpin sections and the narrow OR-138 canyon stretch become genuinely difficult with a large rig
- **Not ideal if you need cell coverage throughout** — expect dead zones between Government Camp and Bend on US-26 east of Warm Springs

## Portland, OR — Start Here

Fuel up in Portland before you leave. The last reliably cheap gas on US-26 heading east is at the Chevron on SE Powell Blvd near the 205 junction. Once you clear the city, stations thin out quickly. Download offline maps for the stretch between Sandy and Government Camp — service drops around milepost 35 on US-26.

If you're starting early, grab breakfast at Lauretta Jean's on SE Clinton Street: proper biscuits and coffee, no frills, opens at 8 a.m. Pack a cooler; grocery options between Government Camp and Bend are sparse.

## Mount Hood / Government Camp (~1 hr from Portland via US-26)

US-26 climbs steadily from Sandy into the Mount Hood National Forest, topping out near 4,000 feet at Government Camp. In late June, the upper snowfields are still visible on the 11,249-foot volcano to your north. Stop at the Timberline Lodge (a 6-mile detour up Timberline Road) if you've never been — the 1937 WPA-built structure is worth 45 minutes of your morning.

Seasonal hazard: afternoon thunderstorms build over the Cascades most days by 2–3 p.m. in summer. Plan your exposed ridge stops for the morning.

## Bend, OR (~2 hrs from Government Camp via US-26 east then US-97 south)

US-26 descends through the Warm Springs Reservation onto the high desert, then meets US-97 at Madras. Head south on US-97 into Bend — total leg from Government Camp is about 120 miles. Bend is your logistics hub: resupply groceries at Market of Choice on NW Galveston Ave, top off your tank (US-97 has competitive prices), and book a night at McMenamins Old St. Francis School if you want a private room with character, or the Campfire Hotel for a more budget-friendly stay.

For solo travelers who like an easy dinner at the bar, 10 Barrel Brewing's original Bend pub on NW Brooks Street is reliable — good burgers, no awkward table-for-one energy.

## Newberry National Volcanic Monument (~30 min south of Bend via US-97)

Newberry Volcanic National Monument sits just off US-97 south of Bend, managed by the Deschutes National Forest. The Paulina Peak Road climbs to 7,985 feet with views across the caldera — on a clear June day you can see all the way to the Three Sisters. The Obsidian Flow Trail (1.5 miles) is a legitimate geological oddity: a 1,300-year-old flow of jet-black volcanic glass you can walk right up to.

Entrance fee is $5 per vehicle (America the Beautiful pass covers it). No entrance station after 5 p.m. — self-pay only.

## Diamond Lake (~1.5 hrs from Bend via US-97 south and OR-138 west)

OR-138 (Umpqua Highway) branches west from US-97 at the small junction town of Chemult. Fill your tank in Chemult — it's the last station before Crater Lake, and the next reliable fuel is 80 miles away in Roseburg if you're continuing west. Diamond Lake Resort has a small general store and a diner that serves hot food until 8 p.m. The lake itself reflects Mount Thielsen's sharp peak and makes a strong campsite base for your Crater Lake day.

Diamond Lake Campground (USFS) takes reservations at recreation.gov — book at least two weeks out for June weekends.

## Crater Lake National Park (~45 min from Diamond Lake via OR-138 west then OR-62 north)

The park entrance fee is $35 per vehicle (valid 7 days). Rim Drive — a 33-mile loop around the caldera — opens in sections as snow clears; by late June the full road is typically accessible, but check nps.gov/crla for current conditions before you drive up. Cloudcap Overlook (the highest paved point on Rim Drive at 7,960 ft) and Watchman Peak Trail (1.6 miles round-trip, 420 ft gain) are the two stops that justify the trip for solo visitors who want to move at their own pace.

Wizard Island boat tours launch from Cleetwood Cove Trail — the only legal water access point, reached by a steep 1.1-mile trail. Reserve boat tickets online well in advance; they sell out weeks ahead in summer.

Seasonal note: afternoon thunderstorms are more intense at Crater Lake's elevation (6,178 ft at rim) than in the valleys below. Plan Rim Drive for morning hours and be off exposed ridges by noon.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Portland to Crater Lake National Park?

The direct drive via US-97 and OR-62 is about 280 miles and takes roughly 4.5 hours nonstop. This route via US-26, Bend, and OR-138 covers 360 miles and runs about 6.5 hours of drive time — spread it over 3 to 4 days to actually enjoy the stops.

### Is OR-138 (Umpqua Highway) safe to drive solo in summer?

Yes, OR-138 is a well-maintained two-lane highway with guardrails on the tighter canyon sections. The main risk in summer is wildlife on the road at dawn and dusk — deer and elk are common between Diamond Lake and the US-97 junction. Drive the canyon section in daylight.

### Is Crater Lake's Rim Drive open in late June?

Typically yes — the full 33-mile Rim Drive usually opens by mid-to-late June, though the north entrance road (OR-138 to the north junction) can remain closed longer after heavy snow years. Check nps.gov/crla for the current road status before your trip.

### Do you need a reservation to camp at Crater Lake in summer?

Mazama Campground inside the park accepts reservations at recreation.gov and books out weeks in advance for June and July. Lost Creek Campground (tent-only, 16 sites) is first-come, first-served and fills by midday on summer weekends. Diamond Lake Campground just outside the park is the best backup option.

### Where do you fill up gas before Crater Lake?

Fill up in Chemult, OR, at the US-97 and OR-138 junction — it's the last reliable station before the park. The distance from Chemult to Crater Lake's south entrance is about 25 miles, but the next fuel beyond that (if you exit west) is roughly 80 miles away in Roseburg. Don't arrive at Chemult on fumes.

### Is this route doable in a standard sedan or do you need an AWD vehicle?

A standard sedan handles every road on this route in summer conditions. All highways and park roads are paved. AWD or 4WD is unnecessary from mid-June through September; if you're traveling before mid-June, check whether Rim Drive's north sections are still snow-covered — those can require chains even in late May.
