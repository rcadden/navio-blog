**New Mexico's Enchanted Circle Scenic Byway is an 84-mile loop starting and ending in Taos, driveable in one long day but best savored over two. It strings together a historic pueblo, a former gold-rush mining town, a high-alpine lake, and a Vietnam veterans memorial — all above 8,000 feet, with almost none of the crowds that pile into Zion or the Tetons in August. Our verdict: the most rewarding under-the-radar mountain loop in the Southwest.**

Most drivers passing through northern New Mexico stick to I-25 or make a straight shot to Santa Fe. That means the Enchanted Circle — officially designated as a New Mexico Scenic Byway and stitched together from NM-522, NM-38, US-64, and NM-434 — stays genuinely quiet even on summer weekends. Elevations peak near 9,800 feet at Bobcat Pass between Questa and Red River, so afternoon thunderstorms are a real factor in August. Start early, and you'll have the best light and the clearest skies.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** 84 miles (full loop)
- **Total drive time:** 3.5–5 hours behind the wheel
- **Best season:** Late May through early October; peak wildflower bloom is mid-July through August
- **Number of stops:** 5 main stops
- **Starting point:** Taos, NM
- **Ending point:** Taos, NM (loop)

## Who This Route Is For

- **Great for couples and solo drivers** who want dramatic scenery without planning months in advance or booking permits
- **Good for most passenger cars and standard SUVs** — all roads are paved and well-maintained; no 4WD required
- **Not ideal for RVs over 35 feet** — NM-38 through Bobcat Pass has tight curves and a steep 9% grade descending into Red River
- **Strong choice for history-focused travelers** — Taos Pueblo, the D.H. Lawrence Ranch, and the Vietnam Veterans Memorial at Angel Fire are all on or just off the loop

## Stop 1: Taos, NM — Starting Point

Fuel up before you leave Taos. There are no gas stations between Questa and Red River on NM-38, a stretch of roughly 12 miles through the high mountains. The Chevron on Paseo del Pueblo Sur (US-64/NM-68) is your last reliable option heading north. Grab breakfast at Michael's Kitchen on Northside, a Taos institution since 1974 that opens at 7 a.m. — the green chile eggs and fresh sopapillas are worth the 20-minute wait on busy mornings.

Head north out of Taos on NM-522 (Paseo del Pueblo Norte). The road runs straight through the high desert sage flats of the Taos Plateau, with the Sangre de Cristo Mountains building on your right and the Rio Grande Gorge cutting invisibly into the plateau to your west.

## Stop 2: Taos Pueblo (15 minutes from Taos center)

Taos Pueblo (off NM-240, just east of NM-522) is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the oldest continuously inhabited communities in North America — people have lived in these multi-story adobe structures for over 1,000 years. Admission is $25 per adult; guided tours run throughout the morning. Budget 1.5–2 hours. The Pueblo closes periodically for tribal ceremonies, so check the official schedule at taospueblo.com before your trip. In August, the San Geronimo Feast Day (September 30) is still weeks out, but smaller closures can happen with little advance notice.

## Stop 3: Questa, NM (35 minutes from Taos Pueblo via NM-522 north)

Questa sits at 7,461 feet at the junction of NM-522 and NM-38. It's a small former molybdenum mining town that most drivers blow through — don't. The Río Grande del Norte National Monument boundary runs just west of town, and the Wild Rivers Recreation Area (a short detour west on NM-378) puts you at the rim of a 800-foot-deep volcanic gorge where the Red River meets the Río Grande. The overlooks take 30 minutes and require zero hiking. Back in Questa, stop at Loly's Restaurant on NM-522 for a green chile cheeseburger before the mountain climb ahead.

This is also your last fuel stop before Red River. Fill up.

## Stop 4: Red River, NM (25 minutes from Questa via NM-38 east)

NM-38 east from Questa climbs sharply over Bobcat Pass (9,820 feet) before dropping into the narrow valley that holds Red River. The drive is 12 miles and takes about 25 minutes — slow down for the switchbacks. Red River itself is a former gold and silver mining camp turned small ski-and-summer resort town. In August, wildflowers line the valley floor and the surrounding Carson National Forest is cool and green. The main street has outfitters for ATV rentals, fly fishing on the Red River (a Gold Medal trout stream), and decent tacos at Sundance Restaurant. Overnight parking for tents and small campers is available at Red River Campground (reservations via Recreation.gov; book at least three weeks out for August weekends).

## Stop 5: Eagle Nest, NM (20 minutes from Red River via NM-38 east)

NM-38 continues east from Red River and drops to Eagle Nest at 8,200 feet. Eagle Nest Lake State Park sits right along the highway — it's one of New Mexico's top kokanee salmon and rainbow trout fisheries, and a New Mexico fishing license (available online at wildlife.state.nm.us) is required. The lake reflects the Cimarron Range on calm mornings, and the small village has a handful of cafes and a genuine old-West main street that hasn't been overdesigned for tourists. This is a good overnight base if you're splitting the loop over two days.

## Stop 6: Angel Fire, NM — Vietnam Veterans Memorial State Park (15 minutes from Eagle Nest via US-64 west)

From Eagle Nest, US-64 west takes you through the Moreno Valley to Angel Fire in about 15 minutes. The Vietnam Veterans Memorial State Park here is one of the most quietly moving sites in the American Southwest — the curved white chapel was built in 1971 by a father who lost his son in Vietnam, making it the first memorial dedicated to Vietnam veterans in the country. Admission is free. Allow 45 minutes.

## Return to Taos (40 minutes from Angel Fire via US-64 west)

US-64 west from Angel Fire crosses the 9,101-foot Palo Flechado Pass before descending back into the Taos Valley. The views of the Sangre de Cristo range on this descent are among the best on the entire loop. You'll rejoin NM-68/US-64 at the edge of Taos, completing the circle.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive the Enchanted Circle Scenic Byway?
The full 84-mile loop takes 3.5 to 5 hours of pure driving time, depending on stops and speed. Most drivers spend one to two days to include Taos Pueblo, Red River, Eagle Nest, and Angel Fire without rushing.

### Is NM-38 over Bobcat Pass safe to drive in summer?
NM-38 is paved and maintained, but Bobcat Pass reaches 9,820 feet and has steep grades and sharp curves. Afternoon thunderstorms are common in July and August — aim to cross the pass before noon. Snow is extremely rare in August but can occur at elevation as early as late September.

### Do you need a permit to visit Taos Pueblo?
No permit is required, but there is a $25 per-adult entrance fee. Taos Pueblo closes to visitors during certain tribal ceremonies and feast days; check the official schedule at taospueblo.com before your visit.

### Where can you get gas on the Enchanted Circle loop?
Gas is available in Taos, Questa, Red River, Eagle Nest, and Angel Fire. The only significant gap is the 12-mile stretch on NM-38 between Questa and Red River — fill up in Questa before heading over Bobcat Pass.

### Is the Enchanted Circle Scenic Byway good for RVs?
Smaller RVs and camper vans (under 35 feet) handle the loop fine. Larger rigs should be cautious on NM-38 between Questa and Red River, where the 9% downgrade into Red River and tight switchbacks can be challenging. Red River Campground accommodates most RVs with reservations.

### What is the best time of year to drive the Enchanted Circle?
Late June through early September offers the best combination of open roads, warm valley temperatures, and peak wildflower color. October brings aspen color but also early snow risk at Bobcat Pass. The loop is typically snow-free from late May through October.
