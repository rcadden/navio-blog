**This 250-mile drive along I-70 and Missouri Highway 94 (MO-94) connects Kansas City and St. Louis in roughly 4–6 hours with food stops, covering world-class BBQ, a German-heritage wine town, and one of America's most iconic regional dishes. It's the most food-dense corridor in the Midwest and one of the few routes where every detour pays off at a table.**

Missouri's I-70 spine is often dismissed as a straight shot between two cities. That's a mistake. Peel off the interstate at the right exits and you'll find smoke pits that have been running since before interstate highways existed, a college town with a serious independent food scene, and a Missouri River valley that turns amber and gold in October. Fall is the best time to make this drive — temperatures are in the 50s and 60s, the Hermann wine harvest is underway, and restaurant wait times haven't hit their summer peaks.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~250 miles
- **Total drive time:** 4–6 hours with stops (3.5 hours non-stop)
- **Best season:** Fall (September–November), particularly mid-October for harvest events
- **Number of stops:** 6
- **Starting point:** Kansas City, MO
- **Ending point:** St. Louis, MO
- **Primary routes:** I-70 East, MO-94 East (scenic Missouri River segment)

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for food-focused travelers** who want a structured eat-your-way-across-a-state experience without flying
- **Good for couples or small groups** — most restaurants seat walk-ins easily on weekday fall afternoons
- **Works well for drivers with standard sedans or SUVs**; MO-94 along the river has some curves but no technical driving required
- **Not recommended for RVs over 35 ft** on the MO-94 Hermann detour — parking in town is tight and some lots have low-clearance overhangs

## Kansas City, MO — Starting Point

Fuel up your car at the QuikTrip on I-70 at Exit 12 (Noland Road) before leaving the metro — it's the last reliable large-format gas station before Columbia if you're sticking to I-70 without detours. Then eat before you leave.

Arthur Bryant's Barbeque (1727 Brooklyn Ave) is the mandatory first stop. Established in 1930, it defined Kansas City's style: thick, slightly sweet, tomato-forward sauce over beef brisket and burnt ends on white bread. Go at 11 a.m. on a weekday and you'll be in and out in 20 minutes. The burnt ends sandwich is the order. If you want a second opinion, Joe's Kansas City Bar-B-Que at the 47th Street location runs a close parallel — the Z-Man sandwich (brisket, smoked provolone, onion rings on a Kaiser roll) is one of the best single items on this entire route.

## Concordia, MO — (1 hour east of Kansas City via I-70)

Concordia sits at I-70 Exit 49 and is easy to skip — don't. Kehde's Barbeque has been operating here since 1957 and represents the rural Missouri BBQ tradition that predates the Kansas City tourist circuit. It's a counter-service spot with wood-paneled walls and pork ribs that are drier and more smoke-forward than KC-style. Worth a half-order if you're pacing yourself. Gas is available at the Casey's General Store on MO-13 just off the exit ramp — prices here run 10–15 cents cheaper per gallon than in Columbia.

## Columbia, MO — (45 minutes east of Concordia via I-70)

Home to the University of Missouri, Columbia punches well above its size for independent restaurants. Skip the chains on Stadium Boulevard and head downtown.

**Booches Billiard Hall** (110 S 9th St) has been serving burgers since 1884 — they arrive on wax paper, no plate, no fuss. The single patty smash burger with American cheese is the standard. Cash only, no substitutions, and the pool tables still work. Lines form at lunch but move fast.

For a sit-down meal, **Sycamore** (800 E Broadway) runs a seasonal menu that in October leans hard into Ozark produce — roasted squash, local pork, apple-based desserts. It's the most polished kitchen on this route.

Columbia is also your last reliable fuel and grocery stop before the MO-94 scenic detour. Top off here.

## Rocheport, MO — (20 minutes west of Columbia via I-70 to MO-BB, then MO-94)

Rocheport is a 200-person town on the Missouri River bluffs, and it earns its stop. Exit I-70 at MO-BB (Exit 115) and drop south to MO-94. **Les Bourgeois Vineyards** sits on a bluff above the river with a bistro that serves flatbreads, charcuterie, and Missouri wines. In mid-October, the hillside below the winery is in full color — oak, hickory, and maple. The Blufftop Bistro operates seasonally; confirm hours before arriving (they typically close weekdays after October 15).

This is also the start of the **MO-94 Rhineland Scenic Byway** stretch. Follow MO-94 east from Rocheport rather than returning to I-70 — it adds about 35 minutes but runs directly along the Missouri River through small German-heritage towns.

## Hermann, MO — (45 minutes east of Rocheport via MO-94)

Hermann was settled by German immigrants in the 1830s and has been making wine commercially since 1843. Stone Hill Winery (1110 Stone Hill Hwy) is the anchor — their Norton varietal is Missouri's signature red grape and pairs well with the smoked meats you've been eating all day. October is Oktoberfest season in Hermann; the town fills up on weekends, so arrive before noon or after 3 p.m. to park without frustration.

For food, **Starck's Market and Deli** on 4th Street does house-made German-style sausages and a smoked pork sandwich that's worth the stop. It's a working butcher shop with a deli counter — not a restaurant — so order at the counter and eat at the picnic tables out front.

From Hermann, reconnect to I-70 East via MO-19 North (about 8 miles) for the final push to St. Louis.

## St. Louis, MO — (1 hour east of Hermann via MO-19 and I-70)

The route ends in St. Louis, where the food priority is toasted ravioli — a St. Louis original where beef-stuffed pasta is breaded and deep-fried, served with marinara. **Charlie Gitto's on the Hill** (5226 Shaw Ave) in the Italian-American Hill neighborhood is the most consistent version in the city. **Mama's on the Hill** (2132 Edwards St) is the more casual counter-service option with shorter waits.

For a final drink, **Schlafly Tap Room** (2100 Locust St) opened in 1991 as Missouri's first brewpub and pours a fall pumpkin ale in October that's less cloying than most of the style. It's a five-minute drive from the Arch and has ample street parking after 5 p.m.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Kansas City to St. Louis?
The non-stop drive on I-70 East is approximately 3.5 hours across 250 miles. Adding all food stops and the MO-94 scenic detour through Rocheport and Hermann brings total travel time to 6–8 hours, making it a comfortable one-day itinerary.

### Is I-70 through Missouri safe to drive in October?
I-70 is a well-maintained divided highway with no significant seasonal hazards in October. Morning fog is common in the Missouri River valley, particularly on MO-94 between Rocheport and Hermann — allow extra following distance and slow down in low-visibility patches. No road closures are typical at this time of year.

### Is the MO-94 scenic byway open to RVs and large vehicles?
MO-94 is a two-lane state highway with no weight restrictions, but the town centers of Rocheport and Hermann have limited parking depth. RVs under 30 feet can typically find pull-through parking at Hermann's main lot on Gutenberg Street. Larger rigs should stay on I-70 and visit Hermann via MO-19 for easier maneuvering.

### Do I need reservations at restaurants on this route?
Arthur Bryant's, Booches, and Starck's Market are all walk-in only and don't accept reservations. Sycamore in Columbia and Les Bourgeois Bistro in Rocheport strongly recommend reservations on fall weekends — book at least a week ahead for Saturday October dates. Weekday visits rarely require advance booking.

### What is toasted ravioli and where was it invented?
Toasted ravioli is a St. Louis original — beef-filled pasta that is breaded and deep-fried rather than boiled, typically served with marinara for dipping. It originated in the Hill neighborhood in the 1940s, with Ruggeri's and Charlie Gitto's both claiming credit for the accidental invention. It remains a regional specialty found almost exclusively in St. Louis metro restaurants.

### Where should I fill up gas on this route?
Fill up in Kansas City before departure (QuikTrip on I-70 at Exit 12), again at the Casey's General Store in Concordia (Exit 49), and once more in Columbia before taking the MO-94 scenic detour. The stretch from Columbia to Hermann via MO-94 has no major fuel stations for roughly 60 miles.
