**This Nashville-to-Knoxville route covers roughly 200 miles via TN-111 South and US-70N East, with a realistic driving time of 4.5 hours non-stop — plan a full day with stops. It's the best summer alternative to the busy I-40 corridor, trading interstate monotony for gorge overlooks, a 256-foot waterfall, and a plateau town with a genuine diner.**

Most drivers default to I-40 for the Nashville–Knoxville run and arrive wondering why they bothered. The Cumberland Plateau alternative via Tennessee State Route 111 (TN-111) and US Highway 70 North (US-70N) adds maybe 45 minutes to the drive but delivers limestone canyon views, one of the tallest waterfalls east of the Rockies, and the kind of two-lane plateau road that actually justifies owning a car.

## Quick Facts

- **Total distance:** ~200 miles
- **Total drive time:** 4.5 hours (non-stop); 7–8 hours with full stops
- **Best season:** Late spring through early fall; peak summer (July) is ideal for waterfall flow
- **Number of stops:** 4 major stops
- **Starting point:** Nashville, TN
- **Ending point:** Knoxville, TN

## Who This Route Is For

- **Ideal for hikers and waterfall chasers** who want a destination day at Fall Creek Falls State Park built into a point-to-point drive
- **Good for sedans and standard SUVs** — all roads are paved and well-maintained; no clearance concerns
- **Not recommended for RVs longer than 35 ft** on the TN-111 descent into the Caney Fork gorge; tight curves and limited pullouts
- **Works well as a one-way trip** if you're flying into BNA and out of TYS (or vice versa) and want to avoid backtracking

## Nashville, TN — Starting Point

Leave Nashville heading east on I-40 East to exit 238 for TN-111 South toward Cookeville. The first 40 miles are unremarkable interstate — use them to top off your tank at the Flying J Travel Center at exit 232 (Cookeville/Baxter area) before you leave the highway system. Gas stations thin out significantly once you drop south toward Fall Creek Falls.

## Cookeville, TN (approx. 1 hour from Nashville)

Cookeville sits just off I-40 at the TN-111 interchange and makes the right breakfast or early-lunch stop. Head to **Crawdaddy's Restaurant** on South Jefferson Avenue — a local fixture with a full Southern breakfast menu, strong coffee, and parking that fits trucks. Budget 45 minutes here. From Cookeville, turn south on TN-111 and the plateau character begins immediately: the road crests the escarpment and the Cumberland Plateau opens up around you.

## Fall Creek Falls State Park (approx. 45 minutes from Cookeville)

Fall Creek Falls State Park is the anchor stop on this route. The park's namesake waterfall drops 256 feet into a plunge pool — the tallest free-falling waterfall in the eastern United States. In July, snowmelt is long gone but consistent summer rainfall keeps flow strong. The overlook trail to the main falls is a 0.75-mile round trip from the parking area; the gorge floor trail adds another mile each way and puts you at the base of the falls.

Practical notes for July 2026: the park sees its highest weekend visitation in summer. Arrive before 10 a.m. or after 3 p.m. to avoid the main parking crunch. The park's inn and cabins book months ahead, but day use is free. Cell service is spotty on the gorge trail — download offline maps before you leave Cookeville. No permit is required for day hiking.

Fill up at the park's general store area or, more reliably, at the BP station in Pikeville on TN-30 before entering the park. The next reliable fuel is 25 miles away in Crossville.

## Crossville, TN (approx. 35 minutes from Fall Creek Falls)

From Fall Creek Falls, take TN-30 West briefly, then pick up US-70N East toward Crossville. Crossville sits at the geographic center of Tennessee and is the plateau's main service town. Fill up here — this is the last comfortable fuel stop before Wartburg. The Cumberland County Courthouse square has a small cluster of local shops worth a 20-minute stretch. Crossville is also where US-70N begins its more scenic eastward run toward the Obed gorge country.

## Obed Wild & Scenic River (approx. 45 minutes from Crossville)

The Obed Wild and Scenic River National Park Unit outside Wartburg, TN is one of the most underappreciated National Park Service sites in the Southeast. The Lilly Bluff Overlook, reached by a 1.2-mile round-trip trail from the visitor center on Catoosa Road, delivers a sheer sandstone cliff view over the Obed River canyon that rewards the detour. The visitor center is small but staffed; rangers can advise on current trail conditions.

In summer, the Obed draws whitewater kayakers when water is up after rain. July can bring afternoon thunderstorms — standard Appalachian summer pattern. Check the forecast before committing to a long gorge hike; the canyon walls offer no shelter and lightning exposure is real on the bluff trails.

From Wartburg, rejoin US-27 South to I-40 West briefly, then take I-40 East into Knoxville — about 50 minutes of easy driving.

## Knoxville, TN — End Point (approx. 50 minutes from Obed)

Knoxville's Market Square and the Old City neighborhood make a natural end-of-drive reward. For dinner, **Stock & Barrel** on Market Square does Tennessee whiskey burgers and local draft beers in a no-fuss setting. The University of Tennessee campus is walkable from downtown if you want to stretch your legs after a day in the car.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to drive from Nashville to Knoxville via the Cumberland Plateau?

Non-stop via TN-111 and US-70N, expect 4.5 to 5 hours depending on traffic through Cookeville. With a full stop at Fall Creek Falls State Park and a shorter stop at the Obed, budget 7 to 8 hours for a comfortable day trip.

### Is TN-111 safe to drive in summer?

TN-111 is a well-maintained two-lane state highway with no unusual hazards in summer. The descent into the Caney Fork River gorge south of Cookeville involves tight curves; drive the posted speed limits and avoid it in heavy rain if you're towing. Afternoon thunderstorms are common in July — pull over if visibility drops.

### Do you need a permit to hike at Fall Creek Falls State Park?

No permit is required for day hiking at Fall Creek Falls State Park. Day use is free. Camping and cabin reservations require advance booking through the Tennessee State Parks reservation system, and July dates fill up weeks in advance.

### Is this route suitable for RVs?

Large RVs (over 35 feet) should stick to I-40 between Nashville and Knoxville. The TN-111 gorge descent and the narrow park roads inside Fall Creek Falls are manageable for shorter Class B vans and truck campers but uncomfortable for full-size Class A rigs.

### Where should you stop for gas on this route?

Fill up in Cookeville before turning south on TN-111. Top off again in Pikeville or at the park store near Fall Creek Falls, then again in Crossville on US-70N. The stretch between Crossville and Wartburg has limited stations — don't rely on finding fuel in Wartburg itself without checking ahead.

### What is the best time of year to drive this route?

Late May through early October works well. July offers the best combination of full waterfall flow at Fall Creek Falls and warm enough temperatures for gorge hiking. Avoid this route in winter — TN-111's gorge section and the park roads ice over and may close without warning.
